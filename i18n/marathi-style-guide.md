# Marathi translation guide — drbhimraoambedkar.info

Everyone who translates for this site follows this guide: people, and the AI used for first drafts. The goal is a Marathi reader who has never opened the English volumes understanding **exactly what Dr. Ambedkar wrote and meant**, in Marathi that is clear and natural.

## 1. Fidelity comes first

1. **Never change the meaning.** Every claim, argument, figure, name, date, condition ("if", "unless", "only"), and degree ("some", "most", "all") stays exactly as in the English.
2. **Leave nothing out and add nothing.** No summarising inside the translation, no explanations slipped into his sentences, no softening. If a passage is harsh, sarcastic or angry in English, it is harsh, sarcastic or angry in Marathi.
3. **Keep his voice.** In his own texts he speaks as "मी" / "आम्ही" (first person, no honorific for himself). Keep rhetorical questions as questions, exclamations as exclamations, irony as irony.
4. **Keep the order of the argument.** Paragraphs stay paragraphs, in the same order. A long English sentence may be split into two or three shorter Marathi sentences **only if every clause is kept**.
5. **Quotations.** Text he quotes (Manu, the Gita, Dewey, Burke, Gandhi, newspaper reports, letters) is translated and stays a quotation, inside “ ” with the same attribution. Sanskrit verses already printed in Devanagari stay as printed; transliterated Sanskrit is written in Devanagari.
6. **Do not "update" his terms.** If he wrote "Untouchables", write अस्पृश्य — not दलित. If he wrote "Depressed Classes", write दलित वर्ग. If he quotes "Harijan", keep हरिजन. His choice of words is part of the history.
7. **Uncertain or garbled source text** (scanning errors, broken words, tables): translate what is clearly readable, keep numbers and tables as they are, and never guess at missing words. Mark an unreadable fragment as [मूळ मजकूर अस्पष्ट].
   Some Sanskrit, Hindi or Marathi in the PDFs is stored in an old font encoding (Kruti Dev) and shows as Latin gibberish, e.g. "o.kkZuke czkã.kks xq#," = "वर्णानाम् ब्राह्मणो गुरुः". Convert it to Devanagari only when certain, and list each case for review.
8. **Sentences split across chapters.** When a chapter starts or ends mid-sentence, render the fragment so that it joins naturally with the neighbouring chapter's translation.
9. **Section marks** such as "II", "III" stay as Roman numerals.

## 2. Clear, everyday Marathi

- Write standard (प्रमाण) Marathi of the kind used in good newspaper editorials: clear, direct, not heavily Sanskritised. Prefer the word an ordinary reader knows.
- When a technical term has no everyday Marathi word, write the Marathi term followed by the English in brackets **the first time it appears in a chapter**: अंतर्विवाह (endogamy), चलनपद्धती (currency system).
- Numbers, years, dates and page numbers use ordinary digits (0–9): 27 जानेवारी 1919, पृ. 243.
- Months: जानेवारी, फेब्रुवारी, मार्च, एप्रिल, मे, जून, जुलै, ऑगस्ट, सप्टेंबर, ऑक्टोबर, नोव्हेंबर, डिसेंबर.

## 3. Talking about Dr. Ambedkar (site pages, not his own texts)

- Always use the respectful plural: डॉ. आंबेडकरांनी लिहिले, ते म्हणाले, त्यांचे विचार.
- Names: डॉ. भीमराव रामजी आंबेडकर, डॉ. बाबासाहेब आंबेडकर, डॉ. आंबेडकर, बाबासाहेब.

## 4. "In simple words" notes

Explanations for general readers are welcome, but **only as separate notes**, clearly labelled "सोप्या शब्दांत" and never mixed into his text. A note explains; it never adds opinions or claims that are not in the text.
