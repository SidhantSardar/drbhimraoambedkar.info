# Social movements and the fight for representation, 1917–1935

This dossier holds everything found while writing the chapters "Awakening" (1919–1929) and "The Round Table" (1930–1935): dates, names, places, conflicting accounts, quotations checked against the original, and where the original records are kept. Every fact carries an inline source.

## 0. How to use the sources in this file

**BAWS (*Dr. Babasaheb Ambedkar: Writings and Speeches*).** All page links point to the Ministry of External Affairs copies at `https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/VolumeN.pdf` (Vol. 17 is `Volume17_Part_I/II/III.pdf`). `#page=N` is the PDF page of *that* file; the printed page number is given in the link text. Page numbers were checked against the project's text extraction in `library/baws-en/*.jsonl`.

- The older Dr. Ambedkar Foundation reprints at `mea.gov.in/images/attach/amb/Volume_NN.pdf` (now dead) had *different* PDF page numbers (for Vol. 2 they run about five pages higher). Do not mix the two.
- The current CPV PDFs **leave out** some editorial front matter that the 2014 reprint had: the 1982 introduction to Vol. 2 and the 2003 editorial to Vol. 17 Part I (which quotes Shahu Maharaj at Mangaon). The 2014 Vol. 2 reprint survives on the Internet Archive [source: Internet Archive, BAWS Vol. 2 (2014 reprint)](https://archive.org/details/PARI.dr-babasaheb-ambedkar-writings-and-speeches-vol-2).
- BAWS Vol. 17 is largely a compilation: it reprints newspaper reports (*The Bombay Chronicle*, *The Indian National Herald*, *The Times of India*, *Janata*), police "Bombay Secret Abstracts", court orders, Keer's biography and C. B. Khairmode's Marathi biography. When it quotes Keer, the citation is really to Keer.

**Keer.** Dhananjay Keer, *Dr. Ambedkar: Life and Mission* (Popular Prakashan, first published May 1954). The Internet Archive copy used here is the edition whose page numbers BAWS Vol. 17 cites (e.g. Yeola at pp. 252–253) [source: Internet Archive, Keer](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). Keer often reconstructs dialogue and writes in a celebratory style; treat his direct speech as his account, not a transcript.

**Pritchett.** Frances W. Pritchett's Columbia University timeline (1910s, 1920s, 1930s pages). Useful but it has some year errors for this period (see §14) [source: Pritchett, 1920s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1920s.html).

## 1. Context, 1917–1918

- Returned to India in June 1917 when his Baroda scholarship ended; his books were lost when the steamer carrying them was sunk [source: Pritchett, 1910s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).
- Served briefly as Military Secretary to the Gaekwar of Baroda; could not find lodging because of untouchability [source: Pritchett, 1910s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).
- November 1917: the Indian National Congress at Calcutta, under Annie Besant, passed its first resolution urging removal of disabilities imposed by custom on the Depressed Classes [source: Pritchett, 1910s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).
- 1918: tried private tutoring and an investment consultancy (failed once clients learned his caste); became Professor of Political Economy at Sydenham College, Bombay; colleagues objected to his drinking from the staff water pot [source: Pritchett, 1910s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html) [source: Keer, p. 39](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- He helped the Rohidas Vidyavardhak Samaj honour the Dalit cricketer P. Baloo [source: Keer, pp. 39–40](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- 16 January 1919: under a pen name he wrote to *The Times of India* that the "advanced classes" must first put the Depressed Classes on a footing of social equality before demanding Home Rule [source: Keer, p. 40](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).

## 2. Southborough (Franchise) Committee, January 1919

- Committee: Lord Southborough (chair), Sir Frank Sly, Sahibzada Aftab Ahmed Khan, Surendranath Banerjea, M. N. Hogg, W. M. Hailey, Srinivasa Sastri (absent 25 and 27 January), with added members L. C. Crump and K. Natarajan; P. C. Tallents secretary [source: BAWS Vol. 1, p. 245](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=260).
- He submitted a written statement and a supplementary statement and was examined orally on 27 January 1919; the source is the *Report of the Reforms Committee (Franchise)*, Vol. II, 1919 [source: BAWS Vol. 1, title page p. 243](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=258).
- Argument: popular government needs "representation of opinions" *and* "personal representation". Society is divided into groups that must communicate ("endosmosis"); untouchability is "the strongest ban" on that. The "real social divisions of India" are: touchable Hindus, untouchable Hindus, Mohammedans, Christians, Parsees, Jews [source: BAWS Vol. 1, pp. 247–250](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=265).
- He quoted, with approval, the view that "the untouchable classes must have their own men in the Council Hall" [source: BAWS Vol. 1, p. 253](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=268).
- Numbers: untouchables were 1,627,980 of 19,626,477 people (8%) in the British districts of the Bombay Presidency (1911 census). He claimed 8 seats in a 100-member council and asked that they elect 9 through communal constituencies grouped by language (Bombay City 1, Gujarati districts 1, Konkan 1, Deccan districts 2+2, Kannada districts, Sind 1); the 9 would elect one member to the Imperial Legislative Council [source: BAWS Vol. 1, pp. 258–261](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=273).
- He rejected the Depressed Classes Mission's "co-option" scheme (members chosen by other councillors) and called the Congress "political Radicals and social Tories" [source: BAWS Vol. 1, p. 263](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=278).
- Quote used in chapter: "One or two is only kind but neither just nor sufficient." [source: BAWS Vol. 1, p. 263](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=278)
- For non-Brahmins he preferred a lower franchise to separate electorates [source: BAWS Vol. 1, p. 255](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=270).
- Keer summarises his demand as "separate electorate and reserved seats ... in proportion to their population"; V. R. (Karmaveer) Shinde of the Depressed Classes Mission also gave evidence [source: Keer, p. 40](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). Pritchett says the same [source: Pritchett, 1910s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).

## 3. Mooknayak, Mangaon and Nagpur (1920)

**Mooknayak**
- First issue 31 January 1920, Bombay; Marathi fortnightly; title "Leader of the Dumb/Voiceless" [source: Keer, p. 41](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- Published on alternate Saturdays; price 2.5 annas, annual Rs 2.50; about 700 subscribers in July 1920 and 1,000 by July 1922 [source: Prabodhan Pol, Janata Weekly, 9 Feb 2020](https://janataweekly.org/100-years-of-mooknayak-ambedkars-first-newspaper/).
- Funding: Rs 2,500 from Shahu Maharaj of Kolhapur; later help from his friend Naval Bhathena [source: Pol](https://janataweekly.org/100-years-of-mooknayak-ambedkars-first-newspaper/). Keer only says "some help" from the Maharaja, through Dattoba Powar [source: Keer, p. 41](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- Editors: Pandurang Nandram Bhatkar (first official editor); Dnyandev Gholap from July 1920. Ambedkar was de facto editor for about 12 issues before leaving for London [source: Pol](https://janataweekly.org/100-years-of-mooknayak-ambedkars-first-newspaper/).
- *Kesari* refused even a paid announcement [source: Keer, p. 41](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer) [source: Pol](https://janataweekly.org/100-years-of-mooknayak-ambedkars-first-newspaper/).
- Masthead verse by Sant Tukaram urging the silent to speak [source: Pol](https://janataweekly.org/100-years-of-mooknayak-ambedkars-first-newspaper/).
- First issue: India "a home of inequality"; Hindu society as a tower of storeys with no ladder or entrance [source: Keer, p. 41](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- Later articles: Swaraj without fundamental rights for the Depressed Classes would be "a new slavery" (Keer's paraphrase) [source: Keer, pp. 41–42](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- End: Ambedkar distanced himself from 1923; Gholap revived it in Satara as *Abhinav Mooknayak* [source: Pol](https://janataweekly.org/100-years-of-mooknayak-ambedkars-first-newspaper/). Wikipedia gives April 1923 for the end (unverified).

**Mangaon conference**
- Keer: 21 March 1920, Mangaon, Kolhapur State; Shahu Maharaj present; ended in an inter-caste dinner with the Maharaja, state officials and jagirdars [source: Keer, p. 42](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- Shahu's words as quoted by Keer, citing S. N. Shivtarkar in the *Janata* special number of April 1933: "You have found your saviour in Ambedkar. I am confident that he will break your shackles. Not only that, a time will come when, so whispers my conscience, Ambedkar will shine as a front-rank leader of all-India fame and appeal." [source: Keer, p. 42](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer) (The same words, dated 21 March 1920, appear in the Vol. 17 Part I editorial of the 2014 reprint; that page is not in the CPV PDF.)
- Round Table India (Somnath Waghmare): 21–22 March 1920; organised by Mangaon Dalits with Appasaheb Patil (a Jain village headman in the Satyashodhak movement); 15-point agenda; afterwards caste Hindus boycotted the Dalits and Patil until Shahu intervened; called Ambedkar's "first public meeting" [source: Round Table India](https://roundtableindia.co.in/?p=14256).
- The Maharashtra government planned a centenary event at Mangaon on 21 March 2020 (Deccan Herald; not opened, page blocked).

**Nagpur, May 1920**
- Last week of May 1920: first All-India conference convened by untouchables, presided over by Shahu Maharaj. Ambedkar opposed Shinde's proposal that the Council choose Depressed Classes representatives; the conference passed a resolution against it [source: Keer, pp. 42–43](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). Pritchett says he got Mahar sub-castes to dine together there [source: Pritchett, 1920s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1920s.html).

## 4. Legal practice and teaching, 1923–1928

- Called to the Bar by Gray's Inn in 1922; admitted November 1916 [source: Supreme Court of India, certificate page](https://api.sci.gov.in/AMB/Certificate.php).
- After returning in 1923 he applied for a *sanad*; set up practice at the Social Service League, Bombay [source: Supreme Court of India, application page](https://api.sci.gov.in/AMB/Application.php). Keer: money for the sanad came from Naval Bhathena; practice began June 1923; he joined the Appellate Side because solicitors shunned him [source: Keer, p. 51](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). A book excerpt quoted in a search result gives 25 June 1923 as the application date (not verified).
- Lecturer in mercantile law at Batliboi's Accountancy Training Institute, June 1925 – March 1928 [source: Keer, p. 66](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer) [source: Pritchett, 1920s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1920s.html).
- 1925: evidence to the Royal Commission on Indian Currency and Finance [source: Pritchett, 1920s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1920s.html).
- October 1926: won the libel case brought by Poona Brahmins against non-Brahmin leaders K. B. Bagde, Keshavrao Jedhe and Dinkarrao Javalkar over a pamphlet (popularly the "Deshache Dushman" case — the name is not in Keer); prosecution led by L. B. Bhopatkar [source: Keer, pp. 63–64](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- Bombay High Court cases listed by the Supreme Court of India for this period: Yashwant Satva Chaugule v. Emperor (1926); Ahmedabad Cotton & Co. v. Bai Budhian Rajaram (1927); Nana Khandera Ghadge v. Emperor (1927); Emperor v. Shantaram S. Mirajkar (1928); Emperor v. Philip Spratt (Nos. 2 and 3, 1928); Emperor v. Vithabai Sukha (1928); and in 1935 Khandesh Laxmi Vilas Mills, Gulabrao Chandgude, and Emperor v. A. A. Alwe (a textile strike case) [source: Supreme Court of India, cases page](https://api.sci.gov.in/AMB/Cases.php).
- Government Law College: acting professor from June 1928 [source: Keer, p. 115](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer); Pritchett says the post ended in 1929 [source: Pritchett, 1920s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1920s.html); the Supreme Court page says he taught there "from 1928" [source: Supreme Court of India](https://api.sci.gov.in/AMB/index.php). Law students once boycotted his class over the Simon Commission [source: Keer, p. 116](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).

## 5. Bahishkrit Hitakarini Sabha (1924) and the motto

- Meeting at Damodar Hall, Bombay, 9 March 1924; Sabha established 20 July 1924; registered under Act XXI of 1860 [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part II, p. 395](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=420) [source: Keer, pp. 54–55](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- Officers: President Sir Chimanlal Setalvad; Vice-Presidents Meyer Nissim, Rustomji Jinwala, G. K. Nariman, Dr R. P. Paranjpye, Dr V. P. Chavan, B. G. Kher; Ambedkar chairman of the managing committee; S. N. Shivtarkar secretary; N. T. Jadhav treasurer [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part II, p. 395](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=420).
- Aims: education (hostels), culture (libraries, social centres, study circles), economic improvement (industrial and agricultural schools), representing grievances [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part II, p. 396](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=421). Board of Trustees had to include Mahar, Chambhar, Mang and Dhed members [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part II, p. 399](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=424).
- It met a need voiced at the Second Bombay Provincial Depressed Classes Conference at Barsi (Sholapur district) early in 1924 [source: Keer, p. 55](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- 4 January 1925: hostel for high-school students at Sholapur; Sholapur municipality grant Rs 40; managed by Jivappa Subha Aydale [source: Keer, p. 62](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- English name: "Depressed Classes Institute". A 1931 appeal in its name says it "was established in June, 1925" and published *Janata* [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part II, pp. 407–408](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=432). The Simon Commission chairman called his 1928 memorandum the statement "on behalf of the Depressed Classes' Institute of Bombay" [source: BAWS Vol. 2, p. 459](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=478).
- Wound up 14 June 1928 and replaced for education work by the Depressed Classes Education Society (see §10) [source: Keer, p. 123](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). (The "Institute" name continued to be used in 1930–31.)

**"Educate, Agitate, Organise" — what the sources actually show**
1. BAWS editors (2003): "The Vow of the Institute was decided 'Educate, Agitate and Organize' and was approved unanimously" at the 1924 founding. No primary source is given [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part II, p. 395](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=420).
2. The printed "Rules of Constitution" reproduced right after (pp. 395–406) do not contain the slogan [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part II, pp. 396–406](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=421). Keer's account of the founding (pp. 54–56) does not mention it either [source: Keer, pp. 54–56](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
3. Pritchett/Columbia: the Sabha's "motto was 'Educate, Agitate, Organise'" (1924 entry; no source shown) [source: Pritchett, 1920s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1920s.html).
4. 27 September 1930, Poona (*Times of India*, 29 Sept 1930): the Depressed Classes "must agitate much more and organise themselves better" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, p. 61](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=90).
5. 20 July 1942, Nagpur, reply to an address at the All-India Depressed Classes Conference (18–20 July 1942): "My final words of advice to you is educate, agitate and organize, have faith in yourselves and never lose hope." Source given: *Report of Depressed Classes Conference, Nagpur Session, July 1942* [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, pp. 273–276](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=305).
6. 6 May 1945, Bombay, All-India Scheduled Castes Federation: "We have no funds to maintain a machinery ... to educate, agitate and organize them." [source: BAWS Vol. 1, *Communal Deadlock*](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=372)
7. Older British use: the slogan "Educate! Agitate! Organise!" was first used by the (Social) Democratic Federation in 1880s Britain, according to the union UNISON (a non-scholarly source; search results attribute an 1883 pamphlet and a Walter Crane design for William Morris's *Chants for Socialists*, 1885 — not verified) [source: UNISON blog, 25 March 2022](https://unison.org.uk/blogs/old-slogan-educate-agitate-organise-relevant-ever).
8. Popular Marathi/Hindi versions often reorder it as "Educate, Organise, Agitate" (शिका, संघटित व्हा, संघर्ष करा). We found no primary source for that ordering in BAWS English volumes.
- **Conclusion for the site:** the three verbs appear in his own recorded words in 1942 and 1945; the link to the 1924 Sabha rests on later editorial statements; the phrase itself predates him.

## 6. Bombay Legislative Council, 1926–1934

- Nominated by the Governor of Bombay (Keer places it before 2 February 1927, when Depressed Class teachers met to celebrate; a purse was presented on 19 April 1927 and given to the Sabha) [source: Keer, pp. 68–69](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). Pritchett lists it under 1926 [source: Pritchett, 1920s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1920s.html).
- Sworn in as a nominated member on Friday 18 February 1927; first budget speech 24 February 1927 (B.L.C. Debates Vol. XIX, pp. 164–68) [source: BAWS Vol. 2, p. 1](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=20).
- Re-nominated in 1932; elected to the Bombay Legislative Assembly in 1937 [source: BAWS Vol. 2 introduction, 2014 reprint (Internet Archive)](https://archive.org/details/PARI.dr-babasaheb-ambedkar-writings-and-speeches-vol-2).
- Speeches 1927–1928 in BAWS Vol. 2: prohibition (10 March 1927), grants for education (12 March 1927), Bombay University Act amendment (27 July, 1, 3, 5 October 1927), Small Holders' Relief Bill (10 October 1927), budget (21 February 1928), Maternity Benefit Bill (28 July 1928), Bill No. XII of 1928 to amend the Bombay Hereditary Offices Act (the Mahar *watan* bill, 19 March 1928; debate 3–4 August 1928), Village Panchayats Bill (1932–33) [source: BAWS Vol. 2, contents](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=14) [source: BAWS Vol. 2, p. 72](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=91).
- **Bole resolutions.** S. K. Bole moved on 4 August 1923 that untouchables may use public watering places, wells, schools, dharmashalas etc.; Government directed departments to comply on 11 September 1923 [source: Supreme Court of India, Mahad page](https://api.sci.gov.in/AMB/mahadsatyagraha.php) [source: Keer, p. 52](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). Bole's second resolution of 5 August 1926 asked Government to stop grants to local bodies that ignored the first [source: Keer, p. 64](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). *The Indian National Herald* (1928) refers to a Council resolution "passed in September 1926" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 40](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=65).

## 7. Koregaon, 1 January 1927

- He held a meeting at the Koregaon victory pillar, which honours Mahar soldiers who fought in the British army in 1818 [source: Pritchett, 1920s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1920s.html). Speech titled "Great Fight", 1 January 1927 [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, contents](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=16).

## 8. Mahad Satyagraha (1927) — full detail

**Background**
- The Mahad Municipality resolved in 1924 (Keer) to open the Chavdar Tank to all; untouchables still did not use it for fear of caste Hindus [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 3](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=24) [source: Supreme Court of India](https://api.sci.gov.in/AMB/mahadsatyagraha.php).
- The tank is "a small lake or large pool; between four and five acres", on the outskirts of the town, surrounded by municipal roads (Bombay High Court, 1937) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 45](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=70).

**First conference, 19–20 March 1927**
- Kolaba District Depressed Classes Conference, organised by Surendranath Tipnis, Subhedar Savadkar and Anantrao Chitre; Ambedkar presided [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 3](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=24).
- Attendance: "about ten thousand" (Keer) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 3](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=24); "over 2,500" (*Bombay Chronicle*) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 9](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=32).
- Drinking water for the conference had to be bought from caste Hindus: Rs 40 (Keer) or Rs 15 a day (*Bombay Chronicle*) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, pp. 3, 9](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=24).
- Presidential address (Keer's summary): memories of Dapoli; closure of the army to Mahars as "a betrayal"; give up eating carrion; self-help, self-respect, self-knowledge [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, pp. 4–5](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=25).
- Caste Hindu speakers Purushottam Prabhakar Joshi and Govind Narayan Dhariya supported a resolution on caste Hindus' duties, except the clause on inter-marriage [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, pp. 9–10](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=32).
- 20 March 1927: procession to the tank; "Dr. Ambedkar took water from the Tank and drank it" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 6](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=29). The Supreme Court page quotes him: "We are going to the Lake to assert that we too are human beings like others" [source: Supreme Court of India](https://api.sci.gov.in/AMB/mahadsatyagraha.php) (no primary citation given there; not used in chapter).
- Two hours later a rumour said they would enter the Veereshwar temple; a mob attacked; about 20 wounded; assaults continued in villages. Ambedkar to officials: "You control others, I will control my people" (Keer). He returned to Bombay on 23 March [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, pp. 6–8](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=29).
- Nine caste Hindus arrested; five sentenced on 6 June 1927 to four months' rigorous imprisonment [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 8](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=31).
- "Purification": 108 earthen pots of cow dung, cow urine, milk and curd; Brahmin priests chanted mantras [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 12](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=35).

**Between the conferences**
- 3 April 1927: *Bahishkrit Bharat* started (see §9).
- 26 June 1927: *Bahishkrit Bharat* called for satyagraha volunteers to enlist at the Sabha's office [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, pp. 12–13](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=35).
- 3 July 1927: protest meeting, Cowasji Jehangir Hall, about 1,000 people, Rs 350 collected (Bombay Secret Abstract, 16 July 1927) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 13](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=36).
- 4 August 1927: the Mahad Municipality revoked its 1924 resolution [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 15](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=38).
- 11 September 1927: committee formed at Damodar Hall; four days later it fixed 25–26 December [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 15](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=38).
- 30 October 1927: meeting of about 1,000, Rs 570 collected; decision to hold the conference on 25 December [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 14](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=37).
- 27 November 1927: opponents met at the Veereshwar temple; 7 December the District Magistrate refused to ban the satyagraha and told them to go to court [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, pp. 15–16](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=38).
- 12 December 1927: Regular Suit No. 405 of 1927 filed in the Mahad civil court, claiming the tank as private property of the "touchable classes"; defendants Ambedkar, S. N. Shivtarkar, Krishnaji S. Kadam and Ganya Malu Chambhar [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 16](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=39).
- 14 December 1927: temporary injunction by Sub-Judge G. V. Vaidya (text reproduced from Khairmode, Vol. 3) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, pp. 16–19](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=39).

**Second conference, 25–27 December 1927**
- Hindu landlords refused land; a site was obtained from a Muslim (Sabrang names him Fattekhan); local merchants boycotted the organisers, so food was bought from outside; Anantrao Chitre and Subhedar Ghatge managed arrangements [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 19](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=42) [source: SabrangIndia](https://www.sabrangindia.in/article/why-did-dr-babasaheb-ambedkar-publicly-burn-manu-smruti-dec-25-1927).
- Ambedkar left Bombay on 24 December with about 200 delegates; Sabrang says he came by the boat *Padmavati* via Dasgaon; about 3,000 waited at Dasgaon; the conference began at 4.30 p.m. on 25 December with about 15,000 present (Keer) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 20](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=43) [source: SabrangIndia](https://www.sabrangindia.in/article/why-did-dr-babasaheb-ambedkar-publicly-burn-manu-smruti-dec-25-1927). D. V. Pradhan wrote of "about 10,000" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 34](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=59).
- Speech (summary in *The Indian National Herald*, 28 Dec 1927): the tank was open even to cattle of untouchables but not to them; the conference "is held to unfurl the banner of Equality and thus may be likened to the National Assembly in France convened in 1789"; "We are avowedly out to smash the steel-frame of the caste-system"; "not failure but low aim is a crime"; inter-caste marriage needed; even without untouchability they would at most be treated as Shudras under the smritis [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, pp. 21–24](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=44).
- Verified quote used in chapter: "We are resorting to this Satyagraha not because we believe that the water of this particular tank has any exceptional qualities, but to establish our natural rights as citizens and human beings." [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 22](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=45) — this is the newspaper's report of his speech.
- Burning of the Manusmriti: G. N. Sahasrabuddhe read extracts on Shudras; first resolution condemned the Manusmriti and similar books "which most flagrantly violate the human rights" and resolved to burn them, with a declaration that all Hindus be treated as one varna; D. V. Pradhan, a caste Hindu, supported; Mr. Mandlik was refused permission to speak; further resolution that priests be selected by competitive examination and licensed (*Indian National Herald*, 28 and 31 Dec 1927) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, pp. 24–25](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=47).
- Keer: the resolution was moved by Sahasrabuddhe, with speeches by Rajbhoj and Thorat; at 9 p.m. the book was burned on a pyre in a pit in front of the pandal "at the hands of untouchable hermits"; Keer calls 25 December 1927 "a red letter day" and Mahad "the Wittenberg of India" [source: Keer, pp. 99–101](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- Sabrang (based on M. S. Gore, *The Social Context of an Ideology*): resolution moved by Gangadhar Neelkanth Sahastrabuddhe, seconded by P. N. Rajbhoj; pit six inches deep, about 1.5 feet square, of sandalwood, prepared over two days by six people; banners: "Manusmruti chi dahan bhumi" (crematorium for the Manusmriti), "Destroy Untouchability", "Bury Brahmanism"; the only photograph in the pandal was Gandhi's [source: SabrangIndia (Himadri Ghosh, 2016/2017)](https://www.sabrangindia.in/article/why-did-dr-babasaheb-ambedkar-publicly-burn-manu-smruti-dec-25-1927).
- Keer on the Manusmriti's content: the resolution cited the rule that molten lead be poured into the ears of a Shudra who heard the Vedas [source: Keer, p. 100](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). (Keer dates the text "over one thousand and five hundred years ago"; Olivelle's critical edition is the scholarly reference [source: Olivelle, *Manu's Code of Law*, OUP 2004/2005](https://academic.oup.com/book/50073).) Note: the molten-lead rule is not in the Manusmriti. It is Gautama Dharmasutra 12.4 [source: Bühler, SBE vol. 2, p. 236](https://archive.org/details/wg902/page/236/mode/1up); Manu 8.272 orders hot oil in the mouth and ears of a Shudra who arrogantly teaches Brahmins their duty [source: Bühler, SBE vol. 25, p. 302](https://archive.org/details/lawsofmanu00manuuoft/page/302/mode/1up). The site attributes the claim to the 1927 resolution, as Keer reports it.
- 1938 interview with T. V. Parvate (full version in Keer): "The bonfire of Manusmriti was quite intentional. It was a very cautious and drastic step, but was taken with a view to forcing the attention of caste Hindus. At intervals such drastic remedies are a necessity. If you do not knock at the door, none opens it. It is not that all the parts of the Manusmriti are condemnable ... We made a bonfire of it because we view it as a symbol of injustice under which we have been crushed across centuries." [source: Keer, p. 106](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer) **Note:** BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 25 prints only the first and last sentences, joined without an ellipsis [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 25](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=50). The chapter uses an ellipsis.
- He defended the burning in *Bahishkrit Bharat* (3 February 1928) [source: SabrangIndia](https://www.sabrangindia.in/article/why-did-dr-babasaheb-ambedkar-publicly-burn-manu-smruti-dec-25-1927) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 30 (Bombay Chronicle, 27 Feb 1928)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=55).
- 26 December: letter from the Collector asking them to obey the injunction; 3,884 registered for satyagraha within an hour (Herald) — "over 4,000" (Keer); Collector Hood addressed the conference in Marathi at 5.30 p.m.; Jawalkar brought the non-Brahmin party's advice to obey the court [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, pp. 25–27](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=50) [source: Keer, p. 102](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- 27 December: satyagraha postponed "till the decision of the Civil Court"; procession round the tank; streets deserted [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, pp. 27–29](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=52).
- Afterwards he visited Raigad with about 100 people and bathed in the Gangasagar tank, which the watchman said was not for untouchables (*Bombay Chronicle*, 12 Jan 1928) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 29](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=54).

**The court case, 1928–1937**
- 24 January 1928: Ambedkar wrote to Advocate Markand Dattatraya Vaidya to prepare affidavits, including one from a Muslim butcher and one from a Brahmin priest, Pandurang Bhaskar Shastri Palaye [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, pp. 30–31](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=55).
- 23 February 1928: Sub-Judge G. V. Vaidya dissolved the injunction; a stone slab read "Municipality Mahad 1899"; another slab looked freshly tampered with; he regretted "having been instrumental in heaping one more wrong upon a community already labouring under the most cruel and unjust social wrongs" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, pp. 35–38](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=60).
- 26 February 1928: Damodar Hall meeting (police: ~500; Pradhan: ~2,000) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, pp. 35, 39](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=60).
- 20–24 March 1928: District Judge B. N. Sanjana (Thana) heard the caste Hindus' appeal and restored an interim injunction pending the suit [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, pp. 42–43](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=67).
- 8 June 1931: Sub-Judge V. R. Saraf (Mahad) decided the suit for Ambedkar and the other defendants [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 43](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=68).
- 30 January 1933: Second Assistant Judge, Thana (named "S. M. Kokani" on p. 43 and "S. M. Kaikini" in the High Court judgment) dismissed the appeal (Appeal No. 32 of 1931) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, pp. 43–44](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=68).
- 17 March 1937: Bombay High Court, Second Appeal No. 462 of 1933, *Narhari Damodar Vaidya and others v. Dr. Bhimrao Ramji Ambedkar and others*, Broomfield and Wadia JJ., dismissed the appeal. Counsel: V. B. Virkar for appellants; S. V. Gupte with B. G. Modak for Ambedkar [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, pp. 44–45](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=69).
- 1932: a Bombay Government committee (Symington) found about 1,100 District Local Board wells in Nasik district still closed to untouchables despite the 1923 resolution [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 42](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=67).

## 9. Newspapers: Bahishkrit Bharat, Samata, Janata

- *Bahishkrit Bharat*: Marathi fortnightly, started 3 April 1927 in Bombay [source: Keer, p. 80](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer); edited by him 1927–1929 and closed in 1929 [source: Pritchett, 1920s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1920s.html). Keer's footnotes cite its issues or editorials of 20 May, 1 July, 29 July and 27 November 1927, and 16 August 1929 [source: Keer, footnotes between pp. 41 and 115](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). Its masthead line came from the *Dnyaneshwari* (per a Commons file description; not verified) [source: Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:MookNayak_(Mute_Hero_-_1920)_and_Bahishkrit_Bharat_(India_Ostracised_-_1927)_were_two_Marathi_journals_edited_by_Dr._Babasaheb_Ambedkar.jpg).
- *Samata*: launched 29 June 1928 as a fortnightly eight-page tabloid at 1.5 annas; editor Devrao Naik (a Govardhan Brahmin from north Konkan who ran *Brahman–Brahmanetar*); written mostly by B. V. Pradhan, D. V. Pradhan and other caste-Hindu supporters; Ambedkar named as "leader" but wrote nothing; 19 issues, ended March 1929 [source: Ashok Gopal, *A Part Apart*, excerpt, The News Minute, 30 Apr 2023](https://www.thenewsminute.com/features/ambedkar-non-brahmins-and-samaj-samata-sangh-176576) [source: The Mooknayak (Kaleem Ullah Fasihi)](https://en.themooknayak.com/discussion-interview/can-caste-hindus-write-against-caste-ambedkars-radical-1928-experiment-with-samata).
- *Janata*: announced by Ambedkar at a Bombay send-off on 2 October 1930 as a fortnightly to replace *Bahishkrit Bharat* [source: Keer, p. 145](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer); started by Devrao Naik and B. R. Kadrekar while he was at the first Round Table Conference [source: Keer, p. 156](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer); converted from fortnightly to weekly by November 1931, published by the Depressed Classes Institute [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part II, p. 408](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=433); ran until 1956 [source: Pritchett, 1920s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1920s.html). The exact date 24 November 1930 appears only on wiki-style pages (unverified). Renamed *Prabuddha Bharat* in 1956 (secondary sources; unverified).

## 10. Samaj Samata Sangh, Samata Sainik Dal, Depressed Classes Education Society

**Samaj Samata Sangh (Social Equality League)**
- Announced August 1927 by B. V. Pradhan (a CKP lawyer); founded September 1927 per a photo caption; office in Nagvekar building, Portuguese Church Street, Dadar; Ambedkar chairman; office-bearers Dr R. N. Bhaindarkar, Devrao Naik, S. S. Gupte, B. V. Pradhan, R. D. Kavali, D. V. Pradhan [source: Gopal, TNM](https://www.thenewsminute.com/features/ambedkar-non-brahmins-and-samaj-samata-sangh-176576).
- Activities: invested Depressed Class members with the sacred thread; members invited each other to dine [source: Keer, p. 94](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). All-caste dinner at Damodar Thakersey Hall on the "5th instant" (reported 15 March 1928): ~150 people incl. ~50 untouchables; D. V. Naik vice-president; speakers A. B. Kolhatkar (*Sandesh*), V. B. Karnik, M. R. Menon [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, pp. 41–42](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=66). Dinner at Shivtarkar's home 3 November 1928, after which the Chambhar caste panchayat boycotted Shivtarkar; Dadar Ganesh festival dispute August–September 1928; Shridhar Tilak (son of B. G. Tilak) opened a Pune branch in April 1928 and died 26 May 1928 [source: Gopal, TNM](https://www.thenewsminute.com/features/ambedkar-non-brahmins-and-samaj-samata-sangh-176576).

**Samata Sainik Dal**
- Founded 1927 per Wikipedia, citing Shri Krishan, *Political Mobilization and Identity in Western India, 1934–47* (SAGE 2005), p. 200 [source: Wikipedia (pointer)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samata_Sainik_Dal). Exact day disputed in online sources: 13 March 1927 (drambedkarbooks blog) or 19 March 1927 (dhammabharat); not verified in a primary source.
- Ganpat Jadhav (Madkebua) was appointed its head [source: Gopal, TNM](https://www.thenewsminute.com/features/ambedkar-non-brahmins-and-samaj-samata-sangh-176576).
- Described in a later Ambedkar document as bands of youth volunteers in Bombay Presidency and the Central Provinces "to inculcate the spirit of discipline" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part II, p. 450](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=475).
- Formal constitution drafted after a conference at Kanpur on 30 January 1944 (president B. K. Gaikwad); oath text and aims in BAWS [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, pp. 566–567](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=595).

**Depressed Classes Education Society**
- He started two hostels in early June 1928 and, closing the Bahishkrit Hitakarini Sabha on 14 June 1928, established the Depressed Classes Education Society; Government approved a scheme on 8 October 1928 and the Governor agreed to five hostels for secondary students; registered under Act XXI of 1860 (Keer writes "1861"); trustees Meyer Nissim, Shankar Sayanna Parsha, Dr P. G. Solanki and Ambedkar; Ambedkar general secretary, Shivtarkar secretary and treasurer; 19-member advisory board [source: Keer, p. 123](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- Hostel locations given online (Panvel, Thane, Nashik, Pune, Dharwad) come from blogs; unverified.

## 11. Simon Commission (Indian Statutory Commission), 1928–1929

- Congress boycotted it because it had no Indian members [source: Pritchett, 1920s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1920s.html).
- Elected to the Bombay Provincial Committee by the Bombay Legislative Council on 5 August 1928 [source: Keer, p. 115](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). BAWS Vol. 2's 1982 introduction says "In 1928-29 he was elected a member of the committee" and also of the State committee on the condition of the Depressed Classes and Aboriginal Tribes [source: BAWS Vol. 2 introduction, 2014 reprint](https://archive.org/details/PARI.dr-babasaheb-ambedkar-writings-and-speeches-vol-2).
- Two memoranda for the Bahishkrit Hitakarini Sabha, 29 May 1928: on education, and on safeguards for the Depressed Classes as a minority [source: BAWS Vol. 2, pp. 407, 429](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=448).
- Population dispute: Government of Bombay 1,478,390; he claimed about 2,800,000 (Census of India 1921, Vol. 1) [source: BAWS Vol. 2, pp. 459–463](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=478). The memorandum said 10.8% of the population would justify 15 of 140 seats, and asked for 22 [source: BAWS Vol. 2, p. 437](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=456).
- Oral evidence at Poona, 23 October 1928, with Dr P. G. Solanki (source: *Indian Statutory Commission*, Vol. XVI, HMSO 1930, pp. 52–75): "we must be treated as a distinct minority, separate from the Hindu community"; "We claim reserved seats if accompanied by adult franchise"; otherwise separate electorates; 22 of 140 seats [source: BAWS Vol. 2, pp. 459–466](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=484).
- Keer: 18 Depressed Class associations gave evidence and 16 asked for separate electorates, while the Sabha asked for joint electorates with reserved seats [source: Keer, p. 116](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- Separate report on the constitution of Bombay Presidency, signed 17 May 1929 (opposes separating Karnataka, discusses electorates, Lucknow Pact, second chamber) [source: BAWS Vol. 2, pp. 315–401](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=420).

## 12. 1929

- *Bahishkrit Bharat* closed [source: Pritchett, 1920s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1920s.html).
- At Jalgaon in 1929 he advised untouchables to adopt any religion that would treat them as human; twelve became Muslims (Keer) [source: Keer, pp. 251–252](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- 23 October 1929: serious accident at Chalisgaon; bedridden until late December [source: Pritchett, 1920s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1920s.html).

## 13. 1930–1935

### 13.1 Kalaram Temple satyagraha, Nashik
- Preparations for three months; Satyagraha Committee secretary Bhaurao Gaikwad; about 15,000 gathered; present: Deorao Naik, Rajbhoj, Pradhan, Shivtarkar, Patitpavandas, B. G. Kher; conference on Sunday 2 March 1930 (Keer, p. 136) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 181](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=206).
- His speech (BAWS editors' translation from Shankarrao Kharat's Marathi *Dr. Babasaheb Ambedkaranchi Patre*, pp. 46–47): temple entry "would not solve the whole problem"; it was "a test to judge the high caste Hindu mind" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 182](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=207).
- Afternoon procession about a mile long with a band, scouts and about 500 women; gates found closed; meeting at Godavari ghat. From 3 March: first batch of 125 men and 25 women at the four gates; over 8,000 enlisted; Police Superintendent "Reynalds" camped at the temple; a citizens' meeting under Dr Kurtakoti, the Shankaracharya, broke up when orthodox people threw stones and shoes [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, pp. 183–184](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=208).
- 9 April 1930 (Ram Navami chariot): agreement to let both sides pull the chariot broke down; a Bhandari youth, Kadrekar, was badly hurt; Ambedkar slightly injured; mediation by Dr Moonje and Dr Kurtakoti; G. D. Birla met Ambedkar in mid-April 1930; the temple stayed closed "for a whole year" (Keer) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, pp. 184–185](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=209).
- Letters to the Governor, Sir Frederick Sykes, 24 March and 11 April 1930 (from Khairmode, Vol. 3): complaints about the priest's private door used as a public entrance; on Ram Navami 18 of 300 satyagrahis were arrested; caste-Hindu constables joined the assault; one man reported killed at the river bank; Section 144 order [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, pp. 185–191](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=210).
- June 1931: advised Trichur temple-entry organisers not to rely on Congress help (*Bombay Chronicle*, 18 June 1931) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 191](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=216).
- November 1931, from London: "We must not take our orders from the Government just as we must not take them from the Orthodox Hindus"; satyagraha restarting on 5 November (*Bombay Chronicle*, 3 Nov 1931) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, pp. 192–193](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=217).
- 6 June 1932: Nasik District Magistrate L. N. Brown barred untouchables from the sacred *kunds* until a civil court decided otherwise (*Times of India*, 7 June 1932) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, pp. 193–194](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=218).
- Duration: Ambedkar (1945) — the satyagraha movement "went on for full six years" and ended at Yeola in 1935; it was "organised by the Untouchables, led by the Untouchables and financed by the Untouchables"; he says Gandhi did not support it [source: BAWS Vol. 9, p. 247](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume9.pdf#page=276). PARI says he pursued it until 1934 [source: PARI (Namita Waikar, 2018)](https://ruralindiaonline.org/article/bhimraya-moved-heaven-and-earth). Yeola speech: "past five years" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, p. 95](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=124).
- Wikipedia's Kalaram article names Sane Guruji as a co-leader; we found no support for this in BAWS or Keer (Sane Guruji is associated with the 1947 Pandharpur temple-entry campaign). Not used.

### 13.2 All-India Depressed Classes Congress, Nagpur, 8–9 August 1930
- Organised by Nagpur leaders Dashrath Laxman Patil and Laxmanrao Ogale (MLC); reception committee chairman T. C. Sakhare (later letterhead names K. G. Nandagawali); secretary Hardas L. N. (Babu Hardas); dates moved from 12–13 July so delegates could read the Simon Report [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, pp. 13–17](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=42).
- Positions: independence from both the Government and the Congress; criticism of Congress for making khaddar, not anti-untouchability, its membership test; civil disobedience not to be supported; need for an All-India organisation [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, pp. 54–57](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=83). Pritchett: he backed Dominion status and called the Salt March ill-timed [source: Pritchett, 1930s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1930s.html).
- Quote used: "political power cannot be a panacea for the ills of the Depressed Classes. Their salvation lies in their social elevation." [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, p. 58](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=87)

### 13.3 Round Table Conferences
- First session: opened by King George V on 12 November 1930 at the House of Lords; adjourned 19 January 1931 [source: Keer, pp. 147, 156](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). He sailed after a Bombay send-off on 2 October 1930 [source: Keer, p. 145](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). Invited by the Viceroy; wrote an unpublished manuscript "The Untouchables and the Pax Britannica" [source: Pritchett, 1930s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1930s.html).
- Plenary speech, 20 November 1930 (source: *Proceedings of the RTC (First Session)*, Calcutta 1931, pp. 123–29): 43 million people, "one-fifth of the total population of British India"; position "midway between that of the serf and the slave"; the "Chinese tailor" image; "nobody can remove our grievances as well as we can"; "ascending scale of reverence and a descending scale of contempt"; "Depressed by the Government, suppressed by the Hindu and disregarded by the Muslim"; preference for a unitary government but open to federation [source: BAWS Vol. 2, pp. 503–509](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=524).
- Committees: Sub-Committee II (Provincial Constitution), III (Minorities), VI (Franchise; argued for adult suffrage), VIII (Services); Federal Structure Committee and Minorities Committee in 1931 [source: BAWS Vol. 2, contents](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=16).
- "A Scheme of Political Safeguards" (with R. Srinivasan): I Equal citizenship (fundamental right abolishing disabilities due to untouchability); II Free enjoyment of equal rights (offence of infringement of citizenship, up to five years; protection against social boycott, citing the 1928–30 Bombay committee); III protection against discrimination; IV adequate representation — adult suffrage and separate electorates for ten years, then joint electorates with reserved seats; V representation in services; and further conditions [source: BAWS Vol. 2, pp. 546–553](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=567).
- Second session: 7 September – 1 December 1931 (Keer; MacDonald's statement of 1 December 1931) [source: Keer, p. 171](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer) [source: BAWS Vol. 9, p. 80](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume9.pdf#page=109).
- Third session: from 17 November 1932 (ended 24 December 1932 per textbooks); Congress absent [source: Keer, p. 223](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). Ambedkar attended all three [source: Pritchett, 1930s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1930s.html).
- Returned to Bombay on 29 January 1932 after the second session (Commons photo caption) [source: Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:On_29_January_1932,_Dr._Babasaheb_Ambedkar_returned_to_Mumbai_after_attending_Second_Round_Table_Conference_in_London.jpg).
- Note to the Lothian (Indian Franchise) Committee, 1 May 1932 [source: BAWS Vol. 2, p. 491](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=510).
- 1933: member of the Joint (Parliamentary) Committee on Indian Constitutional Reform; examined witnesses incl. Sir Michael O'Dwyer, Dr B. S. Moonje, Sir Winston Churchill (24–25 Oct 1933) and Sir Samuel Hoare; "Discussion on Poona Pact" 31 July 1933 [source: BAWS Vol. 2, contents](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=17).

### 13.4 Gandhi and Ambedkar, 1931 (in their own words)
- 6 August 1931 Gandhi wrote asking to meet; 14 August 1931, 2 p.m., Mani Bhavan, Malabar Hill; Ambedkar came with Deorao Naik, Shivtarkar, Pradhan, Bhaurao Gaikwad and Kadrekar; Keer's reconstructed dialogue includes "Gandhiji, I have no homeland" [source: Keer, pp. 164–166](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 53](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=78).
- 28 September 1931: Ambedkar told the Minorities Committee that Gandhi and the Congress "certainly are not in a position to bind us" [source: BAWS Vol. 2, p. 653](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=674).
- 1 October 1931: Ambedkar said Gandhi had told the Federal Structure Committee that the Congress would give political recognition only to Muslims and Sikhs; Gandhi replied, "Who am I to deny political status to any single interest ...?" [source: BAWS Vol. 2, pp. 655–658](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=676).
- 8 October 1931: Gandhi reported failure of informal talks; "I am opposed to their special representation. I am convinced that it can do them no good, and may do much harm"; Congress "wedded to adult franchise"; "the most drastic legislation rendering criminal all the special persecution" [source: BAWS Vol. 2, pp. 659–661](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=680). Ambedkar: "whether I am a nominee or not, I fully represent the claims of my community"; read a telegram from the Depressed Classes Union, Kumaun (Almora) [source: BAWS Vol. 2, pp. 661–662](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=682).
- 13 November 1931: the Minorities Pact (Muslims, Depressed Classes, Anglo-Indians, Europeans, a section of Indian Christians), presented by the Aga Khan, claimed to cover about 46% of British India's population; Gandhi: "I would not sell the vital interests of the Untouchables even for the sake of winning the freedom of India"; "I would far rather that Hinduism died than that Untouchability lived"; "if I was the only person to resist this thing I would resist it with my life" [source: BAWS Vol. 9, pp. 67–69](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume9.pdf#page=96). BAWS Vol. 2 prints the same Gandhi passage straight after the 8 October sitting (with "seal" for "sell" and "fear" for "far") — a layout/typo issue; Vol. 9 and Gandhi's own 1932 letter both date it to 13 November 1931 [source: BAWS Vol. 2, pp. 662–663](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=683) [source: BAWS Vol. 9, p. 82](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume9.pdf#page=111).
- 11 March 1932: Gandhi wrote to Secretary of State Sir Samuel Hoare threatening a fast; Hoare replied 13 April 1932 [source: BAWS Vol. 9, pp. 78–79](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume9.pdf#page=107).

### 13.5 Communal Award
- Announced 17 August 1932 (BAWS); Ambedkar arrived in Bombay the same day and next day wrote to Hoare about paragraph 9 [source: BAWS Vol. 9, p. 79](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume9.pdf#page=108) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, pp. 133–136](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=158). Most modern sources say 16 August 1932.
- Terms (para. 9): Depressed Classes voters vote in general constituencies; special seats from special constituencies where only they vote, in selected areas (whole province in Madras); Bengal to get "not less than 10 seats"; special constituencies to end after 20 years [source: BAWS Vol. 9, pp. 81–82](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume9.pdf#page=110). Centre deferred [source: BAWS Vol. 9, p. 80](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume9.pdf#page=109).
- Seat total: "71" (press report reprinted in BAWS 17-I p. 164 — *Free Press Journal*, 24 Sept 1932; Keer p. 216, reprinted in BAWS 17-I p. 469; P. N. Rajbhoj in the 1930s) vs "78" (Ambedkar, 1945) [source: Keer, p. 216](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 164](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=189) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 469](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=494) [source: BAWS Vol. 9, p. 90](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume9.pdf#page=119). The province-by-province Award table is omitted in BAWS ("as shown in the table"); the original is in the White Paper *Communal Decision* (1932, Cmd. 4147 — command number from memory, not checked). Note: 78 is also the number of reserved seats Ambedkar says Congress won in 1937 [source: BAWS Vol. 9, p. 94](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume9.pdf#page=123).

### 13.6 The fast and the Poona Pact
- Gandhi to MacDonald, 18 August 1932, from Yeravda Central Prison: fast from noon on 20 September unless separate electorates for the Depressed Classes were withdrawn; MacDonald's reply 8 September; Gandhi's answer 9 September ("for me this matter is one of pure religion"; "I sense the injection of poison that is calculated to destroy Hinduism"; "I should not be against even over-representation") [source: BAWS Vol. 9, pp. 82–87](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume9.pdf#page=111).
- Fast began 20 September 1932 [source: BAWS Vol. 9, p. 87](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume9.pdf#page=116). Ambedkar: "I responded to the call of humanity" [source: BAWS Vol. 9, p. 88](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume9.pdf#page=117).
- Negotiators included Sapru, Jayakar, Malaviya, Rajagopalachari, Birla, Chunilal Mehta, M. C. Rajah, P. Baloo; meeting at the jail under a mango tree with Patel and Sarojini Naidu present; "Mahatmaji, you have been very unfair to us" / "I want my compensation" (Keer's account); Gandhi proposed extending the panel system to all seats [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, pp. 157–160](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=182).
- 23 September: Ambedkar demanded 197 seats; leaders offered 126; dispute over a referendum on reserved seats (Ambedkar 25, then 15, then 10 years; Gandhi 5 — "Five years or my life"); panel of four agreed (Ambedkar had wanted two, Gandhi five); death threats to Ambedkar (*Janata*, 24 Sept and 1 Oct 1932) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, pp. 160–165](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=185).
- Signed about 5 p.m., Saturday 24 September 1932, at Shivlal Motilal's bungalow, 1 Bhandarkar Road, Poona; 23 signatories incl. Malaviya, Sapru, Jayakar, Ambedkar, Srinivasan, M. C. Rajah, C. V. Mehta, Rajagopalachari, Rajendra Prasad, G. D. Birla, Solanki, P. Baloo, Devdas Gandhi, P. N. Rajbhoj, G. A. Gavai; Rajah's signature dispute [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, pp. 165–168](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=190).
- 25 September 1932: Hindu leaders' conference in Bombay ratified it and resolved that no one be regarded untouchable by birth and that temple bars be removed by legitimate means; Ambedkar's speech ("so much in common") [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, pp. 169–171](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=194).
- British acceptance and end of fast 26 September 1932 [source: constitutionofindia.net](https://constitutionofindia.net/historical-constitution/poona-pact-1932-b-r-ambedkar-and-m-k-gandhi).
- Text (nine clauses): Madras 30, Bombay with Sind 15, Punjab 8, Bihar and Orissa 18, Central Provinces 20, Assam 7, Bengal 30, United Provinces 20 = 148; joint electorates with primary election of a panel of four; 18% of general seats at the Centre; primary system ends after ten years; reserved seats until ended by mutual agreement; Lothian franchise; no disabilities in local bodies and services; education funds [source: BAWS Vol. 9, pp. 88–89](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume9.pdf#page=117). constitutionofindia.net prints Bombay with Sind as 25 (sum would be 158) [source: constitutionofindia.net](https://constitutionofindia.net/historical-constitution/poona-pact-1932-b-r-ambedkar-and-m-k-gandhi).
- 148 became 151 in the 1935 Act after adjusting Bihar and Orissa; Hammond Committee accepted Ambedkar's views that the panel meant "not more than four" and on cumulative voting [source: BAWS Vol. 9, pp. 91, 94](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume9.pdf#page=120).
- Ambedkar's 1945 verdict: the Award's second vote was "a priceless privilege"; "The Untouchables were sad" [source: BAWS Vol. 9, p. 90](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume9.pdf#page=119).
- Gandhi replaced *Young India* with *Harijan* in 1933 and undertook a 21-day fast [source: Pritchett, 1930s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1930s.html).

### 13.7 1933–1935: Law College, Rajgriha, Ramabai
- House "for books" at Hindu Colony, Dadar (Rajgriha); family photo there, February 1934 [source: Keer, p. 244](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer) [source: Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rajagriha,_Bombay,_February_1934._(L_to_R)_Yashwant,_BR_Ambedkar,_Ramabai,_Laxmibai,_Mukundrao,_and_Tobby.jpg).
- Part-time professor, Government Law College, from June 1934 [source: Keer, p. 244](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- Ramabai died 27 May 1935 after a long illness (six months of decline; earlier a change of air at Dharwar); they had lost three sons and a daughter [source: Keer, pp. 247–248](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). One online article says 26 May (unverified). Pritchett: she wished to visit Pandharpur but he would not let her go where she would be turned away [source: Pritchett, 1930s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1930s.html).
- Principal from 1 June 1935, accepted before her death, took charge mid-June [source: Keer, p. 249](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer); article "Thoughts on the reform of legal education" in the GLC magazine, January 1936 [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part II, pp. 5, 18](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=28); resigned May 1938 [source: Keer, p. 309](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). In 1951 (Jullundur) he recalled being Principal "for five years" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, p. 422](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=451).

### 13.8 Yeola, 13 October 1935
- Before: Kelkar, Bhopatkar, Chitrav and S. M. Mate wrote urging him not to announce conversion [source: Keer, p. 251](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- 12 October: arrived at Nasik 11 a.m., opened a library, inter-caste dinner in Hiralal Lane; 13 October: Vinchur, address from Yeola Municipality (Khairmode, Vol. 6) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, p. 94](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=123).
- Conference "at 10.00 p.m." (BAWS wording; Keer gives no time), about 10,000 people incl. delegates from Hyderabad State and the Central Provinces; reception chairman Amritrao Rankhambe; speech of over 90 minutes; "I solemnly assure you that I will not die a Hindu"; resolution to stop the struggle for equality inside Hinduism and the Kalaram satyagraha (Keer, pp. 252–253) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, pp. 94–96](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=123).
- Reactions: Nasik orthodox Hindus asked for the chariot ban to be lifted; a Sindhi Hindu sent a death threat written in blood (*Vividha Vritta*, 3 Nov 1935); Gandhi (Wardha, 15 Oct): "religion is not like house or cloak, which can be changed at will"; Ambedkar (Bombay, 15 Oct): "Hindu religion is not good for us"; Kavitha village atrocities mentioned by both (*Bombay Chronicle*, 16 Oct 1935) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, pp. 96–99](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=125).
- Follow-ups: Foras Road meeting, Bombay, 8 December 1935 (~10,000); Pune youth conference 11–12 January 1936 [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, pp. 100–101](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=129).
- The Mooknayak (English) reports that in August 1935 he wrote to Bombay Home Secretary Henry Knight that he would end the Kalaram satyagraha by announcing his renunciation of Hinduism (not verified in BAWS) [source: The Mooknayak](https://en.themooknayak.com/books/ambedkar-declaration-renounce-hinduism-yeola-1935).

## 15. Where the original records are held

- **BAWS volumes** (Government of Maharashtra, Education Department; reprinted by the Dr. Ambedkar Foundation, Ministry of Social Justice): Vol. 1 (Southborough evidence), Vol. 2 (Bombay Legislature, Simon Commission, RTC, Joint Committee), Vol. 9 (*What Congress and Gandhi Have Done to the Untouchables*, 1945), Vol. 17 Parts I–III (Mahad, Kalaram, Poona Pact documents, speeches) [source: MEA BAWS page](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf). The BAWS Vol. 2 editors thanked the India Office Library and Records (London), Bombay University Library, the Legislative Council Library (Bombay), Gokhale Institute (Pune), Dr. Ambedkar Research Institute (Nagpur) and the Director of Archives, Government of Maharashtra [source: BAWS Vol. 2 introduction, 2014 reprint](https://archive.org/details/PARI.dr-babasaheb-ambedkar-writings-and-speeches-vol-2).
- **British Library, India Office Records, series IOR/Q/RTC (Round Table Conference)**, within IOR/Q (Commission, Committee and Conference Records). Examples: IOR/Q/RTC/25 — 2nd Session, Minorities Committee (1931), incl. memoranda by Ambedkar and Srinivasan, minutes and stenographic notes of the 7th–10th meetings [source: British Library catalogue](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-000344328); IOR/Q/RTC/31 — 1st & 2nd Sessions, documents (RTC3–RTC19, memoranda on fundamental rights) [source: British Library catalogue](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-000344334). - **Newspapers**: *Mooknayak*, *Bahishkrit Bharat*, *Samata*, *Janata* (Marathi originals; page images of *Mooknayak* and *Bahishkrit Bharat* mastheads on Wikimedia Commons).
