# Research dossier: Dr. Ambedkar's political career, 1936–1956

Scope: everything political from *Annihilation of Caste* (1936) to his plan for the Republican Party (1956). Two topics are left out because other writers cover them: the drafting of the Constitution (1946–1950) and his conversion to Buddhism. This dossier supports two chapters:

- `src/content/life/09-law-minister.md` (1947–1956)

Compiled October 2026. Every fact carries an inline source link, following `research/CONVENTIONS.md`.

## 1. *Annihilation of Caste* (1935–1937)

- **Invitation.** On 12 December 1935 Sant Ram, Secretary of the Jat-Pat-Todak Mandal of Lahore, wrote inviting Dr. Ambedkar to preside over its annual conference. He had at first declined because of differences with the Mandal [source: BAWS Vol. 1, pp. 27–28](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=42). Pritchett dates the invitation to December 1935 [source: Columbia University – Pritchett timeline 1930s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1930s.html).
- **Postponement.** The conference was planned for Easter 1936, then put off to mid-May 1936 [source: BAWS Vol. 1, p. 28](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=43).
- **Who objected.** A letter of 27 March 1936 from Mandal members said almost all Punjab Hindus opposed his invitation. It named Bhai Parmanand, Mahatma Hans Raj, Dr. Gokal Chand Narang and Raja Narendra Nath as having dissociated themselves. Ambedkar countered with a warm letter of 7 February 1936 from Narang himself [source: BAWS Vol. 1, pp. 28–29](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=43).
- **The Har Bhagwan visit.** Har Bhagwan of the Mandal came to Bombay on 9 April 1936 and was given a copy of the speech. His letter of 14 April asked that at least the word "Veda" be left out. Ambedkar ordered 1,000 copies printed [source: BAWS Vol. 1, pp. 29–30](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=44).
- **The breaking point.** Har Bhagwan's letter of 22 April 1936 objected to Ambedkar saying he had "decided to walk out of the fold of the Hindus" and that this was "your last address as a Hindu", and to his attacks on the Vedas [source: BAWS Vol. 1, p. 31](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=46). The Mandal said it would "prefer to postpone the Conference sine die"; Ambedkar replied that he would rather have it cancelled than prune the address [source: BAWS Vol. 1, p. 32](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=47).
- **Publication.** Published 15 May 1936 [source: Columbia University – Pritchett timeline 1930s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1930s.html). The first English edition of 1,500 copies sold out within two months, and it was translated into Gujarati and other languages [source: BAWS Vol. 1, p. 25](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=40). The second edition of 1937 added Gandhi's two *Harijan* articles, his letter to Sant Ram, and Ambedkar's reply. The BAWS text is "printed from the third edition of 1944", whose preface is dated 1 December 1944 [source: BAWS Vol. 1, pp. 25–27](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=40).
- **Verified quotes:**
  - "turn in any direction you like, caste is the monster that crosses your path. You cannot have political reform, you cannot have economic reform, unless you kill this monster." [source: BAWS Vol. 1, p. 47](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=62)
  - "my ideal would be a society based on Liberty, Equality and Fraternity." [source: BAWS Vol. 1, p. 57](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=72)
  - "I am convinced that the real remedy is inter-marriage." [source: BAWS Vol. 1, p. 67](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=82)
  - "Caste is a notion, it is a state of the mind." and "The real remedy is to destroy the belief in the sanctity of the Shastras." [source: BAWS Vol. 1, p. 68](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=83)
- **Related events.** Yeola conversion declaration on 13 October 1935; Sikh Mission Conference at Amritsar, 13–14 April 1936; address to Mahars at Naigaum (Dadar), 31 May 1936 [source: Columbia University – Pritchett timeline 1930s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1930s.html). These belong mainly to the Buddhism story.
- **Image.** The first-edition cover is on Commons (public domain) [source: Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:First_edition_of_Annihilation_of_Caste.jpg).

## 2. Independent Labour Party (ILP), 1936–1942

- **Founded August 1936** [source: Columbia University – Pritchett timeline 1930s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1930s.html). Its aims and programme were printed from *The Times of India* of 15 August 1936 as "Independent Labour Party Publications No. 1" (Shri Laxmi Narayan Press, Bombay) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part II, pp. 413–419](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=438).
- **Why "labour".** He first thought of a party only for the Depressed Classes, but widened the name and programme "at the desire of his friends from other classes". The nucleus would still be the fifteen Depressed Class members, and membership was open to all creeds [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part II, p. 414](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=439). "The word 'Labour' is used instead of the words 'Depressed Classes', because labour includes the Depressed Classes as well." [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part II, p. 415](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=440)
- **Programme** [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part II, pp. 415–419](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=440):
  - Work the 1935 Constitution despite its defects; object to the second chamber.
  - Land mortgage banks and co-operatives; industrialisation to relieve pressure on land; technical education; state management or ownership of industry "whenever it may become necessary".
  - Tenancy laws against khots and talukdars; a minimum standard of living; maximum working hours, leave with pay, pensions, social insurance and cheap housing for workers.
  - Relief for debtors; rent protection for the lower middle class.
  - Social reform laws against outcasting and boycotts; regulation of public charities.
  - Free and compulsory primary education, adult education, regional teaching universities.
  - Separation of the judiciary from the executive; amendment of the Vatan system; "a fair admixture of all castes" in administration.
- **Interview before leaving for Geneva.** He said Congress was "a heterogeneous body composed of the exploiters as well as exploited" and that the ILP would put up fewer candidates to stay principled. Candidates named for general seats: A. V. Chitre (Ratnagiri North), S. V. Parulekar (Ratnagiri South), S. G. Tipnis (Kolaba), V. A. Gadkari (Poona East), C. T. Ranadive (Thana South) and B. V. Pradhan (Khandesh East) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part II, pp. 420–422](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=445). This was *The Times of India*, 12 November 1936, reprinted in Khairmode Vol. 7, pp. 50–53.
- **Earlier union work.** Louise Ouwerkerk (1945) credits him with starting the Bombay Municipal Workers' union in 1934 [source: Round Table India](https://www.roundtableindia.co.in/75-years-of-ambedkar-as-viceroy-s-executive-labour-member/).

## 3. The 1937 Bombay elections

- **Seats.** The ILP contested 17 seats and won 15 [source: Columbia University – Pritchett timeline 1930s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1930s.html) [source: The Leaflet – Sanjoy Ghose, 1 May 2023](https://theleaflet.in/governance-and-policy/the-unknown-ambedkar-indias-first-labour-minister).
- **Ambedkar's own figures.** The ILP "captured 13" of "the 15 seats assigned to the Scheduled Castes in Bombay Presidency … and in addition it won 2 general seats" [source: BAWS Vol. 9, preface p. iii](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume9.pdf#page=26). In the same preface he says the ILP "was started in 1937 just a few months before the elections"; other sources say August 1936.
- **Congress's claim.** Congress publicity said that out of 151 reserved seats across India, the ILP won only 12. Ambedkar called this "a wicked lie" and argued that Congress won only 73 of 151, of which only 38 with a majority of Scheduled Caste votes. Even H. N. Brailsford's *Subject India* had repeated the Congress version [source: BAWS Vol. 9, preface pp. ii–iii](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume9.pdf#page=25).
- **His seat.** Dr. Ambedkar sat for "Bombay City, Byculla and Parel" [source: BAWS Vol. 2, p. 101](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=120). Some debate records say simply "Bombay City" [source: BAWS Vol. 2, p. 94](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=113).
- **Another ILP member.** P. J. Roham, M.L.A. (Ahmednagar), was elected on the ILP ticket and honoured at the Kamgar Maidan on 15 January 1938 [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, p. 172](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=201).
- **Date not verified.** A secondary site gives polling as 17 February 1937, with 13 ILP members and 2 ILP-backed winners; this was seen only in a search summary.

## 4. In the Bombay Legislative Assembly, 1937–1939

### 4a. Khoti Abolition Bill (Bill No. XX of 1937)

- Leave to introduce was granted on 17 September 1937 [source: BAWS Vol. 2, p. 96](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=115). The original record is B.L.A. Debates Vol. 1, pp. 1087–89. The bill's text is in the Bombay Government Gazette, Part V, 21 October 1937, pp. 88–94.
- **Main provisions** [source: BAWS Vol. 2, pp. 96–100](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=115):
  - Short title: "The Khoti Abolition Act, 1937".
  - The government may abolish khoti rights by notification.
  - Compensation is not to exceed 1 per cent of the assessment.
  - Inferior holders become "occupants" under section 3(16) of the Land Revenue Code, 1879, with priority to whoever occupied the land longest in the 12 years before notification.
  - Khots must file statements of holdings since 1920, with fines for failing to do so.
- **Statement of Objects and Reasons** (signed B. R. Ambedkar; H. K. Chainani, Secretary; Poona, 18 October 1937) [source: BAWS Vol. 2, p. 100](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=119):
  - Khoti tenure is found mostly in Ratnagiri and parts of Kolaba and Thana.
  - It is governed by Bombay Act I of 1880 in Ratnagiri, by custom in Kolaba, and by grant in Thana.
  - Inferior holders were "reduced to a state of abject slavery", and "three Khots were murdered" amid the agitation.
- **Opposition.** S. L. Karandikar (Ratnagiri North) opposed introduction because the Revenue Minister had promised a general land-revenue bill. Ambedkar replied that khoti stood outside the Land Revenue Code and offered to withdraw if the government's bill proved better [source: BAWS Vol. 2, pp. 102–103](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=121).
- **Outcome.** Not found in BAWS. A Scribd compilation (seen only in a search summary) says the Congress government did not bring it up for discussion. The later abolition of khoti, said by others to be the Bombay Khoti Abolition Act of 1949, is **not verified** here.

### 4b. Mahar watan: Hereditary Offices Act amendment bills

- **1928 bill.** Bill No. XII of 1928 to amend the Bombay Hereditary Offices Act (Bom. III of 1874), dated 19 March 1928 (Bombay, 13 April 1928, signed with G. S. Rajadhyaksha) [source: BAWS Vol. 2, pp. 72–74](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=91).
  - Objects: better pay for officiating watandars; commutation of inferior watans; conversion of *baluta* (grain dues) into a money cess; freedom from service to ryots; defined duties.
  - In his speeches of 3–4 August 1928 he described Mahars as government servants with undefined duties, paid only through inam land and baluta, and said watans were often suspended on reports by patils and kulkarnis.
  - He warned: "If this bill does not pass, I for myself am not going to be in the Council; I am going to spend the rest of my time in seeing that the Mahars organise a general strike" [source: BAWS Vol. 2, p. 87](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=106).
- **1937 bill.** Bill No. XXIII of 1937, leave granted on 17 September 1937 (B.L.A. Debates Vol. 1, pp. 1091–92). It had three purposes: commutation at the holder's option without losing the land (paying full survey assessment instead), security of remuneration (new sections 19A–19D on converting dues to cess), and duties to be specified by rules [source: BAWS Vol. 2, pp. 94–95](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=113).
- **Haregaon conference, 16 December 1939** (Kopargaon taluka, Ahmednagar district; reported in *Janata*, 23 December 1939) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, pp. 213–214](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=242):
  - Almost all ILP MLAs attended.
  - He traced the 12 village servants (*balutedars*), whose land grants the British commuted for all except the Mahars.
  - He condemned the Congress ministry's increase of the *judi* (concessional land revenue) on watan land as illegal under the 1874 Act.
  - He proposed six months' notice before "direct action" and a deputation to the Governor, calling the Mahar watan "a system of heartless exploitation".
- **Related warning.** A letter warns that watandar Mahars "may go on a strike as per last resolution of the Haregaon Conference" over the extra levy of Rs. 2,201-4-11 a year [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 328](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=353). The printed page is estimated.
- **Not verified.** The final abolition of Mahar watans (often given as 1958–59) was not checked.

### 4c. Peasants' march, 10 January 1938

- Peasants from Thana, Kolaba, Ratnagiri, Satara and Nasik came by train and steamer and marched in three columns, from Parel, the Alexandra Docks and Chowpatty, to the Council Hall. They carried slogans such as "Down with the Khoti System" and "Support Dr. Ambedkar's Bill".
- A deputation met the Premier: Ambedkar with Parulekar, S. C. Joshi, D. W. Raut, Indulal Yagnik and A. V. Chitre.
- **Demands:** minimum wages for farm labour; remission of rent arrears; abolition of khoti, inamdari and landlordism; a 50 per cent cut in irrigation rates for small holders.
- At the Esplanade Maidan meeting he said there were two classes, the haves and the have-nots, and urged a labour front "without any regard to caste or creed" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, pp. 170–171](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=199). Sources given there: *Janata*, 15 January 1938, and Keer pp. 300–301.

### 4d. "Harijan" walkout, January 1938

- When Congress adopted "Harijans" as the official name for the Scheduled Castes, he and his party walked out of the Bombay Legislative Assembly [source: Columbia University – Pritchett timeline 1930s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1930s.html).

### 4e. Industrial Disputes Bill and the strike of 7 November 1938

- **Speech of 15 September 1938** opposing the first reading (B.L.A. Debates Vol. 4, pp. 1330–59) [source: BAWS Vol. 2, pp. 201–232](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=220).
  - The bill was to replace the Bombay Trade Disputes Conciliation Act of 1934.
  - He used Labour Office strike figures for 1921–37 to argue that no emergency existed.
  - He argued that making breach of a contract of service a crime is "involuntary servitude", citing IPC sections 490–492.
  - Verified quote: "the right to strike is simply another name for the right to freedom." [source: BAWS Vol. 2, p. 208](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=227)
- **Nickname for the bill.** The Leaflet says he called it a "Workers Civil Liberties Suspension Act", since it provided six months' jail for joining an illegal strike [source: The Leaflet](https://theleaflet.in/governance-and-policy/the-unknown-ambedkar-indias-first-labour-minister). This was not found in the BAWS speech; treat it as unverified.
- **Ahmedabad, 22 October 1938.** He criticised the Trade Disputes and Tenancy Bills: "I shall stand for principle and will fight alone for it." He also criticised Jinnah's tactics [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 284](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=309). Source: *Bombay Chronicle*, 22 October 1938.
- **The strike.** The one-day protest strike was on 7 November 1938: a Government Inquiry Committee on the "Labour Strike on November 7 last" was sitting by January 1939 [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, p. 201](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=230).
- **His statement afterwards** (*Free Press Journal*, 9 November 1938; the BAWS editors' footnote confusingly adds "The 3rd November 1938"):
  - He demanded "an impartial and public inquiry" into police firing and lathi charges under Home Minister K. M. Munshi.
  - He said Congress could not "manage a single day's strike without resorting to such disgraceful terror", and recalled a past strike lasting seven and a half months [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, pp. 285–286](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=310).
- **ILP Volunteer Corps parade, 8 January 1939**, Kamgar Maidan: over 2,500 volunteers and over 20,000 onlookers. He defended the volunteers against charges made before the inquiry committee, noting that most had served in the army [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, p. 201](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=230).
- **Joint action with the Communists.** Secondary accounts describe the strike as joint ILP–Communist action; this was not verified in the sources opened.

### 4f. Other 1939 events

- **"Federation versus Freedom"**, January 1939, at the Gokhale Institute. In 1939 he said the Untouchables' interests would take precedence over the country's where the two conflicted. In November 1939 he joined Jinnah's "Day of Deliverance", framed as anti-Congress, not anti-Hindu [source: Columbia University – Pritchett timeline 1930s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1930s.html).
- **ILP statement on the European war** [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part II, pp. 306–307](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=331). An earlier ILP statement against the federal scheme called it a "deadly poison" and offered support to Subhas Chandra Bose if Congress fought the scheme [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part II, pp. 302–304](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=327).

## 5. *Thoughts on Pakistan* (1940) and *Pakistan or the Partition of India* (1945, 1946)

- **Editions** (Thacker & Co.): first published December 1940; second edition February 1945; third edition 1946. Government of Maharashtra edition: January 1990 [source: BAWS Vol. 8, edition page](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume8.pdf#page=20).
- **Origin** (prologue) [source: BAWS Vol. 8, pp. 5–6](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume8.pdf#page=30):
  - After the Muslim League's Lahore Resolution, the ILP Executive Council appointed a committee: Ambedkar (chairman), Principal M. V. Donde, S. C. Joshi, R. R. Bhole, D. G. Jadhav and A. V. Chitre, with D. V. Pradhan as secretary.
  - Ambedkar wrote the report, and the Council resolved to publish it.
  - The Council "was attracted to the scheme of Pakistan inasmuch as it meant the creation of ethnic states".
- **Second-edition preface** (1 January 1945, 22 Prithviraj Road, New Delhi) [source: BAWS Vol. 8, pp. 1–3](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume8.pdf#page=26):
  - Part V and his own views were added; the appendices grew from 11 to 25; an index was added.
  - Gandhi and Jinnah cited the book in their talks.
  - The book "is disowned by the Hindus and unowned by the Muslims".
  - He thanked Prof. Manohar B. Chitnis and K. V. Chitre.
- **His conclusion** (Ch. XIII, "Must There be Pakistan?"):
  - "I prefer Freedom of India to the Unity of India" [source: BAWS Vol. 8, p. 367](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume8.pdf#page=392).
  - He preferred partition "as the surest and safest method of providing for the defence of both", while admitting that his fear about the loyalty of the army might be wrong.
  - "Once it becomes certain that the Muslims want Pakistan there can be no doubt that the wise course would be to concede the principle of it." [source: BAWS Vol. 8, p. 368](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume8.pdf#page=393)
- **Problems of Pakistan** (Ch. XIV): the allocation of assets and liabilities, the delimitation of areas, and the transfer of population. He argued that boundaries must be redrawn and that minorities should have safeguards or state-aided transfer, citing the Greece–Turkey–Bulgaria exchanges [source: BAWS Vol. 8, pp. 369–384](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume8.pdf#page=394).
- **Sensitivity.** This is a delicate passage. The chapter quotes only his conclusion, with its conditions. Readers should be told that he called the subject "heart-rending" [source: BAWS Vol. 8, p. 384](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume8.pdf#page=409).

## 6. All India Scheduled Castes Federation (SCF), 1942–1956

- **Founding.** At the All India Depressed Classes Conference, Nagpur, a resolution passed unanimously on 19 July 1942 established the SCF. The constitution was made public on 7 September 1942 (Report of the Conference, Third Session, Annexure 4, pp. 109–121) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part II, p. 455](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=480). The editors give the conference dates as both "17th to 20th July" and "18th, 19th and 20th July".
- **Amended constitution**, printed January 1955 (Thacker's Press), with the first aim "to organise the Scheduled Castes of India" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part II, p. 457](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=482). It was to come into force on 1 May 1957, and amendments were discussed at the Working Committee at Jairaj House, Bombay, on 21 August 1955 [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 435](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=460).
- **Later sessions.** Second session at Kanpur, 29 January 1944; annual conference at Parel, Bombay, 6 May 1945 (published as *The Communal Deadlock and a Way to Solve It*) [source: Columbia University – Pritchett timeline 1940s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1940s.html).
- **Cabinet Mission, 1946** [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part II, pp. 172–237](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=195):
  - Working Committee resolution, Delhi, 2 April 1946: separate electorates, separate settlements, and the view that the Constituent Assembly was "unnecessary".
  - Interview with the Mission, 5 April 1946. Letters to Wavell (3 May), A. V. Alexander (14 May), Pethick-Lawrence (22 May; reply 28 May), Churchill, and Attlee (telegram; memorandum).
  - Hindu–Scheduled Caste clashes in Worli, around 1 June 1946: five killed and nearly seventy injured.
  - A Federation "direct action" resolution.
  - The Attlee correspondence (1 July, 1 August and 12 August 1946) is reproduced in [source: BAWS Vol. 10, pp. 509–515](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=530).
- **His analysis of the 1946 primary elections** [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part II, pp. 226–237](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=249):
  - There were 151 reserved seats; primaries were held in 40 constituencies (Madras 10, Bombay 3, Bengal 12, United Provinces 3, Central Provinces 5, Punjab 7).
  - Congress put up 46 of 283 candidates; 38 of the 168 successful candidates were Congress, against 51 SCF.
  - Congress won 1,03,932 of 3,59,532 primary votes (28 per cent); the SCF won 91,595.
  - He admits that "in the final election the Congress did capture the Scheduled Castes seats".
  - He blamed this on joint electorates, his party's co-operation with the British, and the INA trials.
- **1951–52 election manifesto** [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, pp. 401–403](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=426):
  - Development funds to come from the army budget (Rs. 50 crores), salt (Rs. 30 crores) and excise (Rs. 25 crores).
  - Willing to rename itself the "Backward Classes Federation".
  - No alliance with "any reactionary Party such as the Hindu Mahasabha or the R. S. S.", nor with the Communist Party.
  - Its ideal was a two-party system, possibly through a federal party like the British Labour Party, with possible partners the K.M.P. Party, the Socialist Party and the Justice Party.
- **Interview at Patna, 7 November 1951.** After talks with Jayaprakash Narayan, he would ally with the Socialists and others but not the Communists: "I do not believe in Communism". He also said elections were too hasty [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 406](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=431).
- **First SCF president.** N. Sivaraj is often named as first SCF president and later first RPI president. This was seen only in search summaries; it is unverified here, though BAWS lists "Rao Bahadur N. Shivraj" on the first PES governing body.

## 7. Labour Member of the Viceroy's Executive Council, 1942–1946

### 7a. Appointment and portfolio

- **Royal warrant** "Given at our Court at St. James'" on 9 July 1942, signed by L. S. Amery; he joined on 20 July 1942 [source: BAWS Vol. 10, p. 10](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=27). Source given there: Khairmode Vol. 9, p. 113.
- **Earlier post.** He had been nominated to the National Defence Council by communique of 21 July 1941 (same source).
- **Portfolios.** Labour and the C.P.W.D. (Central Public Works Department): "I held two administrative portfolios" [source: BAWS Vol. 14 Part II, p. 1318](https://archive.org/download/Dr.BabasahebAmbedkarWritingsAndSpeechespdfsAllVolumes/Volume_14_02.pdf#page=538). Mines, power and irrigation questions also came to him; see the Vol. 10 index [source: BAWS Vol. 10, index](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=1094).
- **I. A. Ezekiel** (*Bombay Sentinel*, 1942): no member of the Depressed Classes had held such a high office before; Ambedkar was left out of the first expanded council and nominated after pressure from the Depressed Classes [source: Round Table India](https://www.roundtableindia.co.in/75-years-of-ambedkar-as-viceroy-s-executive-labour-member/).
- **Quit India.** He criticised the 1942 movement [source: Columbia University – Pritchett timeline 1940s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1940s.html). Pritchett quotes him calling it "both irresponsible and insane"; this was not verified in BAWS.
- **Executive Council photographs.** Photographs exist dated July 1942 and 22 March 1944 [source: Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:B._R._Ambedkar_with_notable_persons).
- **Exit.** A Viceroy's House communique of 24 August 1946 says the King accepted the resignation of the Executive Council members; "In the new Ministry Dr. B. R. Ambedkar was not included" [source: BAWS Vol. 10, p. 399](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=416). The Leaflet gives the same date and names Jagjivan Ram as the new labour minister [source: The Leaflet](https://theleaflet.in/governance-and-policy/the-unknown-ambedkar-indias-first-labour-minister). Pritchett says June 1946 [source: Columbia University – Pritchett timeline 1940s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1940s.html); the Columbia CCNMTL page says the same [source: Columbia CCNMTL timeline](https://ccnmtl.columbia.edu/projects/mmt/ambedkar/web/timeline_files/timeline_content07.html).

### 7b. Tripartite machinery

- **First Tripartite Labour Conference**, Delhi, 7 August 1942. It created the Plenary Labour Conference and the Standing Labour Committee. He addressed the first Plenary session on Monday 6 September 1943 (*Indian Information*, 15 September 1943) [source: BAWS Vol. 10, pp. 100–102](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=117).
- **Standing Labour Committee, third meeting**, Bombay, 7–8 May 1943. It discussed employment exchanges (voluntary basis), a fair-wage clause in government contracts, and labour officers [source: BAWS Vol. 10, pp. 78–79](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=95).
- **Seventh Labour Conference** ("which will in future be called the Indian Labour Conference"), New Delhi, 26–28 November 1945 [source: BAWS Vol. 10, pp. 293–300](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=310):
  - He took stock: of the Royal Commission on Labour's 357 recommendations, 133 needed legislation and 126 were accepted. Of 63 ILO conventions, India had ratified only 14.
  - On 27 November the conference unanimously supported the 48-hour week.
  - On 28 November he announced a committee to advise on minimum-wage legislation.
  - Verified quote: "History is not always an example. More often it is a warning." [source: BAWS Vol. 10, p. 296](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=313)
- **The Leaflet's version.** The Leaflet says the working day was cut "from 14 to 8 hours", announced at the "7th session of the Indian Labour Conference in New Delhi on 27 November 1942". It also calls 7 August 1942 the "fourth" Tripartite conference [source: The Leaflet](https://theleaflet.in/governance-and-policy/the-unknown-ambedkar-indias-first-labour-minister). BAWS shows the seventh session was in November 1945 and the change was weekly hours (see 7c), so the Leaflet's year and figures appear to be garbled.

### 7c. Laws and measures, with dates

- **Factories (Amendment) Bill, moved 21 February 1946** (L.A. Debates Vol. I No. 7, pp. 1304–06). It cut maximum weekly hours from 54 to 48 in perennial factories and from 60 to 54 in seasonal ones, and dealt with overtime pay (clause 7) [source: BAWS Vol. 10, pp. 326–333](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=343).
  - His history: the 1919 Washington convention allowed India 60 hours; the Royal Commission on Labour recommended 54, enacted in 1934.
  - His reasons: India's climate, wartime exemptions, and relief of unemployment.
  - His figures: 25–29 per cent of perennial factories already worked 48 hours in 1939–44.
  - N. V. Gadgil interjected "Make it 40."
- **Wavell's version.** The Viceroy's letter of 5 December 1945 (Transfer of Power Vol. VI, No. 268) says Ambedkar's proposal was to cut hours "from 56 hours to 50 hours per week". It also mentions a minimum wages bill ("too ambitious" in Wavell's view), a Workmen's Compensation amendment, a Standing Orders bill, and a bill for compulsory recognition of trade unions [source: BAWS Vol. 10, Part II](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=501).
- **Holidays with pay.** The Factories (Second Amendment) Bill was moved on 1 November 1944, after a 1936 ILO convention; it allowed holidays to accumulate for two years [source: BAWS Vol. 10, pp. 201–210](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=218). Rules were framed "under the Factories (Amendment) Act, 1945" [source: BAWS Vol. 10, p. 292](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=309).
- **Mines Maternity Benefit (Amendment) Bills:**
  - 29 July 1943 (L.A. Debates Vol. III, p. 180) removed the ambiguous words "from work". The 1941 Act gave 8 weeks at 8 annas a day [source: BAWS Vol. 10, pp. 87–88](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=104).
  - 29 March 1945: a select committee, to cover women underground [source: BAWS Vol. 10, p. 264](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=281).
  - 11 April 1945: the select committee's version. Prohibition for 10 weeks before confinement; after confinement, complete prohibition for 26 weeks and partial prohibition for 10 weeks, with rules depending on whether a creche existed [source: BAWS Vol. 10, pp. 274–276](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=291).
- **Coal Mines Labour Welfare Ordinance, 1944**, promulgated around February 1944 (*Indian Information*, 15 February 1944). An Advisory Committee followed [source: BAWS Vol. 10, pp. 137–138](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=154) [source: BAWS Vol. 10, p. 169](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=186).
- **Mica Mines Labour Welfare Fund Bill**: considered 9 April 1946, after a select committee [source: BAWS Vol. 10, pp. 373–384](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=390) [source: BAWS Vol. 10, p. 390](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=407). Enactment as an Act of 1946 is not checked against India Code.
- **Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Bill**: consideration moved 12 April 1946 (L.A. Debates Vol. V No. 9, p. 3914). He called it "a rule of evidence": employers' terms are certified and registered [source: BAWS Vol. 10, pp. 385–389](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=402).
- **Minimum Wages Bill**: leave to introduce on 11 April 1946 (L.A. Debates Vol. V of 1946, p. 3842) [source: BAWS Vol. 10, p. 1063](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=1084). The Minimum Wages Act was passed later, in 1948 (not checked here).
- **Indian Trade Unions (Amendment) Bill** (compulsory recognition of unions): circulated for opinion, 13 November 1943 [source: BAWS Vol. 10, pp. 113–119](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=130). Leave to introduce a fresh bill was granted in February 1946 [source: BAWS Vol. 10, p. 957](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=978).
- **Health insurance**: Prof. Adarkar's report was discussed at the sixth Standing Labour Committee (*Indian Information*, 15 April 1945) [source: BAWS Vol. 10, p. 277](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=294). He linked the Standing Orders bill to a health insurance bill planned for the next session [source: BAWS Vol. 10, p. 386](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=403). That plan later became the ESI Act (1948), which is not checked here.
- **Dearness allowance (DA)**:
  - Motion by Jamnadas Mehta, 12 February 1943 (L.A. Debates Vol. I, pp. 197–99). Ambedkar defended the DA announced in January and February 1943 as not final and described consultations with unions [source: BAWS Vol. 10, pp. 50–52](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=67).
  - The Gregory Committee report on DA went to the Standing Labour Committee [source: BAWS Vol. 10, p. 136](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=153).
  - **Caution:** popular lists say he "introduced DA". BAWS shows him managing and defending wartime DA, not inventing it.
- **Employment exchanges:**
  - Visit to the Calcutta Employment Exchange, 23 August 1944. From January to 31 July 1944, 2,264 technical personnel registered and 537 were placed [source: BAWS Vol. 10, pp. 186–187](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=203).
  - Exchanges in ten centres (Bombay, Ahmedabad, Nagpur, Madras, Calcutta, Dhanbad, Cawnpore, Delhi, Lahore, Karachi), answer around 21 February 1945 [source: BAWS Vol. 10, p. 846](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=867).
  - Directorate-General of Resettlement and Employment; 71 exchanges planned [source: BAWS Vol. 10, pp. 334–338](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=351). Its progress report covers 18 July – 31 December 1945 [source: BAWS Vol. 10, p. 1000](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=1021).
  - Costs of regional exchanges shared 60:40 between the Centre and the provinces [source: BAWS Vol. 10, p. 999](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=1020).
- **Reservation in central services:**
  - Home Department Resolution No. 23/5/42-Ests.(S), 11 August 1943: 8⅓ per cent of direct recruitment reserved for Scheduled Castes [source: BAWS Vol. 10, p. 755](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=776).
  - Resolution in the Gazette of 15 June 1946 raised this to 12½ per cent, "to bring it in accord with the population ratio" (*Indian Information*, 15 July 1946) [source: BAWS Vol. 10, p. 398](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=415).
  - This was a Home Department measure; Ambedkar's role is implied but not spelled out in this source.
- **Women working underground in coal mines:**
  - The ban was lifted in August 1943 (Bengal, Bihar) and December 1943 (Orissa) because of a shortage of male labour. About 16,000 women were then underground [source: BAWS Vol. 10, p. 798](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=819).
  - An adjournment motion on 8 February 1944 was moved by the "Lady Member". He replied that he regarded the decision "as a great misfortune" but not a mistake [source: BAWS Vol. 10, p. 139](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=156).
  - Renuka Ray's cut motion of 13 March 1945 (with Mrs. Subbarayan's earlier protest, and the All-India Women's Conference behind them) [source: BAWS Vol. 10, pp. 253–259](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=270).
  - The ban was reimposed from 1 February 1946; about 20,000 women were affected and about half found other work [source: BAWS Vol. 10, pp. 997, 1012](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=1018).
  - The Leaflet says he went down a 400-ft mine at Dhanbad on 9 December 1943 and then lifted the ban [source: The Leaflet](https://theleaflet.in/governance-and-policy/the-unknown-ambedkar-indias-first-labour-minister). The BAWS index lists "Dhanbad Coalmines visited: 130–34" and "Digwadi Colliery visited: 131".
- **"Labour Charter" of 2 September 1945.** Mentioned in one search summary only. **Not verified.**
- **Bombay Labour Commissioners photo.** A photo exists of him addressing a Conference of Labour Commissioners (date unknown) [source: Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Dr._Ambedkar_as_Labour_Minister_addressing_the_Conference_of_Labour_Commissioners.jpg).

### 7d. Water and electric power

- **Electric power.** The Reconstruction Policy Committee on electric power, chaired by him, met in New Delhi on 25 October 1943 (*Indian Information*, 15 November 1943) [source: BAWS Vol. 10, pp. 120–128](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=137). A question about the creation of a Central Technical Power Board was answered on 7 November 1944 [source: BAWS Vol. 10, p. 792](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=813). In January 1945 he said the government "have created" the Central Technical Power Board and were "contemplating" the CWINC [source: BAWS Vol. 10, p. 222](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=239).
- **CWINC.** The Central Waterways, Irrigation and Navigation Commission was set up in 1945 on Ambedkar's advice; he argued for a central technical body. The final proposal was prepared with Rai Bahadur A. N. Khosla, who became its founder chairman. It is now the Central Water Commission [source: Central Water Commission](https://cwc.gov.in/en/75th_year_cwc).
- **Damodar Valley:**
  - First Calcutta conference, 3 January 1945, with the Bengal and Bihar governments. It discussed the report of the Damodar River Flood Enquiry Committee (appointed by Bengal in 1944) and agreed on a multi-purpose approach (*Indian Information*, 1 February 1945) [source: BAWS Vol. 10, pp. 220–225](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=237).
  - Second conference, 23 August 1945, on the Preliminary Memorandum. Investigation priority: Maithon, then Aiyar, then Sonalapur. Engineers were to be invited from the USA (*Indian Information*, 1 October 1945) [source: BAWS Vol. 10, pp. 284–290](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=301).
  - A 1946 conference on resettling villagers displaced by the scheme: land for land where possible [source: BAWS Vol. 10, pp. 397–398](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=414).
  - The Damodar Valley Corporation itself (said to be set up in 1948) came after his term; not checked here.
- **Orissa rivers / Hirakud:**
  - Cuttack conference, 8 November 1945, with the Central, Orissa, Central Provinces and Eastern States governments (*Indian Information*, 15 December 1945) [source: BAWS Vol. 10, pp. 302–311](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=319).
  - Wavell to Ambedkar, 20 January 1946, hoping Governor Lewis could lay the Sambalpur dam foundation stone before 31 March: "You know my interest in water control and irrigation". Ambedkar replied in February 1946 that Khosla judged the dam "must definitely form part of the Mahanadi Scheme" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part II, pp. 345–346](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=370). Source given there: Thorat, pp. 172–174.
  - **Fairness note:** Wavell, the Bengal and Orissa governments, Khosla and other engineers all shared in these plans.
- **Orissa Review article** ("Dam across Mahanadi", April 2005) describes the Cuttack conference. Its certificate had expired, so it was not opened: `https://magazines.odisha.gov.in/Orissareview/apr2005/englishpdf/damacrossmahanadi.pdf`.

## 8. Books and memoranda, 1943–1947

- ***Ranade, Gandhi and Jinnah***: lecture of 19 January 1943 (Ranade's 101st birth anniversary), published April 1943. ***Mr. Gandhi and the Emancipation of the Untouchables***: September 1943 [source: Columbia University – Pritchett timeline 1940s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1940s.html).
- ***What Congress and Gandhi Have Done to the Untouchables*** (Thacker; first edition June 1945; second edition 1946) [source: BAWS Vol. 9, edition page](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume9.pdf#page=21):
  - It began as a study of the 1937 returns, with figures requested from provincial governments and tables helped by Karan Singh Kane, M.L.A.
  - A "friend and coworker" urged him to widen it into a critique of Congress and Gandhi [source: BAWS Vol. 9, preface](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume9.pdf#page=25).
  - Its epigraph from Thucydides: "It may be your interest to be our masters, but how can it be ours to be your slaves?"
- ***Who Were the Shudras?*** Preface dated 10 October 1946, at "Rajgriha", Dadar [source: BAWS Vol. 7, preface](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume7.pdf#page=38). Inscribed to Mahatma Jotiba Fule, "The Greatest Shudra of Modern India" [source: BAWS Vol. 7, dedication](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume7.pdf#page=23). Its conclusions [source: BAWS Vol. 7, preface](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume7.pdf#page=30):
  - The Shudras were "one of the Aryan communities of the Solar race" and once part of the Kshatriya varna.
  - A feud with the Brahmins led to the Shudras being denied *upanayana* (the sacred-thread ceremony), which reduced them to the fourth varna.
  - Pritchett gives publication as October 1946.
- ***States and Minorities***: preface dated 15 March 1947, "Raja Graha", Dadar [source: BAWS Vol. 1, pp. 383–385](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=398).
  - It is a memorandum for the SCF Working Committee to submit to the Constituent Assembly, published at the urging of Caste Hindu friends.
  - It is drafted as constitutional articles for a "United States of India".
  - He regretted not being elected to the States Committee.
  - It proposes key industries owned and run by the state, insurance as a state monopoly, and agriculture as a state industry with collective farms. It argues "State Socialism is essential for the rapid industrialization of India" and wants this fixed in the constitution so a legislature cannot alter it [source: BAWS Vol. 1, pp. 396, 408–409](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=411).
- **Later works.** *The Untouchables* (1948); *Maharashtra as a Linguistic Province* (October 1948) [source: Columbia University – Pritchett timeline 1940s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1940s.html).
- **Unsubmitted report.** In 1946 he prepared a report on the condition of the Scheduled Castes for the United Nations but did not submit it. The editors could not trace it [source: BAWS Vol. 14 Part II, p. 1320](https://archive.org/download/Dr.BabasahebAmbedkarWritingsAndSpeechespdfsAllVolumes/Volume_14_02.pdf#page=540).

## 9. People's Education Society (PES), Siddharth College and Milind College

- **Loan application**, 1 February 1945, from 22 Prithviraj Road, to Sir Jogendra Singh, Member for Education, Health and Lands [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part II, pp. 423–428](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=448):
  - He asked for an interest-free loan of Rs. 6,00,000 for a college in Bombay.
  - He quoted Hartog Committee (1930) tables on Depressed Class pupils.
  - He argued that Scheduled Castes needed executive, not merely ministerial, posts, and that students dropped out at college because of poverty, admissions, freeships and hostels.
  - The college would be non-communal, with mixed staff, open to all.
  - He named three reasons for Bombay: he would manage and teach there; there was huge demand (ten new college applications were before the Senate); and he had standing with Bombay University.
  - The site was about six acres belonging to the Bombay Municipality.
- **PES**: established 8 July 1945 and registered under Act XXI of 1860 (No. 1375 of 1945-46, dated 9 July 1945), and under the Bombay Public Trust Act (F 303 (Bom.), 2 June 1953). Head office: Anand Bhavan, Dr. Dadabhai Naoroji Road, Fort [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part II, p. 429](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=454).
  - First governing body: B. R. Ambedkar; Rao Bahadur N. Shivraj; D. G. Jadhav; R. R. Bhole; J. H. Subbiah; H. K. Patel; G. T. Meshram; Rao Bahadur S. K. Bole; M. V. Donde; S. C. Joshi; M. B. Samarth [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part II, p. 434](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=459).
  - **Caution:** the memorandum printed in BAWS is a later, amended version. Its clauses requiring Buddhist control date from after 1956.
- **Siddharth College**: opened 20 June 1946 as the first PES institution [source: The Mooknayak – Geetha Sunil Pillai, 20 June 2025](https://en.themooknayak.com/education/june-20-siddharth-college-marks-79-years-of-babasahebs-vision-nurturing-jurists-scholars-leaders-and-stars). Pritchett gives June 1946 [source: Columbia University – Pritchett timeline 1940s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1940s.html).
  - Classes began in army barracks at Marine Lines. In 1951 the college moved to the Fort area into buildings once named Menkawa and Albert, later Buddha Bhavan and Anand Bhavan (Mooknayak).
  - His library and ashes are said to be kept there (Mooknayak); not verified.
  - "Siddharth College Parliament" was inaugurated on 25 September 1947 [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, p. 374](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=403).
- **PES institutions list** (Appendix XII) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part II, pp. 544–545](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=569):
  - Siddharth College of Arts, Science & Commerce, Bombay, 1946.
  - Milind College of Arts, Aurangabad, 1950; Milind College of Science, Aurangabad, 1950.
  - A second "Siddharth College of Arts, Science & Commerce, Bombay", 1953 (probably the commerce college).
  - Siddharth College of Law, 1956.
  - Siddharth Night High School, 1947; Milind Multipurpose High School, 1955.
- **Milind College and Aurangabad:**
  - Commons photos: Ambedkar evaluating land for the college (1949); President Rajendra Prasad laying the foundation stone, with a representative of the Nizam of Hyderabad present (caption: 1 September 1950); and him inspecting the work (1950) [source: Wikimedia Commons – Milind College](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Milind_College). Another photo shows Ambedkar and Prasad at Ellora in September 1950 [source: Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:B._R._Ambedkar_with_notable_persons).
  - The campus is "Nagsen Vana, College Road", where he signed the preface of *Thoughts on Linguistic States* [source: BAWS Vol. 1, p. 140](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=155).
  - On 3 July 1953 in Aurangabad he insisted on English as the medium of instruction at his college [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part II, pp. 385–386](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=410).
  - In July–August 1953 he stayed mostly in Aurangabad working on his colleges [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, p. 497](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=526).
  - Land satyagraha in Marathwada (then in Hyderabad State): about 1,700 arrested; his letter to Minister Bindu of 6 November 1953; withdrawal of the satyagraha on 16 November 1953 [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 426](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=451) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, p. 498](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=527).
  - Principal M. B. Chitnis of Milind College is mentioned at the 1956 Nagpur ceremony [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, p. 532](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=561).
  - **Not verified:** the exact opening date often given online (19 June 1950), and the claim that the foundation stone was laid on 1 September 1951.

## 10. 1946: the Constituent Assembly seat from Bengal (hand-over to the Constitution chapter)

- **Sen.** "In late 1946, Dr. Bhimrao Ramji Ambedkar turned to Bengal". He could not "muster support from within his home constituency in the Bombay Presidency" and relied on SCF activists in eastern India against "stiff opposition from a hostile Congress party" [source: Dwaipayan Sen, *The Decline of the Caste Question* (Cambridge UP), excerpt p. 1](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811084/17761/excerpt/9781108417761_excerpt.pdf). Sen cites Keer, p. 380.
- **Raghavan.** The provincial assemblies elected 296 members in July 1946; Ambedkar was elected from the undivided Bengal legislature with five transferable votes (four were needed) [source: Law and Other Things – Vikram Raghavan, 4 May 2009](https://lawandotherthings.com/yesterday-at-election-rally-in-new/).
  - Because the SCF had fewer than five MLAs there, Raghavan *speculates* that the other votes came from Anglo-Indian, independent Dalit or perhaps Muslim League members.
  - After partition he lost the seat. Congress decided to keep him: M. R. Jayakar's Bombay vacancy, meant for G. V. Mavalankar, went to Ambedkar. Rajendra Prasad wrote to B. G. Kher on 30 June 1947, and Patel to Kher on around 30 June / 1 July 1947 and to Mavalankar on 3 July. He returned in July 1947.
- **Mandal's role.** Oral historian Avishek Biswas says Jogendranath Mandal played a big role in securing his election from Bengal [source: ThePrint – Deep Halder, 23 April 2025](https://theprint.in/opinion/jogendranath-mandal-bengals-ambedkar-backing-jinnah-historys-footnote/2599364/). ## 12. The Hindu Code Bill

### 12a. Background

- **1941 Rau Committee.** The Hindu Women's Rights to Property Act, 1937 led the Home Department to appoint a committee in 1941, known as the Rau Committee, which reported on 19 June 1941. In 1942 it submitted draft bills on succession and marriage, and a Hindu Succession Bill was introduced in 1943 and sent to a joint committee. All this comes from Ambedkar's reply of 9 April 1948 [source: BAWS Vol. 14 Part I, pp. 37–38](https://archive.org/download/Dr.BabasahebAmbedkarWritingsAndSpeechespdfsAllVolumes/Volume_14_01.pdf#page=55).
- **1944 Hindu Law Committee.** By Resolution of 20 January 1944 the Central Government appointed a Hindu Law Committee "for the purposes of formulating a Code of Hindu Law". The Ministry of Law later re-arranged its draft for the Select Committee [source: BAWS Vol. 14 Part I, pp. 249–250](https://archive.org/download/Dr.BabasahebAmbedkarWritingsAndSpeechespdfsAllVolumes/Volume_14_01.pdf#page=267).
- **Earlier laws on women.** The BAWS editorial lists them: the Caste Disabilities Removal Act 1850 through the Hindu Women's Rights to Property Act XVIII of 1937 [source: BAWS Vol. 14 Part I, editorial](https://archive.org/download/Dr.BabasahebAmbedkarWritingsAndSpeechespdfsAllVolumes/Volume_14_01.pdf#page=13).

### 12b. Timeline, from his own resignation statement

[source: BAWS Vol. 14 Part II, pp. 1323–1324](https://archive.org/download/Dr.BabasahebAmbedkarWritingsAndSpeechespdfsAllVolumes/Volume_14_02.pdf#page=543)

- 11 April 1947: introduced.
- 9 April 1948: referred to a Select Committee.

### 12c. What it proposed: his speech of 9 April 1948

C.A. (Leg.) D. Vol. IV, pp. 3628–33 [source: BAWS Vol. 14 Part I, pp. 4–12](https://archive.org/download/Dr.BabasahebAmbedkarWritingsAndSpeechespdfsAllVolumes/Volume_14_01.pdf#page=22)

- **Scope.** It codified seven matters: intestate succession (male and female), the order of heirs, maintenance, marriage, divorce, adoption, and minority and guardianship.
- **Inheritance.** It adopted the Dayabhaga rule of individual ownership in place of the Mitakshara coparcenary and survivorship.
  - The widow, daughter and widow of a pre-deceased son ranked equal with the son.
  - The daughter got half a son's share. Ambedkar noted this was the only new female heir; the others came from the 1937 Act.
  - It recognised more female heirs and ended discrimination by marital or economic status.
  - The mother came before the father.
  - Stridhan was unified, and a son got half a daughter's share in it.
  - A woman's limited estate became an absolute estate, and reversioners' rights were abolished.
  - Dowry was to be held in trust for the woman until she turned 18.
- **Maintenance.** Eleven classes of dependants. A wife could claim separate maintenance if the husband had a loathsome disease, kept a concubine, was cruel, abandoned her for two years, converted, or for any other just cause.
- **Marriage.** Sacramental and civil forms, with ages 18 for the groom and 14 for the bride. Caste, sub-caste and gotra-pravara were no bar. Monogamy was required.
- **Nullity and divorce.** Grounds for nullity and invalidity, with a three-year limit; children stayed legitimate. Seven divorce grounds: desertion, conversion, concubinage, incurable unsound mind, virulent leprosy, communicable venereal disease, and cruelty.
- **Adoption.** The husband needed his wife's consent. A widow could adopt only with a registered deed or will from her husband. Adoptions could be registered.
- **His list of the six key points:** abolition of birth-right and survivorship; half share to daughters; limited estate made absolute; abolition of caste in marriage and adoption; monogamy; divorce.
- **Select Committee proposed** on 9 April 1948 (partial list): Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar, Bakshi Tek Chand, M. Ananthasayanam Ayyangar, G. Durgabai, L. Krishnaswami Bharathi, U. Srinivasa Mallayya, Mihir Lal Chattopadhyay, P. S. Deshmukh, Renuka Ray, P. K. Sen, Ramnarayan Singh and others [source: BAWS Vol. 14 Part I, p. 12](https://archive.org/download/Dr.BabasahebAmbedkarWritingsAndSpeechespdfsAllVolumes/Volume_14_01.pdf#page=30).

### 12d. Opposition and support

- **Outside Parliament** [source: SabrangIndia – L. S. Herdenia, 26 August 2017, drawing on Ramachandra Guha's *India After Gandhi*](https://sabrangindia.in/when-rss-likened-hindu-code-bill-atom-bomb-hindu-society/):
  - The All-India Anti-Hindu Code Bill Committee formed in March 1949 and held hundreds of meetings. The Shankaracharya of Dwarka issued an "encyclical".
  - The RSS held a meeting at the Ram Lila grounds, Delhi, on 11 December 1949, where a speaker called the Bill "an atom bomb on Hindu society".
  - On 12 December RSS workers marched on the Assembly and burned effigies of Nehru and Ambedkar.
  - Swami Karpatri challenged Ambedkar to a debate on the Shastras and referred to his caste. On 16 September 1951 he challenged Nehru, and on 17 September 1951 he marched on Parliament.
- **Inside the Congress** [source: SabrangIndia – Bhavuk, 30 December 2024](https://sabrangindia.in/a-principled-pm-determined-law-minister-nehru-ambedkar-opposition-in-indian-politics/):
  - Rajendra Prasad opposed the Bill, calling it the view of a "microscopic minority". As President he reportedly cited procedural lapses, called the push undemocratic, and threatened to withhold assent.
  - Others named: M. Ananthasayanam Ayyangar (Deputy Speaker); Pattabhi Sitaramayya criticised Ambedkar's manner.
  - Ambedkar wrote to Nehru on 10 August 1951 asking for priority. Nehru wrote to him on 27 September 1951 and to Chief Ministers on 4 October 1951; the article calls the Bill controversial and a non-whip measure.
  - The article cites Chitra Sinha (2012) and Guha (2008).
- **Prasad's note of 15 September 1951 and Setalvad's advice.** These come from a Deccan Herald report on a Supreme Court bench recalling a Lok Sabha monograph. The page returned 403 and was **not opened**; seen only in a search summary.
- **His view of the opposition's size.** In a party meeting "out of 120 only 20 were found to be against it"; 44 clauses passed in about 3½ hours; House divisions on clauses 2, 3 and 4 went overwhelmingly in favour, clause 4 being "the soul of the Hindu Code" [source: BAWS Vol. 14 Part II, p. 1325](https://archive.org/download/Dr.BabasahebAmbedkarWritingsAndSpeechespdfsAllVolumes/Volume_14_02.pdf#page=545).
- **Chief whip.** He blamed "the Minister for Parliamentary Affairs, who is also the Chief Whip" as "the deadliest opponent of the Code". He also said the Benares and Aligarh University Bills and the Press Bill were given priority [source: BAWS Vol. 14 Part II, p. 1324](https://archive.org/download/Dr.BabasahebAmbedkarWritingsAndSpeechespdfsAllVolumes/Volume_14_02.pdf#page=544). The chief whip's name is not given in the statement and is not added here.
- **Nehru's promises he quoted:** 28 November 1949 (the government "will stand or fall" on it), 19 December 1949 and 26 September 1951 [source: BAWS Vol. 14 Part II, pp. 1326–1327](https://archive.org/download/Dr.BabasahebAmbedkarWritingsAndSpeechespdfsAllVolumes/Volume_14_02.pdf#page=546).
- **Supporters.** The All-India Women's Conference and Congress women's groups supported the Bill [source: Wikipedia – Hindu code bills (pointer)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_code_bills).
- **Conference photo.** Ambedkar inaugurated a conference on the Hindu Code Bill in New Delhi on 21 April 1950 [source: Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Dr._Ambedkar_inaugurating_the_conference_on_Hindu_Code_Bill_at_New_Delhi,_21_April_1950.jpg).
- **Annexure II.** A Publications Division book in Hindi, *Hindu Code Bill and its purpose* (1950s), includes press reports of speeches against the Code by the Shankaracharya of Jyotirmath and Swami Karpatriji, and articles for it by Pandit Dharmadeo Vidyavachaspati [source: BAWS Vol. 14 Part II, Annexure II](https://archive.org/download/Dr.BabasahebAmbedkarWritingsAndSpeechespdfsAllVolumes/Volume_14_02.pdf#page=549).

### 12e. Outcome

- The BAWS editorial names the Hindu Succession Act, 1956 and the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 as the Bill's successors [source: BAWS Vol. 14 Part I, editorial](https://archive.org/download/Dr.BabasahebAmbedkarWritingsAndSpeechespdfsAllVolumes/Volume_14_01.pdf#page=11).
- The Hindu Minority and Guardianship Act and the Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act, both of 1956, were the other two [source: Wikipedia – Hindu code bills (pointer)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_code_bills).
- A search summary of the India Code records gave the Hindu Marriage Act as Act 25 of 1955, enacted 18 May 1955, and the Hindu Succession Act as Act 30 of 1956, enacted 17 June 1956. The India Code pages could not be opened from this machine, so these exact dates are **unverified**.

## 13. The resignation, September–October 1951

- **Resignation.** He tendered his resignation on Thursday 27 September 1951, and Nehru accepted it on Friday 28 September. He stayed until the end of the session at Nehru's request [source: BAWS Vol. 14 Part II, p. 1317](https://archive.org/download/Dr.BabasahebAmbedkarWritingsAndSpeechespdfsAllVolumes/Volume_14_02.pdf#page=537).
- **The statement of 10 October 1951** was reprinted from a press copy "found in Ambedkar papers received from the Bombay High Court by the Government of Maharashtra". The editors say he "was not allowed to present his statement in the Lok Sabha by the Speaker", so he left and distributed copies [source: BAWS Vol. 14 Part II, Annexure I note](https://archive.org/download/Dr.BabasahebAmbedkarWritingsAndSpeechespdfsAllVolumes/Volume_14_02.pdf#page=535).
  - Press reports reprinted in Vol. 17 say the Deputy Speaker had fixed 6 p.m. for the statement and Ambedkar refused to speak then. Nehru read out their correspondence at 6 p.m. in his absence. On 12 October H. V. Kamath raised the propriety of this, and Ambedkar said he had "other channels open" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 405](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=430). The *Times of India* item there is dated 11 October 1951, a date the editors themselves doubt.
- **Not because of illness.** His statement of 12 October 1951: the letter of 10 August mentioned illness only to ask for priority, and the resignation letter of 27 September did not mention it [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 404](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=429). The statement itself says: "I am the last man to abandon my duty because of illness." [source: BAWS Vol. 14 Part II, p. 1325](https://archive.org/download/Dr.BabasahebAmbedkarWritingsAndSpeechespdfsAllVolumes/Volume_14_02.pdf#page=545)
- **His five reasons**, with page refs on the Internet Archive copy (PDF 538–547):
  1. **Personal grievances** (pp. 1318–1319): Law was "administratively of no importance"; he was denied Planning; he was left out of cabinet committees.
  2. **Neglect of the Backward Classes and Scheduled Castes** (pp. 1319–1321): no commission appointed more than a year after the Constitution; "The same old tyranny, the same old oppression, the same old discrimination … exists now". He contrasted this with the Prime Minister's concern for Muslims, and cited a Home Department circular that found "NIL" or nearly nil recent Scheduled Caste recruits against the 12½ per cent rule.
  3. **Foreign policy** (pp. 1321–1322): "We have no friends left". Of Rs. 350 crores of revenue, about Rs. 180 crores went to the army. On Kashmir: "the right solution is to partition Kashmir", or a plebiscite only in the Valley; he worried more about the condition of people in East Bengal.
  4. **Cabinet by committee** (pp. 1322–1323): the Cabinet had become "a merely recording and registration office".
  5. **The Hindu Code** (pp. 1323–1326): the "greatest social reform measure"; the "palace on a dung heap"; and "I got the impression that the Prime Minister, although sincere, had not the earnestness and determination required to get the Hindu Code Bill through."
- **Leader of the Opposition.** Pritchett says he "became Leader of the Opposition" after resigning [source: Columbia University – Pritchett timeline 1950s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1950s.html). No supporting record was found, and no formal post of that name existed in the Provisional Parliament. Treat with caution.

## 17. "Conditions Precedent for the Successful Working of Democracy", Poona, 22 December 1952

- **Occasion.** Poona District Law Library: he unveiled L. R. Gokhale's portrait and opened the book collections given by A. B. Sethna and H. V. Tulpule. P. C. Bhat (District and Sessions Judge) presided, and Savita Ambedkar attended. The text was printed as a booklet by V. B. Gogate (Lokasangraha Press) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, pp. 472–486](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=501).
- **Definition.** He cited Bagehot ("government by discussion") and Lincoln, then gave his own definition: a form and method of government by which revolutionary changes in economic and social life are brought about without bloodshed (p. 475).
- **Conditions:**
  1. No glaring inequalities: "There must not be an oppressed class" (p. 475).
  2. An opposition. He noted that the Leader of the Opposition is paid in Britain and Canada (pp. 476–478).
  3. Equality in law and administration, including party favouritism in licences (pp. 478–480).
  4. Constitutional morality: "the conventions of the constitution". He gave examples from Washington and from Laski on the Labour Party (pp. 480–482).
  5. No tyranny of the majority. He criticised Bombay Assembly ministers Morarji Desai, Munshi and Kher, with Speaker Mavalankar, for blocking adjournment motions (pp. 482–483).
  6. A moral order in society, citing Laski (p. 483).
  7. A public conscience, with examples of Jews in England and of Reverend Scott and white youths in South Africa (pp. 484–485).
- **Warnings.** He warned against "smug feeling" and cited Syria, Egypt (Naguib), and the post-1919 states (pp. 485–486). Verified quotes: "there must be no glaring inequalities in the society." and "democracy is not a plant which grows everywhere." [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, p. 485](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=514)
- **Related.** The same chapter heading, "Conditions precedent for the successful working of democracy", is Chapter 2 of his Republican Party blueprint (see §19).
- **Other 1952–53 events:**
  - Columbia University honorary LL.D., 5 June 1952 [source: Columbia University – Pritchett timeline 1950s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1950s.html).
  - BBC interview in 1953, pessimistic about Indian democracy (same source).
  - Harrison interviews of 21 and 28 February and 9 October 1953 on Communists in Maharashtra [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 425](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=450).

## 18. Linguistic states

- ***Maharashtra as a Linguistic Province*** (October 1948), for the Linguistic Provinces (Dar) Commission [source: Columbia University – Pritchett timeline 1940s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1940s.html).
- **"Need for Checks and Balances"**, *Times of India*, 23 April 1953 [source: Columbia University – Pritchett timeline 1950s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1950s.html). It is reproduced in BAWS Vol. 1 [source: BAWS Vol. 1, p. 135](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=150).
- ***Thoughts on Linguistic States***: preface dated 23 December 1955, Milind Mahavidyalaya, Nagsen Vana, Aurangabad [source: BAWS Vol. 1, p. 140](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=155).
  - **Against the Commission's map.** He criticised the States Reorganisation Commission's "consolidation of the North and balkanisation of the South" [source: BAWS Vol. 1, p. 143](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=158).
  - **Splitting large states:**
    - Uttar Pradesh into three states, with capitals at Meerut, Cawnpore and Allahabad, each of about 2 crore people.
    - Bihar into two.
    - Madhya Pradesh into Northern and Southern Madhya Pradesh. He calls the Commission's Madhya Pradesh a "monster State" and says even Nehru was surprised at its creation [source: BAWS Vol. 1, pp. 151–152](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=166).
  - **Maharashtra.** Four states: a Maharashtra City State (Bombay), Western, Central and Eastern Maharashtra. Later he speaks of "the three Maharashtrian States". He listed Marathi-speaking talukas given to other states (Belgaum, Khanapur, Chikodi, Supa, Karwar, Nilanga, Ahmadpur, Udgir, Rajura and parts of Vidarbha, 13,89,648 people in all) and called this "political vandalism" [source: BAWS Vol. 1, pp. 152–165](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=167).
  - **Seven principles** (p. 165): abandon mixed states; "One State, one language"; not "one language, one State"; size to depend on administration, needs, sentiment and the minority ratio; small states; minority protection through plural-member constituencies (two or three) with cumulative voting [source: BAWS Vol. 1, p. 165](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=180).
  - **Separate electorates.** "Separate electorates or reservation of seats must not be restored to." [source: BAWS Vol. 1, p. 170](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=185)
  - **Second capital.** Hyderabad–Secunderabad–Bolarum, to be made a Chief Commissioner's Province, citing the summer capitals of the Mughals (Srinagar) and the British [source: BAWS Vol. 1, pp. 170–171](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=185).

## 19. The Republican Party plan

- *Janata* of 10 December 1955 carried news of a "Republican Party of India", and he corresponded with Ram Manohar Lohia.
- His blueprint had five chapters: how a movement becomes a party; conditions precedent for democracy; why parliamentary government needs an opposition; what a party is; aims and objects. D. T. Rupwate's Marathi translation appeared in the *Prabuddha Bharat* "Republican Party Establishment issue" of 1957 as an "Open letter" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part II, pp. 151–152](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=174).
- **30 September 1956.** The SCF Executive Committee, meeting at his New Delhi residence with him presiding, resolved (resolution No. 2) to dissolve the SCF and establish the "Republican Party" (*Prabuddha Bharat*, 6 and 13 October 1956). At a press conference in Nagpur on 13 October 1956 he said he had already drafted its constitution (same source).
- **Aims.** The book *India Votes* (Chandidas, Morehouse, Clark, Fontera) quotes the RPI's aims: parliamentary democracy and a secular state (same source).
- **Founding.** The RPI was formally founded on 3 October 1957. Eleanor Zelliot is quoted that "the Untouchables would be brought into a larger group" [source: The News Minute – Prashant Bhaware and Sunny Uke, 8 October 2022](https://www.thenewsminute.com/news/urgent-need-revive-ambedkar-s-republican-ideology-168688).
- **Unverified:** the place of the 1957 founding (Nagpur is often given) and N. Sivaraj as first president.
- **Related.** The 1951 SCF manifesto already described a two-party ideal and a federal opposition party (see §6).
