# Research dossier: Dr. Ambedkar's education, 1913–1923 (plus schooling and honorary degrees)

Covers the chapters `src/content/life/03-columbia.md` and `src/content/life/04-london.md`, plus the data file
`src/data/education.json`. Compiled October 2026. Every fact carries an inline source. Where a fact comes from a
secondary writer quoting a book I could not open (Keer, Khairmode, Kadam, Rathore), the note says so.

**Abbreviations used below**

- **LSE file** = *Ambedkar's LSE student file*, 63-page PDF digitised by LSE Library:
  `https://www.lse.ac.uk/library/assets/documents/Ambedkars-LSE-student-file.pdf`. Page numbers are PDF pages.
  The file runs roughly newest to oldest: 1994 bust notes come first and the 1916 forms come last.
- **BAWS** = *Dr. Babasaheb Ambedkar: Writings and Speeches*, CPV PDFs at `https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/VolumeN.pdf`.
  `#page=` numbers are PDF pages; printed page numbers are given where known.
- **Pritchett** = Frances W. Pritchett's Columbia timeline, `https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/`.

## 2. The Baroda scholarship

- **The offer.** The Gaekwar of Baroda announced scholarships for higher education at Columbia. Ambedkar got £11.50 a month for three years [source: Pritchett 1910s, citing Kadam p. 72](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).
- **Term and obligation.** 15 June 1913 to 14 June 1916, with ten years' service to Baroda afterwards (The Beacon, citing Khairmode) [source: The Beacon](https://thebeacon.in/?p=3103). His Baroda service was "a condition of his scholarship" [source: Pritchett 1910s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html). LSE puts it as being "bound to return to India and serve the Baroda State" [source: LSE exhibition](https://www.lse.ac.uk/library/whats-on/online-exhibitions/educate-agitate-organise).
- **Extension.** The Beacon says the Maharaja refused a two-year extension. He then granted one year after a second application backed by Seligman. Ambedkar heard of it two days after reaching Britain in June 1916, and Baroda refused any further extension in 1917 [source: The Beacon](https://thebeacon.in/?p=3103).
- **Channel.** Zelliot says the stipend came through Baroda's Education Department, and that "it is not clear whose idea" the American study was [source: Zelliot 1991](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/graphics/txt_zelliot1991.html).
- **Loan versus grant (unverified).** A search snippet from historyofvadodara.in mentioned that Baroda records first treated the help as a loan of about Rs. 26,000, later written off. Round Table India gave Rs. 20,434. **I did not open either page, so this is unverified.**
- **Earlier help.** At Elphinstone College he already had a Rs. 25/month stipend from Sayajirao, arranged by Dada Keluskar [source: Pritchett 1900s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1900s.html).
- **Gratitude.** *The Evolution of Provincial Finance* (1925) is dedicated to Sayajirao "as a token of my gratitude for his help in the matter of my education" [source: BAWS Vol. 6, PDF p. 67](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume6.pdf#page=67).

## 3. Columbia, 1913–1916

### Residence and daily life
- Hartley Hall for about a week (he disliked the food). In August he moved to the Cosmopolitan Club, 554 West 114th Street, run by Indian students. He then settled in Livingston Hall (now Wallach Hall) with Naval Bhathena, a Parsi [source: Pritchett 1910s, citing Keer pp. 26–27](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html). ThePrint's columnist Urvish Kothari says little is known of how the friendship began [source: ThePrint](https://theprint.in/opinion/parsis-in-baroda-berated-ambedkar-for-his-caste-but-naval-bhathena-changed-everything/331098/).
- He studied in Low Library's rotunda, then the main reading room [source: Pritchett 1910s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).
- Lala Lajpat Rai tried to recruit him to nationalist politics; he stayed aloof [source: Pritchett 1910s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).
- In a Marathi letter from New York to a friend of his father (Keer pp. 26–27), he urged that girls' education go side by side with boys' [source: Pritchett 1910s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).
- He audited extra classes he could not take for credit [source: Zelliot 1991](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/graphics/txt_zelliot1991.html).

### Coursework
- Totals from the Registrar: 29 economics, 11 history, 6 sociology, 5 philosophy, 4 anthropology, 3 politics, plus elementary French and German [source: Pritchett 1910s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).
- A full course list by term, with instructors, was transcribed from a blurry copy of his transcript (grades hidden). Instructor names come from Columbia Bulletins, researched by Rohini Shukla (2018–19) [source: Pritchett courses](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/graphics/courses.html). Highlights:
  - Seligman: Science of Finance; History of Economics since Adam Smith; Railroad Problems; Seminar in Political Economy & Finance.
  - John Dewey: Psychological Ethics (1914–15); Moral and Political Philosophy (1915–16).
  - James Harvey Robinson: The Protestant Revolt; History of the Intellectual Class in Europe.
  - James T. Shotwell: Origins of European Society; Social and Industrial History of Modern England; Primitive Institutions.
  - Alexander Goldenweiser: General Ethnology; Primitive Religion and Social Organization (1915–16).
  - Franklin Giddings: Principles of Sociology; Theory of Social Evolution.
  - Others: John Bates Clark, Henry R. Seager, Wesley Clair Mitchell, Henry L. Moore, Robert Chaddock, Vladimir Simkhovitch, A. V. W. Jackson (History of India and Persia), Charles A. Beard (Principles of Politics), William R. Shepherd, Edward M. Sait, Alvin S. Johnson, Karl Rathgen.
- A 1920s CV, probably prepared by Ambedkar himself and kept at Columbia, names his professors as Seligman, Clark, Seager, Moore, Mitchell, Chadwick [sic], Simkovitch, Giddings, Dewey and Goldenweiser. Pritchett notes he was admitted to the Graduate School generally, not to a "political science department" [source: Pritchett 1910s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).

### Mentors
- **Seligman** (1861–1939): a friend of Lajpat Rai, a "particularly sympathetic mentor", with years of correspondence [source: Pritchett 1910s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html). He supervised the PhD [source: Zelliot 1991](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/graphics/txt_zelliot1991.html). In his 1925 preface Ambedkar wrote that from Seligman "I learned my first lessons in the theory of Public Finance" [source: BAWS Vol. 6, p. 54 (PDF p. 69)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume6.pdf#page=69).
- **Dewey** (1859–1952): called "my teacher and to whom I owe so much" in *Annihilation of Caste* (1936), §25 [source: Pritchett 1910s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html). Zelliot thought Dewey probably had the greatest influence. She also mentions a 1954 letter asking a student to copy out an out-of-print Dewey book [source: Zelliot 1991](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/graphics/txt_zelliot1991.html).
- **Shotwell** (1874–1965) and **Robinson** (1863–1936): Barnard historians [source: Pritchett 1910s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).
- **Goldenweiser** (1880–1940): his anthropology seminar produced "Castes in India" [source: Pritchett 1910s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).
- **The 1930 quote, checked against the original scan**: "The best friends I have had in my life," Dr. Ambedkar says, "were some of my classmates at Columbia and my great professors, John Dewey, James Shotwell, Edwin Seligman, and James Harvey Robinson." [source: Columbia Alumni News, 19 Dec. 1930, p. 12 – Columbia Archives scan](https://guides.library.columbia.edu/ld.php?content_id=43945313). Pritchett's transcription drops the word "my" before "life" [source: Pritchett 1910s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).

### MA (1915)
- Thesis "Administration and Finance of the East India Company … Submitted in partial fulfilment of the requirements of the degree of Master of Arts, May 15, 1915" [source: BAWS Vol. 6, PDF p. 18](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume6.pdf#page=18).
- Dr. Frank F. Conlon (University of Washington) got a copy from Columbia and gave it to Vasant Moon in 1979. BAWS printed it with Columbia's permission, "in their ownership and possession" [source: BAWS Vol. 6, PDF p. 20](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume6.pdf#page=20).
- Columbia catalogue record (CLIO 4295556) is listed in the Archives guide [source: Columbia Archives](https://guides.library.columbia.edu/uarchives/notables). (CLIO itself blocked automated access.)
- Keer: MA exam passed in June 1915, majoring in economics with sociology, history, philosophy and anthropology; thesis "Ancient Indian Commerce"; honoured at a dinner by the Faculty of Arts [source: Pritchett 1910s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).
- 1951 career note: MA conferred 2 June 1915 [source: LSE file p. 36](https://www.lse.ac.uk/library/assets/documents/Ambedkars-LSE-student-file.pdf#page=36). His 1921 German CV says MA 1914 [source: Bellwinkel-Schempp facsimile](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/graphics/germanvisit.pdf).

### "Castes in India" (1916)
- Header in BAWS: "Paper read before the Anthropology Seminar of Dr. A. A. Goldenweizer at The Columbia University, New York, U.S.A. on 9th May 1916. From: Indian Antiquary, May 1917, Vol. XLI" [source: BAWS Vol. 1, PDF p. 18](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=18). Note the BAWS spelling "Goldenweizer". I have not checked whether "Vol. XLI" is the correct *Indian Antiquary* volume number for 1917.
- Main argument on endogamy: BAWS Vol. 1, p. 9 (PDF p. 24) [source: BAWS Vol. 1](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=24). Zelliot says the paper denied that caste arose from race, colour or occupation [source: Zelliot 1991](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/graphics/txt_zelliot1991.html).
- In 1936 he wrote that only lack of time stopped him working this paper into *Annihilation of Caste* [source: Pritchett 1910s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).
- Shukla (2020) discusses the paper in the setting of the study of religion at Columbia (linked from the Archives guide) [source: Columbia Archives](https://guides.library.columbia.edu/uarchives/notables).

**Interpretation, written in the chapter as an inference rather than a fact.** He finished his residence and
examinations by 1916–17. The degree was formally conferred only after a dissertation was printed (1925) and
copies were handed in, so 8 June 1927. The "8 June 1917" date has the same day and month as the transcript
date, so it may come from a misreading of 1927. **That last point is a guess, not established.** The "'28"
form may follow a class-year convention (the 1927–28 academic year). **That is unverified too.**

**Related puzzle: the LSE MSc and the Columbia book.** In September 1920 his LSE form gives his MSc thesis topic as
"the Evolution of Provincial Finance in British India" [source: LSE file pp. 60–61](https://www.lse.ac.uk/library/assets/documents/Ambedkars-LSE-student-file.pdf#page=61).
The MSc was awarded for "Provincial Decentralisation of Imperial Finance in British India" [source: LSE file p. 41](https://www.lse.ac.uk/library/assets/documents/Ambedkars-LSE-student-file.pdf#page=41).
The 1925 book's full title combines both phrases [source: BAWS Vol. 6, PDF p. 66](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume6.pdf#page=66).
Pritchett therefore calls the 1925 book his "London School of Economics M.A. thesis" [source: Pritchett 1920s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1920s.html).
So the same research seems to have served for the LSE MSc (1921) and, once printed, the Columbia PhD (1927).
**No source states this outright.** The book's preface thanks Cannan for reading "the rough draft of a small part of the manuscript" and the India Office's
Financial Secretary, Mr. Robinson, for documents [source: BAWS Vol. 6, p. 54 (PDF p. 69)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume6.pdf#page=69), which also points to London work.

### Columbia honours
- **Honorary LLD.** Proposed by Dr. Gregg M. Sinclair via Dean Carman on 10 Feb. 1950, and via A. H. Sulzberger on 6 March 1950. The card cites a *Reader's Digest* article of March 1950 [source: Columbia honorary degree file, p. 1](https://guides.library.columbia.edu/ld.php?content_id=43945058). Ambedkar cabled that he could attend. The Trustees approved unanimously on 5 May 1952. Taraknath Das, Lecturer in History, was named his escort, and he stayed at the King's Crown Hotel, 420 West 116th St. He flew by TWA from Bombay on 1 June, arriving in New York on 2 June 1952, and accepted Kirk's dinner invitation for 4 June at Faculty House. The citation calls him "one of India's leading citizens—a great social reformer and a valiant upholder of human rights" [source: Columbia honorary degree file](https://guides.library.columbia.edu/ld.php?content_id=43945058) [source: Columbia c250](https://c250.columbia.edu/c250_celebrates/remarkable_columbians/bhimrao_ambedkar.html).

- **Arrival.** Police suspected him of being a revolutionary and searched his clothes and shoes. He carried introductions from Henry R. Seager to Edwin Cannan and from Seligman to Sidney Webb [source: Pritchett 1910s, citing Khairmode I:88–89](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html). Scott Stroud says he travelled in May 1916 [source: Scroll (Stroud excerpt)](https://scroll.in/article/1047299/scroll_in). The Beacon says he reached Liverpool [source: The Beacon](https://thebeacon.in/?p=3103).
- **1916 LSE form.** Register No. 16520; "22nd Yr Lent Term 1916–17 / Summer Term"; London address 21 Cromwell Rd SW; permanent address Bombay; "Bombay & Columbia", M.A.; "Full Course", £10 10s [source: LSE file p. 63](https://www.lse.ac.uk/library/assets/documents/Ambedkars-LSE-student-file.pdf#page=63). 21 Cromwell Road was rented by the India Office for Indian students [source: LSE exhibition](https://www.lse.ac.uk/library/whats-on/online-exhibitions/educate-agitate-organise).
- **Attendance 1916–17.** Geography (81), Political Ideas (26), Social Evolution (105), Social Theory (109) in Michaelmas term. The register shows several absences in Geography and Political Ideas, and "Research" in Lent and Summer terms [source: LSE file p. 62](https://www.lse.ac.uk/library/assets/documents/Ambedkars-LSE-student-file.pdf#page=62). The LSE blog says he was keenest on L. T. Hobhouse [source: LSE History blog 2023](https://blogs.lse.ac.uk/lsehistory/2023/10/25/the-life-and-thought-of-dr-b-r-ambedkar-in-london/). Thesis: Provincial Decentralisation of Indian Finance. Awarded Degree of M.Sc. (Econ) June 1921. Granted Degree of D.Sc. (Econ) Nov. 1923" [source: LSE file p. 59](https://www.lse.ac.uk/library/assets/documents/Ambedkars-LSE-student-file.pdf#page=59).

## 5. The 1917 return and the lost books

- **Leaving.** The LSE Registrar wrote that he "left the School in July 1917" [source: LSE file p. 41](https://www.lse.ac.uk/library/assets/documents/Ambedkars-LSE-student-file.pdf#page=41). The Beacon gives 27 July 1917 [source: The Beacon](https://thebeacon.in/?p=3103).
- **The ship.** Rathore (*Becoming Babasaheb*, Vol. 1, via PTI): books and papers went as cargo on the SS Salsette while he sailed on the SS Kaisar-i-Hind. On 20 July 1917 the Salsette was torpedoed by UB-40 and sank 45 minutes later with 15 crew killed, in the English Channel. The losses included the first draft of his Columbia dissertation, "The National Dividend". He reached Bombay on 21 Aug. 1917 [source: The Week (PTI)](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2025/04/14/del54-ambedkar-thesis.html) [source: ETV Bharat (PTI)](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/!bharat/when-a-german-torpedo-sank-ambedkars-phd-thesis-enn25041404027).
- **Ambedkar's own words (1922).** "Having lost my manuscript of the original thesis when the steamer was torpedoed on my way back to India in 1917 …" [source: Seligman papers via Pritchett](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/graphics/seligmanletter.html).
- **Keer** (p. 32, via Pritchett): his book collection was on a steamer "torpedoed and sunk by a German submarine". He paid his Baroda travel with damages from Thomas Cook and Company for the torpedoed luggage [source: Pritchett 1910s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).
- **LSE**: his ship with "personal belongings and academic work" was torpedoed and his luggage lost [source: LSE exhibition](https://www.lse.ac.uk/library/whats-on/online-exhibitions/educate-agitate-organise).
- **Stroud** (2023): trunks of books "sank to the bottom of the Mediterranean" [source: Scroll](https://scroll.in/article/1047299/scroll_in). This conflicts with Rathore's English Channel. The Salsette is generally recorded as lost off Portland Bill, Dorset. I saw that only in search-result summaries; the Dorset HER page refused my connection.
- **Unverified.** A search summary mentioned a December 1920 letter in a Columbia collection, saying his books including National Monetary Commission publications "went to the bottom of the sea". The collection site is behind a human-only CAPTCHA, so I did not see it.

## 6. Baroda, 1917 (*Waiting for a Visa*, Part Two)

- **Source text.** BAWS Vol. 12, Part I, pp. 661–691. The manuscript is in his own handwriting, found in the People's Education Society's collection and published as a booklet on 19 March 1990 [source: Pritchett edition](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/txt_ambedkar_waiting.html). Part Two is at PDF pp. 694–699 of the CPV PDF [source: BAWS Vol. 12](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume12.pdf#page=694).
- **His own (muddled) dates.** "In 1916 I returned to India … studied at Columbia … from 1913 to 1917. In 1917 I came to London … In 1918 I was obliged to return" [source: BAWS Vol. 12, PDF p. 694](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume12.pdf#page=694). Pritchett's editorial note: "the dates here appear to be a bit confused" [source: Pritchett edition](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/txt_ambedkar_waiting.html).
- **The story.**
  - At the station he asked, "Where to go? Who will take me?" Hindu hotels (vishis) would not take him.
  - A hackney driver suggested a Parsi inn in the camp area. The caretaker noticed he had no *sadra* and *kasti* (Parsi sacred garments) and refused a Hindu.
  - Ambedkar offered to register under a Parsi name and pay Re. 1½ a day.
  - He lived alone in a dark upstairs hall full of junk and bats, with a hurricane lamp. His sister's son, bringing luggage from Bombay, cried at the sight and was sent back.
  - He worked as "a probationer in the Accountant General's Office" from about 10 a.m. to 8 p.m. and applied for a State bungalow, but "the Prime Minister" did not act quickly [source: BAWS Vol. 12, PDF pp. 694–697](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume12.pdf#page=696).
  - On the 11th day, a dozen Parsis with sticks shouted "You scoundrel! You have polluted the Parsi inn!" and gave him until evening.
  - A Hindu friend said "If you come to my home, my servants will go." An Indian Christian friend from a Brahmin family said he must consult his wife.
  - From 4 p.m. he spent five hours in Kamathi Baug, collected his luggage at 8 p.m., and caught the 9 p.m. train [source: BAWS Vol. 12, PDF pp. 697–699](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume12.pdf#page=697).
- **Ending.** "And here I was driven to leave Baroda and return to Bombay after a stay of only eleven days." He adds that "so long a period as 18 years" had not dimmed the memory, and that he learnt "a person who is an untouchable to a Hindu is also an untouchable to a Parsi". BAWS prints "unouchable", a typo [source: BAWS Vol. 12, p. 678 (PDF p. 699)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume12.pdf#page=699).
- **Other versions of the job.**
  - Military Secretary to the Gaikwar (Keer, via Pritchett) [source: Pritchett 1910s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).
  - Joined 31 Aug. 1917 as Military Secretary, resigned Nov. 1917 (The Beacon) [source: The Beacon](https://thebeacon.in/?p=3103).
  - "Finanz- und Rechnungs-Departement Baroda" from Sept. 1917 to March 1918 (his own German CV, 1921) [source: Bellwinkel-Schempp facsimile](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/graphics/germanvisit.pdf).
  - *The Times* (1956): "gave orders for his entry into the Finance Department of the State … refused living accommodation" [source: LSE file p. 22](https://www.lse.ac.uk/library/assets/documents/Ambedkars-LSE-student-file.pdf#page=22).
  - Note: the "eleven days" may refer only to this stay and its ending. The longer dates may cover formal employment. **This reconciliation is not proven.**
- **ThePrint** retells the inn episode (Urvish Kothari, 6 Dec. 2019) [source: ThePrint](https://theprint.in/opinion/parsis-in-baroda-berated-ambedkar-for-his-caste-but-naval-bhathena-changed-everything/331098/). Stroud's excerpt describes his failed job search after Europe [source: Scroll](https://scroll.in/article/1047299/scroll_in).

## 7. Bombay, 1918–1920

- Private tutor and accountant. An investment consultancy failed when clients learned his caste (Keer pp. 37–38) [source: Pritchett 1910s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).
- **Sydenham College.** Professor of Political Economy, through Lord Sydenham, former Governor of Bombay. Colleagues objected to sharing the drinking-water jug (Keer p. 39) [source: Pritchett 1910s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html). The Beacon gives 11 Nov. 1918 to 11 March 1920 and Rs. 450 a month [source: The Beacon](https://thebeacon.in/?p=3103). The college's history page has his photo captioned 1918 and calls Lord Sydenham "the pillar behind the inception of the college" [source: Sydenham College](https://sydenham.ac.in/History/). A Commons image claims he was appointed on 19 Nov. 1918 [source: Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Dr._Babasaheb_Ambedkar_as_Professor_of_Economics_in_1918.jpg). Seligman (1920) described him as "filling the chair of Commerce and Economics at the Sydenham College of Commerce" [source: LSE file p. 56](https://www.lse.ac.uk/library/assets/documents/Ambedkars-LSE-student-file.pdf#page=56). His 1920 LSE form gives his occupation as "Ex-Professor of Economics" [source: LSE file p. 61](https://www.lse.ac.uk/library/assets/documents/Ambedkars-LSE-student-file.pdf#page=61).
- **Publications, 1918.** A review of Bertrand Russell in the *Journal of the Indian Economic Society* (1, 1), and "Small Holdings in India and Their Remedies" (1, 2–3) [source: Pritchett 1910s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).
- **Southborough Committee.** Examined on 27 Jan. 1919 (BAWS Vol. 1, Part IV, ch. 7). The committee was chaired by Lord Southborough; Sir Frank Sly put the questions. He was introduced as a Sydenham professor, an Elphinstone graduate and "an M.A. of the Columbia University" [source: BAWS Vol. 1, PDF pp. 258–289](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=289). He argued "The untouchable classes must have their own men in the Council Hall" [source: BAWS Vol. 1, p. 253 (PDF p. 268)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=268). **A later chapter covers this.**

## 8. Shahu Maharaj and the money for London

- Shahu I of Kolhapur helped start *Mooknayak* (1920). At Mangaon in March 1920 he praised Ambedkar as a future national leader and dined with him. A second conference followed at Nagpur in May 1920 [source: Pritchett 1920s, citing Keer pp. 41–43](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1920s.html).
- In July 1920 he went to London on his own savings plus loans from Naval Bhathena and the Maharaja of Kolhapur (Keer pp. 44–46) [source: Pritchett 1920s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1920s.html). LSE: "He had saved money while teaching at Sydenham College and received help from the Maharaja of Kolhapur" [source: LSE exhibition](https://www.lse.ac.uk/library/whats-on/online-exhibitions/educate-agitate-organise). The Supreme Court says "financial assistance from Chatrapati Shahuji Maharaj of Kolhapur, a personal loan from a friend and his savings" [source: Supreme Court of India](https://www.sci.gov.in/centenary-of-dr-b-r-ambedkars-enrolment-as-an-advocate/).
- Amounts: Shahu gave Rs. 1,500 (The Beacon, citing Pawar, *Rajarshree Shahu Smarak Granth*) [source: The Beacon](https://thebeacon.in/?p=3103). Bhathena lent Rs. 5,000 [source: ThePrint](https://theprint.in/opinion/parsis-in-baroda-berated-ambedkar-for-his-caste-but-naval-bhathena-changed-everything/331098/).
- Later letters to Shahu: 3 Feb. 1921 (about meeting Montagu); 4 Sept. 1921 (asking for a £200 loan). In Nov. 1921 Ramabai wrote that family savings were gone, and he sent Rs. 100 [source: The Beacon](https://thebeacon.in/?p=3103). **I have not seen the originals.**

- **Baroda students' adviser.** W. A. Douglas Rudge of Rugby, "general adviser to the Baroda students", asked LSE on 2 May 1922 whether Ambedkar was still a student [source: LSE file p. 37](https://www.lse.ac.uk/library/assets/documents/Ambedkars-LSE-student-file.pdf#page=37). ## 11. University of Bonn (1921–22)

- **Source.** Maren Bellwinkel-Schempp, "Ambedkar studies at Heidelberg", hosted by Pritchett, with facsimiles of his German letter and CV. She visited Bonn's university archives on 14 Jan. 2003 with help from Dr Thomas Becker and Johannes Arens [source: Bellwinkel-Schempp](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/graphics/germanvisit.pdf).
- **Documents found.**
  - His application to the Prussian ministry of science, art and public education, dated 25 Feb. 1921, stamped as received in Berlin on 28 Feb.
  - A handwritten German CV.
  - The university register entry of 29.4.1921: father's profession "general" (Bellwinkel-Schempp notes he "upgraded his father's military rank"); religion Hindu; previous universities Bombay, Columbia, London; 18 semesters; subject economics; born 14.4.1891 at Mhow.
  - He was removed on 12.1.1922 for not signing up for or attending lectures [source: Bellwinkel-Schempp](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/graphics/germanvisit.pdf).
- **What the letter says** (my summary of the German).
  - He wishes to study economics at Bonn. He knows German because it was his minor for the Columbia doctorate. He is financially independent.
  - Through Prof. Dr. H. Jacobi, Bonn assured him he could take a doctorate if he enrolled for three semesters and submitted a dissertation.
  - He learnt only the day before that applications to Berlin were due by 1 March. He enclosed a copy of a Seligman letter to Prof. Nicholson (Edinburgh) and a certificate from the High Commissioner for India's office about his means [source: facsimile](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/graphics/germanvisit.pdf).
- **CV claims.** Columbia MA "1914", Columbia doctorate "1916". London studies from 1916, interrupted by a stay in India from August 1917 to August 1920, finished March 1921. Baroda Finance and Accounts Department Sept. 1917 to March 1918. Sydenham 1918 to March 1920 [source: facsimile](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/graphics/germanvisit.pdf).
- **Hermann Jacobi** (1850–1937) held Bonn's chair of Indology until 1922. How Ambedkar contacted him is unknown [source: Bellwinkel-Schempp](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/graphics/germanvisit.pdf).
- **Conflicting accounts.** Keer: a three-month stay in Bonn in 1923 (Keer 1995: 49, cited by Bellwinkel-Schempp). The Beacon: research at Bonn was interrupted in March 1923 by Cannan's message about the examiners. Pritchett (1922): planned Bonn research, considered Sanskrit; "the whole project didn't work out". The 1951 career note: LSE "from September 1920 to October 1922 after having studied at Bonn, Germany" [source: Pritchett 1920s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1920s.html) [source: The Beacon](https://thebeacon.in/?p=3103) [source: LSE file p. 36](https://www.lse.ac.uk/library/assets/documents/Ambedkars-LSE-student-file.pdf#page=36).
- **Assessment.** The Bonn archive shows he never studied there in 1921–22. A short visit in 1923 is possible but undocumented in anything I saw.

## 13. DSc (1923): *The Problem of the Rupee*

- **Official chronology** (LSE Registrar to C. B. Khairmode, 21 Dec. 1950; repeated in 1979):
  - October 1922: submitted for the D.Sc. (Econ).
  - March 1923: examined, "the award of the degree was not recommended, but the candidate was permitted to re-present his thesis in a revised form".
  - August 1923: re-entered.
  - November 1923: conferred [source: LSE file pp. 12, 41](https://www.lse.ac.uk/library/assets/documents/Ambedkars-LSE-student-file.pdf#page=41).
- **On the rejection.** The 1979 letter says it is "quite impossible to say what, if any, revisions were made … nor what the examiners' detailed views of it were", since these were confidential to the University of London [source: LSE file p. 12](https://www.lse.ac.uk/library/assets/documents/Ambedkars-LSE-student-file.pdf#page=12).
- **"Revolutionary in character".** A centenary-era LSE note says that "according to one source" the rejection was because the thesis was "revolutionary in character for that period of time", and that the final version was submitted "as originally prepared" [source: LSE file p. 8](https://www.lse.ac.uk/library/assets/documents/Ambedkars-LSE-student-file.pdf#page=8). The 1994 Director's speaking notes repeat this via I. G. Patel [source: LSE file p. 3](https://www.lse.ac.uk/library/assets/documents/Ambedkars-LSE-student-file.pdf#page=3). LSE's exhibition says the original source for the "anti British political views" claim cannot be found [source: LSE exhibition](https://www.lse.ac.uk/library/whats-on/online-exhibitions/educate-agitate-organise).
- **The Beacon's version.** In March 1923 Cannan told him the examiners objected. He resubmitted from Bombay in August 1923 [source: The Beacon](https://thebeacon.in/?p=3103).
- **Publication.** London: P. S. King & Son, Orchard House, Westminster, 1923; printed by Butler & Tanner [source: BAWS Vol. 6, PDF pp. 332–333](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume6.pdf#page=332).
  - Dedication: "to the memory of my father and mother …" [source: BAWS Vol. 6, PDF p. 333](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume6.pdf#page=333).
  - Preface thanks Cannan; "his severe examination of my theoretic discussions has saved me from many an error" [source: BAWS Vol. 6, p. 330 (PDF p. 345)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume6.pdf#page=345). It also thanks Professor Wadia of Wilson College for proofs.
  - Cannan's foreword opens by saying he disagrees "with a good deal of his criticism" [source: BAWS Vol. 6, PDF p. 346](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume6.pdf#page=346).
  - The LSE exhibition mentions a signed dedication to Cannan dated 1924 [source: LSE exhibition](https://www.lse.ac.uk/library/whats-on/online-exhibitions/educate-agitate-organise).
- **Significance.** LSE credits the thesis with influencing the setting up of the Reserve Bank of India [source: LSE exhibition](https://www.lse.ac.uk/library/whats-on/online-exhibitions/educate-agitate-organise). He is "believed to be the first Indian to receive a doctorate from the LSE" [source: LSE exhibition](https://www.lse.ac.uk/library/whats-on/online-exhibitions/educate-agitate-organise).
- **Cannan's later verdict** (to Beveridge, 13 Dec. 1932): "I always said he was by far the ablest Indian we ever had in my time". He recalled "the obstinacy with which he used to hold out even when quite wrong—so unlike most of the Indians who would say black was white to please their supervisor" [source: LSE file p. 30](https://www.lse.ac.uk/library/assets/documents/Ambedkars-LSE-student-file.pdf#page=30).

## 19. Errors spotted in secondary sources

- **Pritchett 1925 entry.** Calls *Evolution of Provincial Finance* his "London School of Economics M.A. thesis"; Columbia lists it as the PhD dissertation [source: Pritchett 1920s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1920s.html) [source: Columbia Archives](https://guides.library.columbia.edu/uarchives/notables).
- **Pritchett 1923 entry.** Calls the London doctorate a "Ph.D."; LSE records say D.Sc. (Econ) [source: LSE file p. 59](https://www.lse.ac.uk/library/assets/documents/Ambedkars-LSE-student-file.pdf#page=59).
- **Pritchett 1952 entry.** "Bicentennial Special Convocation". The correspondence shows the regular 198th Commencement; Columbia's bicentennial was 1954 [source: Columbia honorary degree file](https://guides.library.columbia.edu/ld.php?content_id=43945058).
- **LSE Director's 1994 speaking notes and an older LSE note.** Both say he left LSE in 1917 "to pursue studies at Columbia", which is the wrong order [source: LSE file pp. 3, 8](https://www.lse.ac.uk/library/assets/documents/Ambedkars-LSE-student-file.pdf#page=3).
- **_The Times_ obituary (1956).** Born "April 11, 1893"; scholarship "in 1917 to enter the Columbia University". Both are wrong [source: LSE file p. 22](https://www.lse.ac.uk/library/assets/documents/Ambedkars-LSE-student-file.pdf#page=22). *Political India* (1932) also says "born 1893" [source: LSE file p. 31](https://www.lse.ac.uk/library/assets/documents/Ambedkars-LSE-student-file.pdf#page=31).
- **Supreme Court centenary page.** Dates the Columbia master's thesis to 1916 (BAWS shows May 1915) and spells "Grey's Inn" [source: Supreme Court of India](https://www.sci.gov.in/centenary-of-dr-b-r-ambedkars-enrolment-as-an-advocate/).
- **LSE exhibition.** Attributes Seligman's "exceedingly pleasant fellow" to Foxwell [source: LSE exhibition](https://www.lse.ac.uk/library/whats-on/online-exhibitions/educate-agitate-organise).
- **The Beacon.** Says he was "admitted to the Bar" at Gray's Inn in November 1916 (that was admission to the Inn, not call to the Bar). It also attributes Bonn study to "Heidelberg" via the article's title [source: The Beacon](https://thebeacon.in/?p=3103).
