# Research dossier: Dr. Ambedkar and the Constitution of India (1946–1950)

Compiled 22 October 2026 for chapter 8 (`src/content/life/08-constitution.md`) and `src/data/constitution.json`.
This is the long-term knowledge base. Every fact carries an inline source. Where sources disagree, all versions are listed.

## How the sources were used

- **Constituent Assembly Debates (CAD).** The official record was published by the Lok Sabha Secretariat in 12 volumes. We read the full day-by-day text on constitutionofindia.net (run by the Centre for Law and Policy Research, Bangalore), which reproduces the official volumes. Paragraph numbers below (for example "7.70.172") are that website's numbers, not printed page numbers. Printed CAD page numbers are taken from the footnotes of BAWS Vol. 13, which reprints Dr. Ambedkar's Assembly speeches with page references [source: constitutionofindia.net – blog on primary sources](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/blog/the-making-of-indias-constitution-5-essential-primary-sources/).
- **The official per-day CAD PDFs on the Lok Sabha eParliament library** (`https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/.../cad_DD-MM-YYYY.pdf`) could **not be reached** from our network on 22 October 2026 (connection timed out or refused, also through the browser). They should be checked later.
- **BAWS Vol. 13** (*Dr. Babasaheb Ambedkar: Writings and Speeches*, Vol. 13, ed. Vasant Moon, Govt. of Maharashtra 1994; reprinted by the Dr. Ambedkar Foundation) is entirely about the Constitution. Official PDF: `https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf` (23,211,889 bytes; matches the copy in `archive/baws/Volume13.pdf`). For most of the body, printed page = PDF page − 33. Page text was checked in `library/baws-en/volume-13.jsonl`.
- **Small transcription differences** exist between constitutionofindia.net and BAWS (for example "makes"/"make", "luster"/"lustre", "Will she lost it" typos on the website). Where they differ, both are noted.

## 1. The Constituent Assembly meets (December 1946)

- The Assembly first met in the Constitution Hall, New Delhi, on Monday 9 December 1946 at 11 a.m. Acharya J. B. Kripalani invited Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha to be temporary Chairman [source: CAD 9 Dec 1946 – constitutionofindia.net](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/09-dec-1946/).
- Dr. B. R. Ambedkar presented his credentials and signed the register that day as a member from **Bengal** (listed second under "BENGAL", after Sarat Chandra Bose) [source: CAD 9 Dec 1946](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/09-dec-1946/) [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 5](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=38).
- How he got the Bengal seat: the BAWS editor writes that, "having failed to get elected from Bombay due to Congress opposition", he "managed to enter the Constituent Assembly through the Bengal Assembly with the support of Jogendranath Mandal and other Scheduled Caste members" [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 5 (editor's note)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=38).
- Members were elected by the provincial legislative assemblies under the Cabinet Mission plan. The BAWS editor says 296 members were entitled to attend the inaugural session but only 207 attended, mainly because the Muslim League boycotted it [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 5](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=38). constitutionofindia.net describes the Assembly as "a 389-member body" (the planned total including princely states) [source: constitutionofindia.net – member profile page header](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/members/b-r-ambedkar/).
- Dr. Rajendra Prasad was elected permanent Chairman (President) on 11 December 1946 [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 5](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=38).
- Nehru moved the Objectives Resolution on 13 December 1946 [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 6](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=39). It was adopted on 22 January 1947, "all members standing" [source: CAD 22 Jan 1947](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/22-jan-1947/).
- Dr. Ambedkar's first Assembly speech, 17 December 1946 (CAD Vol. I, pp. 99–103): he said he was surprised to be called so early, criticised the resolution for listing rights without remedies, and said: "I know today we are divided politically, socially and economically. We are a group of warring camps and I may go even to the extent of confessing that I am probably one of the leaders of such a camp. But, Sir, with all this, I am quite convinced that given time and circumstances nothing in the world will prevent this country from becoming one." [source: CAD 17 Dec 1946](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/17-dec-1946/) [source: BAWS Vol. 13, pp. 7–9](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=42). (BAWS prints "to-day".) In the same speech he complained that "the usual formula that no man's life, liberty and property shall be taken without the due process of law, finds no place in the Resolution" [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 8](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=41).

## 2. Committee work in 1947

- The Advisory Committee on Fundamental Rights, Minorities etc. was set up on 24 January 1947 under Sardar Patel, with 50 members including Dr. Ambedkar. He sat on the Fundamental Rights and Minorities sub-committees and submitted a memorandum later published as *States and Minorities* [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 25 (editor's note)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=58). constitutionofindia.net calls *States and Minorities* "a mini-Constitution in itself" [source: constitutionofindia.net – Ambedkar profile](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/members/b-r-ambedkar/).
- He was a member of the Union Constitution Committee (Chairman: Nehru), whose report was submitted on 4 July 1947 [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 25](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=58).
- On 29 July 1947 (as a Bombay member) he explained to the Assembly the origin of the clause for an independent Election Commission: the Fundamental Rights Committee wanted elections "taken out of the hands of the Government of the day" [source: BAWS Vol. 13, pp. 23–24](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=56).

## 3. Losing the Bengal seat and returning from Bombay (1947)

- After the partition of Bengal, Dr. Ambedkar ceased to be a member [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 25](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=58).
- On 30 June 1947 Dr. Rajendra Prasad wrote to B. G. Kher, Premier ("Prime Minister") of Bombay, asking that Dr. Ambedkar be elected immediately: "we have found Dr. Ambedkar's work both in the Constituent Assembly and the various committees to which he was appointed to be of such an order as to require that we should not be deprived of his services" [source: BAWS Vol. 13, pp. 25–26](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=59).
- He was re-elected from Bombay in July 1947 [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 26](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=59), and presented his credentials as a Bombay member on 14 July 1947 [source: CAD 14 Jul 1947](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/14-jul-1947/).
- He joined Nehru's first cabinet, formed on 15 August 1947, as Law Minister [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 26](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=59).
- **Not verified here:** many secondary accounts say his Bengal seat came from the Jessore–Khulna area that went to East Pakistan, and that the Bombay vacancy was created by M. R. Jayakar's resignation. We did not find these details in a primary source during this research. constitutionofindia.net's profile lists his constituency as "West Bengal, Bombay"; "West Bengal" is anachronistic, because in 1946 Bengal was undivided [source: constitutionofindia.net – Ambedkar profile](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/members/b-r-ambedkar/).

## 4. The Drafting Committee (from 29 August 1947)

- **Resolution of 29 August 1947**, moved by Satyanarayan Sinha: a Committee consisting of (1) Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar, (2) N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar, (3) Dr. B. R. Ambedkar, (4) K. M. Munshi, (5) Saiyid Mohd. Saadulla, (6) Sir B. L. Mitter and (7) D. P. Khaitan, "be appointed to scrutinise and to suggest necessary amendment to the draft Constitution of India prepared in the Office of the Assembly" [source: CAD 29 Aug 1947](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/29-aug-1947/) [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 29 (CAD Vol. V, pp. 293–94)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=62).
- On the same day a member raised a point of order that Saadulla had been unseated after the Sylhet referendum, re-elected, and had not yet signed the roll. The President took power to fill vacancies on the Committee (a suggestion of Begum Aizaz Rasul) and the original list was adopted [source: CAD 29 Aug 1947](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/29-aug-1947/).
- **First meeting:** 30 August 1947. "Since August 30th it sat for 141 days during which it was engaged in the preparation of the Draft Constitution." [source: CAD 25 Nov 1949, para 11.165.300](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/25-nov-1949/) [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 1206](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=1239).
- **Who chose the Chairman — sources differ.** Dr. Ambedkar himself said: "I was therefore greatly surprised when the Assembly elected me to the Drafting Committee. I was more than surprised when the Drafting Committee elected me to be its Chairman." [source: CAD 25 Nov 1949](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/25-nov-1949/) [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 1208](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=1241). The BAWS editor's note instead says "On 29th August the Assembly unanimously elected him as Chairman of the Drafting Committee" [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 26](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=59). The 29 August resolution names the members but not a chairman, so Dr. Ambedkar's own account (the Committee elected him) fits the record better.
- **Starting text:** the Constitutional Adviser, Sir B. N. Rau, had prepared a draft of 243 articles and 13 Schedules "as a text for the Drafting Committee to work upon" [source: CAD 25 Nov 1949](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/25-nov-1949/). constitutionofindia.net says the Committee began scrutinising Rau's draft on 27 October 1947 [source: constitutionofindia.net – stages of constitution-making](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/stages-of-constitution-making/).
- **Membership changes (dates not verified):**
  - B. L. Mitter was replaced by N. Madhava Rau. By November–December 1948 Madhava Rau was acting as a Committee member: T. T. Krishnamachari mentions "Dr. Ambedkar and Mr. Madhava Rau" meeting as the Drafting Committee (5 Nov 1948), and a Committee amendment on 9 Dec 1948 stood "in the names of Dr. Ambedkar, Mr. Madhava Rau and Mr. Saadulla" [source: CAD 5 Nov 1948](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/05-nov-1948/) [source: CAD 9 Dec 1948](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/09-dec-1948/). Secondary sources say Mitter resigned because of ill health (not confirmed in a primary source here).
  - D. P. Khaitan died in 1948; the Assembly mourned him on 4 November 1948 [source: CAD 4 Nov 1948](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/04-nov-1948/) [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 47](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=80).
  - T. T. Krishnamachari later served on the Committee: the Foreword to BAWS Vol. 13 (by Vice-President K. R. Narayanan) calls him "a colleague of his in the Drafting Committee" [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. v](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=22), and constitutionofindia.net says that "as a member of the Drafting Committee, he dedicated 4014 hours" [source: constitutionofindia.net – T. T. Krishnamachari](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/members/t-t-krishnamachari/). Secondary sources say he replaced Khaitan; the exact date of his appointment was not found.
- **People who were not Committee members but were vital:** Sir B. N. Rau (Constitutional Adviser) and S. N. Mukherjee (Chief Draftsman). Dr. Ambedkar said "Much greater, share of the credit must go to Mr. S. N. Mukherjee, the Chief Draftsman of the Constitution" [source: CAD 25 Nov 1949](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/25-nov-1949/) [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 1209](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=1242). Rajendra Prasad said B. N. Rau "worked honorarily all the time that he was here" [source: CAD 26 Nov 1949](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/26-nov-1949/).

## 5. The Draft Constitution of February 1948

- Dr. Ambedkar's covering letter to the President of the Assembly is dated New Delhi, 21 February 1948. He wrote that he had been authorised to sign the Draft for the Committee but that "not all the members were present at all the meetings of the Committee", though a quorum was present whenever a decision was taken [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 95 (Gazette reprint)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=128).
- The Draft and the letter were published in the **Gazette of India Extraordinary, Thursday 26 February 1948** (No. CA/99/Cons./47), "for general information" [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 95](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=128). The Gazette text ends with separate notes by Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar, signed "By Order, H. V. R. Iengar, Secretary" [source: BAWS Vol. 13, pp. 314–317](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=347).
- Size: 315 articles and 8 Schedules [source: CAD 4 Nov 1948](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/04-nov-1948/); constitutionofindia.net adds that it was organised in 18 Parts [source: constitutionofindia.net – Draft Constitution 1948](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/committee-report/draft-constitution-of-india-1948/).
- Changes the Committee pointed out in the letter: "Sovereign Democratic Republic" instead of "Sovereign Independent Republic"; a clause about **fraternity** added to the Preamble ("the need for fraternal concord and goodwill in India was never greater than now"); India described as a "Union of States" [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 96](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=129).
- The Draft's Preamble ended "IN OUR CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY this_____ of (day of May, 1948 A.D.)" — the drafters then expected adoption in 1948 [source: constitutionofindia.net – Draft Constitution 1948](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/committee-report/draft-constitution-of-india-1948/).
- Draft article 11 read: "'Untouchability' is abolished and its practice in any form is forbidden. The enforcement of any disability arising out of 'Untouchability' shall be an offence punishable in accordance with law." [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 110](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=143)
- Draft article 15 used "procedure established by law" instead of "due process of law" because "the former is more specific (c.f. Art. XXXI of the Japanese Constitution, 1946)" [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 112](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=145).
- Draft article 37 (now 46): the State "shall promote with special care the educational and economic interests of the weaker sections of the people, and, in particular, of the Scheduled Castes and the scheduled tribes" [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 118](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=151).
- Draft article 292 reserved seats in the House of the People for "the Muslim community and the Scheduled Castes", the scheduled tribes, and the Indian Christian community in Madras and Bombay [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 243](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=276); draft article 305 limited these reservations to ten years unless extended by amendment [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 254](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=287). The final Constitution kept reserved seats only for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes (Articles 330 and 332) [source: Constitution of India (Legislative Dept.), Art. 330](https://www.legislative.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/08/b9a933fb88c0a5afba254256bc55a3f0.pdf#page=220).
- Public consultation: the Draft was "widely circulated to members of the Assembly, provincial governments, central ministries, the Supreme Court and High Courts, and the general public"; comments were reviewed in March and October 1948 [source: constitutionofindia.net – Draft Constitution 1948](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/committee-report/draft-constitution-of-india-1948/). Dr. Ambedkar said on 4 November 1948 that the Draft "has been before the public for eight months" and had been discussed in the Provincial Assemblies of Bombay, C.P., West Bengal, Bihar, Madras and East Punjab [source: CAD 4 Nov 1948](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/04-nov-1948/).

## 6. Introducing the Draft: 4 November 1948 (CAD Vol. VII, pp. 31–44)

- Opening words: "Mr. President, Sir, I introduce the Draft Constitution as settled by the Drafting Committee and move that it be taken into consideration." [source: CAD 4 Nov 1948, para 7.48.190](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/04-nov-1948/) [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 49](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=82)
- "The Draft Constitution as it has emerged from the Drafting Committee is a formidable document. It contains 315 Articles and 8 Schedules." [source: CAD 4 Nov 1948](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/04-nov-1948/)
- He explained that India would have a parliamentary system, with the President in the position of the King under the English Constitution [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 50](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=83); called the Indian federation "a flexible federation" [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 57](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=90); and called the Directive Principles "a novel feature in a Constitution framed for Parliamentary Democracy", comparing them to the old "Instrument of Instructions" [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 65](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=98).
- On constitutional morality (quoting the historian Grote first): "Constitutional morality is not a natural sentiment. It has to be cultivated. We must realize that our people have yet to learn it. Democracy in India is only a top-dressing on an Indian soil, which is essentially undemocratic." [source: CAD 4 Nov 1948, para 7.48.229](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/04-nov-1948/) [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 61](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=94)
- On the village: "What is the village but a sink of localism, a den of ignorance, narrow-mindedness and communalism? I am glad that the Draft Constitution has discarded the village and adopted the individual as its unit." [source: CAD 4 Nov 1948, para 7.48.232](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/04-nov-1948/) [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 62](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=95). This drew strong objections from several members in the following days (for example in the excerpts reprinted in BAWS Vol. 13, pp. 83 and 87) [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 87](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=120).
- Closing: "I feel that it is workable, it is flexible and it is strong enough to hold the country together both in peace time and in war time. Indeed, if I may say so, if things go wrong under the new Constitution, the reason will not be that we had a bad Constitution. What we will have to say is, that Man was vile. Sir, I move." [source: CAD 4 Nov 1948, para 7.48.252](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/04-nov-1948/) [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 70](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=103)
- Stages that followed (BAWS editor): first reading (general discussion) after 4 November; second reading (clause by clause) from 15 November 1948 to 17 October 1949; third reading from 14 November 1949, finished on 26 November 1949 [source: BAWS Vol. 13, pp. 47–48](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=80).

## 7. Tributes and criticism during the first reading (5–9 November 1948)

- **T. T. Krishnamachari, 5 November 1948** (CAD Vol. VII, pp. 231–32): "The House is perhaps aware that of the seven members nominated by you, one had resigned from the House and was replaced. One died and was not replaced. One was away in America and his place was not filled up and another person was engaged in State affairs, and there was a void to that extent. One or two people were far away from Delhi and perhaps reasons of health did not permit them to attend. So it happened ultimately that the burden of drafting this constitution fell on Dr. Ambedkar and I have no doubt that we are grateful to him for having achieved this task in a manner which is undoubtedly commendable." [source: CAD 5 Nov 1948, para 7.49.107](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/05-nov-1948/) [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 72](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=105)
  - **Important context:** the same speech begins by saying "that amount of attention that was necessary ... has not been given to it by the Drafting Committee", and continues "But my point really is that the attention that was due to a matter like this has not been given to it by the Committee as a whole." TTK was criticising the *Committee's* attendance, not Dr. Ambedkar; he wished people like Gopalaswami Ayyangar or Munshi had attended throughout [source: CAD 5 Nov 1948](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/05-nov-1948/). The quotation is therefore praise of Dr. Ambedkar inside a complaint about the Committee as a whole. The BAWS Foreword quotes it (with "One had died" and "happend ... upon") [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. v](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=22).
  - We have **not** tried to name the absent members TTK had in mind; he did not name them.
- Many other members praised the introductory speech; BAWS Vol. 13 reprints excerpts (Frank Anthony, Krishna Chandra Sharma, Biswanath Das, H. Das, Lokanath Misra and others) [source: BAWS Vol. 13, pp. 71–92](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=104).

### Abolition of untouchability (draft art. 11 → Art. 17)
- Adopted on 29 November 1948. When asked whether he wished to reply to K. T. Shah's suggestion, Dr. Ambedkar said simply "No." An amendment to reword it was negatived; the article was adopted and members shouted "Mahatma Gandhi ki Jai" [source: CAD 29 Nov 1948, paras 7.62.181–186](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/29-nov-1948/).
- In the same debate (on draft art. 9, now 15), Dr. Ambedkar said that rivers, streams and canals not covered by article 9 "would certainly be covered by the provisions of article 11 which make any interference with the rights of an untouchable for equal treatment with the members of the other communities an offence" [source: CAD 29 Nov 1948](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/29-nov-1948/).
- Current text of Article 17 [source: Constitution of India, Art. 17](https://www.legislative.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/08/b9a933fb88c0a5afba254256bc55a3f0.pdf#page=40).

### Equality of opportunity and reservation in jobs (draft art. 10 → Art. 16)
- 30 November 1948: he described three points of view to reconcile (equality of opportunity; no reservations at all; reservations for communities "which have not so far had a 'proper look-in'" into the administration). Reservations "must be confined to a minority of seats", and the word "backward" was needed or else "the exception made in favour of reservation will ultimately eat up the rule altogether" [source: CAD 30 Nov 1948](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/30-nov-1948/) [source: BAWS Vol. 13, pp. 391–393](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=424).

### Directive Principles (Part IV; draft art. 37 → Art. 46)
- 22 November 1948 (CAD Vol. VII, pp. 494–95): the Constitution lays down political democracy ("one man, one vote") and also "our ideal is economic democracy"; the word "strive" was used deliberately so that governments must keep trying even "under hard and unpropitious circumstances" [source: BAWS Vol. 13, pp. 351–352](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=384).

### Right to constitutional remedies (draft art. 25 → Art. 32), 9 December 1948
- Exact words: "If I was asked to name any particular article in this Constitution as the most important–an article without which this Constitution would be a nullity–I could not refer to any other article except this one. It is the very soul of the Constitution and the very heart of it and I am glad that the House has realised its importance." [source: CAD 9 Dec 1948, para 7.70.172](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/09-dec-1948/) [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 440](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=473)
  - **Note on wording:** the popular phrase "heart and soul" is a paraphrase. He actually said "the very soul of the Constitution and the very heart of it". The article he meant was draft article 25, which became Article 32. His reply began at CAD Vol. VII, p. 950 [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 436 (footnote)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=469).
- Same speech: these writs could no longer be taken away by an ordinary legislature; and "It is the remedy that makes a right real." (constitutionofindia.net wording; BAWS prints "It is the remedy that make a right real". The next sentence reads "If there is no remedy, there is no right of all" on the website and "... no right at all" in BAWS — both sources contain a typo, so only the first sentence is used as a quote.) [source: CAD 9 Dec 1948, para 7.70.174](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/09-dec-1948/) [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 441](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=474). He called the English writs "knave-proof and fool-proof" [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 439](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=472).
- Other members agreed: Ananthasayanam Ayyangar and others called it "the most important article"; one member called it "the crowning section of the whole chapter" [source: CAD 9 Dec 1948](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/09-dec-1948/).
- Article 25 as amended was adopted the same day [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 443](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=476).

### "Due process" vs "procedure established by law" (draft art. 15 → Art. 21), 13 December 1948
- He laid out both views (trusting the legislature vs trusting judges), said "It is rather a case where a man has to sail between Charybdis and Scylla and I therefore would not say anything. I would leave it to the House to decide in any way it likes." The "due process" amendment was negatived [source: CAD 13 Dec 1948](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/13-dec-1948/) [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 457](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=490). (Article 22 — safeguards on arrest — was added later as draft art. 15A, 16 Sep 1949.)

### Amendment (draft art. 304 → Art. 368)
- Adopted 17 September 1949 [source: CAD 17 Sep 1949](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/17-sep-1949/). On 25 November 1949 he defended it as "a most facile procedure for amending the Constitution" compared with other countries [source: CAD 25 Nov 1949, para 11.165.313](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/25-nov-1949/).

### Safeguards for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes (final text)
- Art. 330 and 332 (reserved seats), Art. 334 (time limit — now eighty years for SC/ST seats, after the 104th Amendment, 2019), Art. 335 (claims to posts "consistently with the maintenance of efficiency of administration"), Art. 338 (now the National Commission for Scheduled Castes; restructured by the 65th Amendment 1990 and 89th Amendment 2003), Art. 340 (commission on backward classes), Art. 341–342 (President's lists of SCs and STs) [source: Constitution of India, Arts. 330–342](https://www.legislative.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/08/b9a933fb88c0a5afba254256bc55a3f0.pdf#page=220).
- Dr. Ambedkar on his own purpose: "I came into the Constituent Assembly with no greater aspiration than to safeguard the interests of the Scheduled Castes." [source: CAD 25 Nov 1949](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/25-nov-1949/) [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 1208](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=1241)

### The Preamble
- Adopted on 17 October 1949, the last day of the second reading: "That the Preamble stand part of the Constitution." — adopted [source: CAD 17 Oct 1949](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/17-oct-1949/). The Drafting Committee had added fraternity to the Preamble (letter of 21 Feb 1948, above).

## 9. The closing speech: 25 November 1949 (CAD Vol. XI, pp. 972–981)

Text read in full on constitutionofindia.net (paras 11.165.299–330) and BAWS Vol. 13, pp. 1206–1218 [source: CAD 25 Nov 1949](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/25-nov-1949/) [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 1206](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=1239). BAWS's footnote misprints the volume as "Vol. X"; 25 November 1949 is in Vol. XI.

- **Facts he gave:** "two years, eleven months and seventeen days since it first met on the 9th of December 1946"; 11 sessions; 165 days, of which 114 on the Draft; Drafting Committee sat 141 days; Rau's draft 243 articles/13 Schedules; first Draft 315 articles/8 Schedules; 386 articles after consideration stage; final 395 articles and 8 Schedules; about 7,635 amendments tabled, 2,473 moved.
- **Credit-sharing:** "The credit that is given to me does not really belong to me." Credit to B. N. Rau, the Drafting Committee members, S. N. Mukherjee and staff "sometimes even beyond midnight"; the Congress Party's discipline; the "rebels" (Kamath, P. S. Deshmukh, Sidhwa, Prof. Saxena, Thakur Das Bhargava, K. T. Shah, H. N. Kunzru); and the President for "not permitting legalism to defeat the work of Constitution making" [source: BAWS Vol. 13, pp. 1208–1209](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=1241).
- **"Drifting committee":** Naziruddin Ahmad had mocked it; Ambedkar replied there is "a difference between drift without mastery and drift with mastery" [source: CAD 25 Nov 1949](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/25-nov-1949/).
- **People, not paper:** "however good a Constitution may be, it is sure to turn out bad because those who are called to work it, happen to be a bad lot. However bad a Constitution may be, it may turn out to be good if those who are called to work it, happen to be a good lot." [source: CAD 25 Nov 1949, para 11.165.309](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/25-nov-1949/)
- **Federalism:** States are not dependent on the Centre for their authority; emergency overriding powers are admitted [source: CAD 25 Nov 1949](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/25-nov-1949/).
- **Will India lose its independence again?** He recalled betrayals in history and warned against parties placing "creed above country" [source: CAD 25 Nov 1949, paras 11.165.318–319](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/25-nov-1949/).
- **The three things "we must do" (the three warnings)** [source: CAD 25 Nov 1949, paras 11.165.323–325](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/25-nov-1949/) [source: BAWS Vol. 13, pp. 1215–1216](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=1248):
  1. "The first thing in my judgment we must do is to hold fast to constitutional methods of achieving our social and economic objectives. It means we must abandon the bloody methods of revolution. It means that we must abandon the method of civil disobedience, non-cooperation and satyagraha. ... These methods are nothing but the Grammar of Anarchy and the sooner they are abandoned, the better for us."
  2. "The second thing we must do is to observe the caution which John Stuart Mill has given ... not 'to lay their liberties at the feet of even a great man, or to trust him with powers which enable him to subvert their institutions'." ... "Bhakti in religion may be a road to the salvation of the soul. But in politics, Bhakti or hero-worship is a sure road to degradation and to eventual dictatorship."
  3. "The third thing we must do is not to be content with mere political democracy. We must make our political democracy a social democracy as well."
- **Life of contradictions:** "On the 26th of January 1950, we are going to enter into a life of contradictions. In politics we will have equality and in social and economic life we will have inequality." [source: CAD 25 Nov 1949](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/25-nov-1949/) [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 1216](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=1249). Also: "graded inequality"; "one man one vote and one vote one value"; "Without fraternity equality and liberty will be no deeper than coats of paint."
- **Ending:** "By independence, we have lost the excuse of blaming the British for anything going wrong. If hereafter things go wrong, we will have nobody to blame except ourselves." [source: CAD 25 Nov 1949, para 11.165.330](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/25-nov-1949/) [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 1218](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=1251)
- The popular name "Grammar of Anarchy speech" comes from the first warning; it is a later nickname, not a title he gave.

## 10. Tributes on 25–26 November 1949

- **Pattabhi Sitaramayya, 25 Nov 1949 (CAD Vol. XI, p. 945):** "Our friend, Dr. Ambedkar, has gone away, else I should have liked to tell him what a steam-roller intellect he brought to bear upon this magnificent and tremendous task : irresistible, indomitable, unconquerable levelling down tall palms and short poppies : whatever he felt to be right he stood by, regardless of consequences." He began by speaking of "the half a dozen men that have fashioned this constitution" [source: CAD 25 Nov 1949](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/25-nov-1949/) [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 1202](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=1235).
- **Rajendra Prasad, 26 Nov 1949 (CAD Vol. XI, p. 994):** "... with what zeal and devotion the members of the Drafting Committee and especially its Chairman, Dr. Ambedkar in spite of his indifferent health, have worked. (Cheers). We could never make a decision which was or could be ever so right as when we put him on the Drafting Committee and made him its Chairman. He has not only justified his selection but has added luster to the work which he has done." (BAWS: "lustre".) He added that "it would be invidious to make any distinction as among the other members of the Committee" [source: CAD 26 Nov 1949](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/26-nov-1949/) [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. 1219](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=1252).
- Prasad's same speech credits the committee chairmen (Nehru, Patel) "for the fundamentals of our Constitution", B. N. Rau for the original draft, and the Drafting Committee for working on Rau's draft [source: CAD 26 Nov 1949](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/26-nov-1949/).

## 11. Adoption, signing, coming into force

- **26 November 1949:** "That the Constitution as settled by the Assembly be passed." — "The motion was adopted. (Prolonged Cheers)." The President then signed ("authenticated") it [source: CAD 26 Nov 1949](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/26-nov-1949/).
- Article 394 brought some provisions into force at once (Arts. 5–9 on citizenship, 60, 324, 366, 367, 379, 380, 388, 391–393) and the rest on 26 January 1950, "referred to in this Constitution as the commencement of this Constitution" [source: Constitution of India, Art. 394](https://www.legislative.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/08/b9a933fb88c0a5afba254256bc55a3f0.pdf#page=283).
- **24 January 1950 (12th and last session day):** Jana Gana Mana declared national anthem (Vande Mataram to be honoured equally); Rajendra Prasad elected first President unopposed; Hindi translation signed by the President; then members signed. Prasad: "There are three copies ready. One is in English completely hand-written and illuminated by artists. The second copy is in print in English. The third copy is also hand-written in Hindi." Nehru signed first; members signed in seating order; Prasad signed last [source: CAD 24 Jan 1950](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/debates/24-jan-1950/).
- Number of signatories: New India Samachar (a PIB publication) says 284 members signed [source: New India Samachar (PIB), p. 12](https://newindiasamachar.pib.gov.in/archive/nov2/english/files/basic-html/page12.html). The CAD record does not give a number.
- **26 January 1950:** commencement. 26 January was chosen to recall the 1929 Purna Swaraj resolution and the Independence Day celebrated on 26 January 1930 [source: New India Samachar (PIB), p. 12](https://newindiasamachar.pib.gov.in/archive/nov2/english/files/basic-html/page12.html).

## 12. The handwritten (calligraphed) originals

- **English original:** calligraphed by **Prem Behari Narain Raizada**; 221 calligraphed sheets of hand-made parchment, each 45.7 × 58.4 cm, about 13 kg; "decorated and illuminated by Nand Lal Bose" with scenes "from the Mohen-jo-Daro and Vedic periods to the Indian freedom movement" [source: Aswal & Mehrotra, *Current Science* 115(4), 2018, p. 788 (CSIR-NPL)](https://www.currentscience.ac.in/Volumes/115/04/0788.pdf).
- **Hindi original:** 252 calligraphed sheets, about 14 kg, calligraphed by "Basantrao Vaidya" (CSIR-NPL spelling) [source: Current Science 2018](https://www.currentscience.ac.in/Volumes/115/04/0788.pdf); "Vasant Krishna Vaidya" in the PIB's New India Samachar ("while Vasant Krishna Vaidya prepared Hindi copy"; the same page says "In all, 284 members actually signed the Constitution") [source: New India Samachar (PIB), p. 12](https://newindiasamachar.pib.gov.in/archive/nov2/english/files/basic-html/page12.html); "Vasant Krishan Vaidya" in many popular sources. These are the same person; the name is spelled differently.
- Both volumes are bound in Morocco leather embossed in gold, held by the Parliament Library, and carry the members' signatures [source: Current Science 2018](https://www.currentscience.ac.in/Volumes/115/04/0788.pdf).
- **Preservation:** CSIR-National Physical Laboratory and the Getty Conservation Institute (USA) signed an agreement in July 1993; two hermetically sealed glass cases (55 × 70 × 25 cm) were installed in the Parliament Library in **March 1994**; the documents are kept "in a nitrogen atmosphere with an oxygen concentration of less than 1% by volume" at 40–50% relative humidity; NPL scientists evaluate the cases every year [source: CSIR-NPL eprint 4027 (full text)](https://npl.csircentral.net/4027/).
- **Illuminators:** Nandalal Bose led the work with students and colleagues of Kala Bhavana, Santiniketan [source: New India Samachar (PIB), p. 12](https://newindiasamachar.pib.gov.in/archive/nov2/english/files/basic-html/page12.html) [source: Wikisource – description of the illuminated edition](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Constitution_of_India_(Original_Calligraphed_and_Illuminated_Version)). The **Preamble page** is credited to **Beohar Rammanohar Sinha** by Wikisource, LiveHistoryIndia and the Saffronart catalogue [source: LiveHistoryIndia (Carol Lobo, 2021)](https://livehistoryindia.com/story/eras/indian-constitution) [source: Saffronart catalogue note, lot 91, July 2024](https://www.saffronart.com/auctions/PostWork.aspx?l=47745). Saffronart's note reads: "The 'Preamble' page was done by Beohar Rammanohar Sinha, Dinanath Bhargava (credited for the emblem of India)", and also names A. Perumal, Vinayak Shivram Masoji, Biswarup Bose, Gauri Bhanja, Jamuna Sen, Amala Bose, Jagdish Mittal, Rajniti Singh, Dhirendra Krishna Deb Barman and Kripal Singh Shekhawat as team members [source: Saffronart](https://www.saffronart.com/auctions/PostWork.aspx?l=47745). We did not find an official government source naming the individual artists.
- **Raizada details (popular/secondary):** used No. 303 nibs; asked only to write his name on each page and his grandfather's name on the last page; "Prem" appears at the bottom of pages [source: LiveHistoryIndia](https://livehistoryindia.com/story/eras/indian-constitution). Wikisource adds that he used a No. 303 nib and worked for Govan Bros., Rampur [source: Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Constitution_of_India_(Original_Calligraphed_and_Illuminated_Version)).
- **Survey of India facsimile:** "one of 1,000 photolithographic reproductions" ... "photolithographed at the Survey of India Offices", Dehra Dun [source: Survey of India – note on the Constitution facsimile](https://surveyofindia.gov.in/documents/the-constitution-of-india.pdf). New India Samachar: about 1,000 copies were printed at the Survey of India's Northern Printing Group, Hathibarkala, Dehradun, and the first printed copy is kept there [source: New India Samachar (PIB), p. 12](https://newindiasamachar.pib.gov.in/archive/nov2/english/files/basic-html/page12.html). The two printing machines were reportedly sold as scrap in 2019 [source: LiveHistoryIndia](https://livehistoryindia.com/story/eras/indian-constitution).
- A digitised Survey of India copy is held by the Library of Congress (World Digital Library item 2672; `https://www.loc.gov/item/57026883/`). We could not open the LoC page (bot-check page), but Wikimedia Commons hosts a copy of that scan, credited to `dl.wdl.org/2672/service/2672.pdf` [source: Wikimedia Commons file page](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Constitution_of_India_(Original_Calligraphed_and_Illuminated_Version).djvu). BAWS Vol. 13 also prints a facsimile of the signature pages 222–223 "from the calligraphic edition published by the Survey of India Offices at Dehra Dun" [source: BAWS Vol. 13, p. xxv](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=20).
- How the handwritten copy came about: members asked the President for "a handwritten copy of the document signed by all members"; Prasad felt a printed document "would not be an appropriate symbol" [source: PRS Legislative Research – Chakshu Roy (Indian Express, 26 Nov 2023)](https://prsindia.org/articles-by-prs-team/a-visually-striking-milestone-of-our-constitutional-history).
- **Attribution in catalogues:** Saffronart catalogues the Survey of India facsimile under the author line "Bhimrao Ramji Ambedkar (Babasaheb Ambedkar), The Constitution of India" [source: Saffronart](https://www.saffronart.com/auctions/PostWork.aspx?l=47745). This library-style author line is one reason people believe he physically wrote the copy.

## 13. Constitution Day (Samvidhan Divas)

- "it was only in 2015 that the Government decided to celebrate November 26 as the 'Constitution Day' every year", as a tribute to Dr. Ambedkar in his 125th birth-anniversary year [source: New India Samachar (PIB), p. 8](https://newindiasamachar.pib.gov.in/archive/nov2/english/files/basic-html/page8.html).
- Secondary sources say the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment notified it in the Gazette on 19 November 2015, and that 26 November had earlier been observed as "Law Day" (from 1979, by the Supreme Court Bar Association). We could not open the Gazette notification itself; treat as unverified.
- 2019 (70th anniversary): mass reading of the Preamble and a function in the Central Hall of Parliament [source: New India Samachar (PIB), p. 12](https://newindiasamachar.pib.gov.in/archive/nov2/english/files/basic-html/page12.html).

## 14. Later reflections by Dr. Ambedkar (outside 1946–1950, for context)

- Rajya Sabha (Council of States), 2 September 1953, Andhra State Bill debate: "People always keep on saying to me : 'Oh, you are the maker of the Constitution'. My answer is I was a hack. What I was asked to do, I did much against my will." and "my friends tell me that I have made the Constitution. But I am quite prepared to say that I shall be the first person to burn it out. I do not want it. It does not suit anybody." [source: BAWS Vol. 15, pp. 860–862](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume15.pdf#page=879)
- Rajya Sabha, 19 March 1955, when reminded of this: "We built a temple for a god to come in and reside, but before the god could be installed, if the devil had taken possession of it, what else could we do except destroy the temple? We did not intend that it should be occupied by the Asuras. We intended it to be occupied by the devas." [source: BAWS Vol. 15, p. 949](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume15.pdf#page=968)
- These show frustration about how the Constitution was being *worked* (especially on minority protection), not a rejection of constitutional democracy.

## 16. Where sources disagree (full list)

1. **Who elected him Chairman.** Ambedkar: the Drafting Committee elected him (CAD 25 Nov 1949). BAWS editor: the Assembly elected him Chairman on 29 August 1947 (BAWS Vol. 13, p. 26). The 29 August resolution lists members only.
2. **Gas in the display cases.** Nitrogen: CSIR-NPL (the laboratory that built and tests the cases) [source](https://npl.csircentral.net/4027/); also Wikisource's description [source](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Constitution_of_India_(Original_Calligraphed_and_Illuminated_Version)). Helium: New India Samachar (PIB) [source](https://newindiasamachar.pib.gov.in/archive/nov2/english/files/basic-html/page12.html), the Wikimedia Commons description copied from the World Digital Library [source](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Constitution_of_India_(Original_Calligraphed_and_Illuminated_Version).djvu), Saffronart and LiveHistoryIndia. We follow CSIR-NPL.
3. **Size and weight of the English original.** 221 sheets, ~13 kg (CSIR-NPL). "251 pages", "3.75 kg" (The Better India, repeated on Wikipedia); "251 pages", 1,17,369 words (LiveHistoryIndia). The difference may be sheets vs. pages, but the weights cannot both be right.
4. **Hindi calligrapher's name.** Basantrao Vaidya (CSIR-NPL); Vasant Krishna Vaidya (New India Samachar); Vasant Krishan Vaidya (popular sources).
5. **When Raizada did the work, and payment.** Saffronart: began 28 November 1949, finished "last week of April 1950", paid "a token amount of Rs. 4000/-" including lodging. Popular accounts: six months' work, no fee, only his name on each page. CAD shows the English copy was "completely hand-written and illuminated" and signed on 24 January 1950, which conflicts with an April 1950 finish (unless later finishing work was meant).
6. **Who illuminated the Preamble page.** Beohar Rammanohar Sinha (Wikisource, LiveHistoryIndia, Saffronart). Saffronart's sentence also names Dinanath Bhargava next to it. No official source found.
7. **How long the Assembly took.** Ambedkar: "two years, eleven months and seventeen days" (counting to 25 Nov 1949). Many sources say "2 years, 11 months, 18 days" (counting to 26 Nov 1949). Days of sitting: 165 days in 11 sessions (Ambedkar, to 25 Nov 1949); constitutionofindia.net counts "167 days of plenary assembly debates from 9 December 1946 to 24 January 1950" [source: constitutionofindia.net](https://www.constitutionofindia.net/members/b-r-ambedkar/).
8. **Number of members.** 389 planned; 296 entitled at the first session, 207 attended (BAWS editor); about 299 after Partition (common figure, not verified here); 284 signed (New India Samachar).
9. **When the Draft was completed and when the Draft letter was published.** Letter dated 21 Feb 1948; Gazette publication 26 Feb 1948. Some sources give only one of the two dates.
10. **"Heart and soul".** Popular phrase; exact words are "the very soul of the Constitution and the very heart of it" (9 Dec 1948).
11. **Transcription.** constitutionofindia.net vs BAWS: "luster"/"lustre"; "makes"/"make"; "upon"/"on"; "happend"; BAWS footnotes print "Vol. X" for 25 Nov 1949 (it is Vol. XI).
12. **Constitution Day announcement.** Secondary sources variously credit a Cabinet decision in 2015, the Prime Minister's announcement on 11 October 2015, and a Gazette notification of 19 November 2015. PIB's New India Samachar says only "in 2015".
13. **Re-election from Bombay.** BAWS says "in July 1947"; the exact polling date and whose seat he filled were not verified.
