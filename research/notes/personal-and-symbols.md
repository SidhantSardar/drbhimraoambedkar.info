## 0. Method, key sources and limits

**Limits**

- The shared web-search budget ran out partway through. After that, only URLs already known could be opened.
- Some sites blocked automated fetching: archive.indianexpress.com, web.archive.org, thehindu.com (2003 archive), structurae.net, theosophy.wiki and the Sri Lankan *Sunday Observer*. Claims that rest only on those sites are marked **unverified** below.
- Page numbers for Keer's 3rd edition were read from the running heads of the OCR text. Treat them as ±1 page.

## 1. Ramabai Ambedkar (c. 1897/98 – 27 May 1935)

### Name and family

- Before marriage she was called Rami. PARI spells the family name Valangkar and gives her home as Vanandgaon in the Konkan, where her father worked as a porter [source: PARI (Namita Waikar, 29 April 2022)](https://ruralindiaonline.org/en/articles/remembering-bhimrao-and-ramabai-ambedkar).
- Keer says she was the second daughter of a "deceased father" who worked as a porter at Dapoli. He names the father as Bhiku Walangkar [source: Keer, pp. 20–21](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- Pritchett names her as the "nine-year-old daughter of Bhiku Dhutre of Wanand, near Dapoli" [source: Pritchett 1900s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1900s.html). **Conflict:** Walangkar or Dhutre.
- After her parents died she lived with her paternal uncle in a chawl in the Byculla market, Mumbai [source: PARI](https://ruralindiaonline.org/en/articles/remembering-bhimrao-and-ramabai-ambedkar).

### Marriage

- **Year.** Pritchett (citing Kadam) and PARI give 1906 [source: Pritchett 1900s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1900s.html) [source: PARI](https://ruralindiaonline.org/en/articles/remembering-bhimrao-and-ramabai-ambedkar). Keer says it came "a short time after his success in the Matriculation Examination", which Pritchett and Keer date to 1907 [source: Keer, p. 20](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). **Conflict:** 1906 or 1907/08.
- **Ages.** Keer: Bhimrao "hardly seventeen", Rami nine [source: Keer, p. 20](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). PARI: Bhimrao 14, Rami 9 [source: PARI](https://ruralindiaonline.org/en/articles/remembering-bhimrao-and-ramabai-ambedkar). Born in April 1891, he would have been 15 in late 1906 and 16 in 1907.
- **Place.** The open shed of the Byculla fish market in Bombay. The two parties slept in opposite corners, stone platforms served as benches, and everyone left before the fisherwomen arrived in the morning [source: Keer, p. 20](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). PARI: at night, at the Byculla chawl, when the market was empty.
- **Earlier betrothal.** Ramji Sakpal had approved one girl and then changed his mind. He paid a fine of five rupees to the caste panchayat to set that betrothal aside [source: Keer, p. 20](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- **Names for each other.** He called her "Ramu"; she called him "Saheb" [source: PARI](https://ruralindiaonline.org/en/articles/remembering-bhimrao-and-ramabai-ambedkar). Keer also says his family and his people called him "Saheb" [source: Keer, p. 245](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).

### Life and sacrifices

- **Learning to read.** He taught her "a little reading and writing" [source: Keer, p. 44](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- **Early schooling.** Khairmode's Marathi biography, translated on the Pritchett site, says she had never attended school and that he tried hard to educate her [source: Pritchett – Khairmode translation](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/khairmode_shukla.html).
- **Home life in Parel.** As a Sydenham College professor he lived frugally in two rooms of the Improvement Trust Chawl at Parel. He gave her a fixed part of his salary to run the house. She also cared for the family of his dead elder brother [source: Keer, p. 43](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- **While he was abroad (1913–17 and 1920–23).** She lived "in a state of extreme destitution" and kept news of illness from him so that his studies would not be disturbed [source: Keer, pp. 43–44](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- **Refusing gifts; the jewellery story.** She refused money from well-wishers. Once, at a friend's residential school where the children had gone hungry for days, she gave her gold jewellery to be sold or pawned for food. The children called her "Rama Aai" or "Ramai" [source: PARI](https://ruralindiaonline.org/en/articles/remembering-bhimrao-and-ramabai-ambedkar).
- **Folk songs.** Ovi (grindmill songs) about Ramabai were recorded at Majalgaon, Beed district, from Parvati Bhadarge and Rangu Potbhare [source: PARI](https://ruralindiaonline.org/en/articles/remembering-bhimrao-and-ramabai-ambedkar).
- **Religious practice.** She observed rigid fasts, taking only water and black gram on Saturdays [source: Keer, p. 248](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). In 1924 the couple still followed the Hindu rituals traditional in his family [source: Pritchett 1920s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1920s.html).
- **Pandharpur.** She longed to visit the temple at Pandharpur. He refused because as an "untouchable" she would have had to pray from a distance. Keer reports him promising they would "create another Pandharpur" through service [source: Keer, p. 248](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer) [source: Pritchett 1930s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1930s.html).
- **Fear for his safety.** She worried about his safety at conferences, and once packed her bags to go with him [source: Keer, p. 248](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). Pritchett records that in 1927 news of threats to his life distressed her greatly [source: Pritchett 1920s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1920s.html).

### Illness and death

- He took her to Dharwar for a change of air, without much improvement. He had "lost three of his sons and one daughter" [source: Keer, p. 247](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- **Date of death.** She died on 27 May 1935 after about six months of serious illness [source: Keer 1954](https://archive.org/details/dli.csl.6522) [source: Keer, p. 248](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). The 3rd-edition OCR misprints the year as "1985". Pritchett says only "May 1935" [source: Pritchett 1930s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1930s.html). Some web articles give 26 May. Those were seen only in search snippets and not checked.
- **His return.** He had come back the night before from a weekend visit to Dr. Sadanand Galvankar at Bassein (Vasai) [source: Keer, p. 249](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- **Funeral.** About 10,000 people attended the procession. He wept "for a week" [source: Keer, p. 249](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
  - The funeral rites were performed by their son in the Hindu tradition, led by a Mahar priest, Sambhoo More.
  - Keer says that a few days earlier he had shaved his head and put on a saffron hermit's robe.
- **Place of death.** Online sources say Rajgriha, Dadar. Keer implies it but does not state it outright (his tribute mentions "the grandeur and glory attached to Rajagriha") [source: Keer, p. 248](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- **Work after her death.** He became Principal of Government Law College, Bombay, from 1 June 1935 [source: Keer, p. 249](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).

### Dedication of *Thoughts on Pakistan*

The full text is verified in two places:

- in BAWS [source: BAWS Vol. 8, dedication page](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume8.pdf#page=22);
- in the 1941 Thacker printing [source: Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/dli.ernet.1802).

> "INSCRIBED TO THE MEMORY OF RAMU As a token of my appreciation of her goodness of heart, her nobility of mind and her purity of character and also for the cool fortitude and readiness to suffer along with me which she showed in those friendless days of want and worries which fell to our lot."

**Editions** [source: BAWS Vol. 8, imprint page](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume8.pdf#page=20):

| Edition | Date |
|---|---|
| Thacker, first edition | December 1940 |
| Second edition, as *Pakistan or the Partition of India* | February 1945 |
| Third edition | 1946 |
| Government of Maharashtra edition | January 1990 |

The scanned Thacker copy's title page is dated 1941. Its prologue explains that the book began as a report to the Independent Labour Party (ILP) Executive Council after the Muslim League's Lahore Resolution [source: Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/dli.ernet.1802). Pritchett also gives December 1940 for the first edition [source: Pritchett 1940s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1940s.html).

### How Ramabai is remembered

- **1942.** A "Ramabai Ambedkar Gate" was among the triumphal arches at the Nagpur Depressed Classes conference of 18 July 1942 [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, p. 242](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=271).
- **1945.** Nare Park, Parel, was named "Sadhvi Ramabai Ambedkar Nagar" for the 3rd All India Scheduled Castes Federation conference, held on 5–6 May 1945 [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, p. 352](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=381).
- **1965.** The People's Education Society opened "Matoshri Ramabai Ambedkar High School" in Aurangabad [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part II, p. 545](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=570).
- **Pune statue.** A Commons photograph shows President Ram Nath Kovind unveiling a statue of "Matoshree Ramabai Bhimrao Ambedkar" in Pune. It was seen as an image title on the Wikipedia page; the date was not verified.
- **Images:**
  - [Portrait of Ramabai Ambedkar](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ramabai_Ambedkar_-_wife_of_Dr._Babasaheb_Ambedkar.jpg)
  - [Family photo at Rajagriha, February 1934](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rajagriha%2C_Bombay%2C_February_1934._%28L_to_R%29_Yashwant%2C_BR_Ambedkar%2C_Ramabai%2C_Laxmibai%2C_Mukundrao%2C_and_Tobby.jpg) — Yashwant, Dr. Ambedkar, Ramabai, Laxmibai (his brother's wife), Mukundrao (nephew) and the dog Tobby. Keer's 3rd edition has the same group photo with a matching caption [source: Keer, plates](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).

Notes on the children:

- **Pritchett's version.** "In the seven years between 1913 and 1920, Bhimrao had three children: two sons, Gangadhar and Ramesh, and one daughter, Indu", all dying "when a little over a year old" [source: Pritchett 1910s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html). **Conflict:** Keer's order is Ramesh, then Gangadhar; Pritchett lists Gangadhar first. Pritchett also places Indu before 1920, which does not conflict with Keer's "before Rajratna".
- **Rajratna's death.** Keer says his father would not let go of the body, and for days would not enter the room where the boy died [source: Keer, p. 66](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer) [source: Keer 1954](https://archive.org/details/dli.csl.6522).
- **Letter to Dattoba Powar, 16 August 1926.** Reproduced in Keer [source: Keer, pp. 66–67](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). Its key sentence, "We have in all buried four precious children, three sons and a daughter, all sprightly, auspicious and handsome children", is also summarised by Pritchett [source: Pritchett 1920s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1920s.html). The letter also says "My last boy was a wonderful boy the like of whom I have seldom seen" and "With his passing away life to me is a garden full of weeds", and signs off "Yours in grief". Dr. Ambedkar died in 1956, so his own words are out of copyright in India.
- **Total.** PARI agrees: five children, four of whom died in childhood [source: PARI](https://ruralindiaonline.org/en/articles/remembering-bhimrao-and-ramabai-ambedkar).

## 3. Rajgriha (Rajgruha / Rajgraha / Rajagriha), Hindu Colony, Dadar

### Building the house

- **1931.** He bought two plots in Bombay and built a home for his family on one of them [source: Pritchett 1930s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1930s.html).
- **Dates on Wikipedia (pointer only).** Construction of the plot 129 building ran from January 1931 to 1933; the plot 99 building was named "Char Minar"; the family moved in during 1933; Char Minar was sold on 9 May 1941 to buy books and clear loans; the house became a heritage structure in 2013 [source: Wikipedia – Rajgruha (pointer)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajgruha). These are **not independently verified**.
- **Why it was built.** Keer calls it a house built to hold a library, comparing him to Thomas Jefferson. He studied architecture books and had parts demolished and rebuilt; a colleague joked it would never get its roof [source: Keer, p. 244](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). The ground floor was used by the family, and Ramabai moved in "with strange emotions and embarrassment" [source: Keer, p. 245](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- **The name.** Named Rajgriha in 1934 "after the ancient city of the Buddhist kings" [source: Zelliot, p. 137](https://archive.org/details/sochara.fromuntouchablet0000zell).

### The address in the records

Letterheads show several spellings:

- "Rajgriha Colony, Dadar", 3 March 1934 [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 202](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=227).
- "'Rajgriha' Dadar", 8 June 1937 [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=280).
- "'RAJGRIHA' DADAR, HINDU COLONY", 14 July 1941 [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 328](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=353).
- "'RAJGRAHA'", 2 June 1947 [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=387).
- His 1952 election petition gives his residence as "Rajgriha, Hindu Colony, Dadar" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 409](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=434).

### Events at the house

- **18 June 1936.** He met Dr. B. S. Moonje of the Hindu Mahasabha at Rajgriha [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=264).
- **June 1946.** A Scheduled Castes Federation (SCF) meeting was held at Rajagriha in the first week of June 1946 [source: Keer 1954](https://archive.org/details/dli.csl.6522).
- **January 1947.** Told that his house was surrounded by hooligans, he went home anyway, saying his son, his nephew and his books, "as dear to him as life itself", were in danger [source: Keer, p. 389](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- **1948.** He wrote that he had given Yashwant about Rs. 30,000 and "a house which is today worth at least Rs. 80,000" [source: Keer, p. 404](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- **7 December 1956.** His body was flown to Bombay, taken to Rajagriha in a silent procession, and seen there by lakhs of people before cremation at Dadar [source: Keer, pp. 515–516](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- **7 July 2020.** Vandalism at the house (Wikipedia, pointer only); the Maharashtra government then ordered permanent police protection [source: Wikipedia – Rajgruha (pointer)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajgruha). **Not independently verified**: the Indian Express archive was blocked.

### The library and the book count

- **What Keer says.** He "had purchased thousands of books and had collected some rare books". Pandit Madan Mohan Malaviya once offered Rs. two lakhs for the library; Ambedkar refused, saying that if he lost his library he would lose his life. He was a poor lender of books, and once said he would kill a bailiff who came to seize his library [source: Keer, p. 474](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- **Reading habits.**
  - He would come back from court via bookstalls with a heap of new books and read late into the night [source: Keer, pp. 472–473](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
  - "What instructs me," he observed, "amuses me" [source: Keer, p. 473](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). This is Keer's report and is not in a primary source, so it is not used as a quote on the site.
  - Keer says he could remember the colour of a book's cover and the chapter where he had read something [source: Keer, p. 474](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
  - The 1954 police report records that he "had none of the bad habits but he was fond of reading" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, p. 505](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=534).
- **"50,000 books".** This figure is **not verified**.
  - Wikipedia cites V. Geetha in The Wire (29 Oct 2017) and an Indian Express archive story for "more than 50,000 books … one of the largest personal libraries in the world" [source: Wikipedia – Rajgruha (pointer)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajgruha).
  - The Wire article contains no such number. It says only that the Siddharth College collection "comprises only part of Ambedkar's great library", looked after by librarians S. S. Rege and Shrikant Talvatkar [source: V. Geetha, The Wire](https://thewire.in/caste/unpacking-library-babasaheb-ambedkar-world-books).
  - The Indian Express archive page could not be opened.
- **What the Siddharth College collection holds.** V. Geetha lists law, constitutions, Sacred Books of the East, Kabirpanthi and other sect literature, Buddhist and Jain texts, works by Dewey and William James, British labour history, Soviet publications (including a 1947 Lenin and Vyshinsky on Soviet law), Needham, Rostow and Merriam, and R. Palme Dutt's *India Today* with a personal inscription [source: The Wire](https://thewire.in/caste/unpacking-library-babasaheb-ambedkar-world-books).
- **Lucknow anecdote (second-hand).** Lalji Nirmal of the Ambedkar Mahasabha, citing Savita Ambedkar, says that on a Lucknow visit as a minister he declined to stay at Raj Bhavan and stayed in his railway saloon with his books [source: The Week (PTI), 15 April 2018](https://www.theweek.in/news/india/2018/04/15/how-colour-blue-got-associated-with-ambedkar.html).

## 5. Dr. Savita Ambedkar (Sharda/Sharada Kabir), 1909 – 29 May 2003

### Background

- **Birth (pointer only).** Born Sharada Krishnarao Kabir on 27 January 1909 (Wikipedia, pointer only) [source: Wikipedia (pointer)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savita_Ambedkar). News18 gives only the year 1909, in Ratnagiri district [source: News18, 27 Jan 2024](https://www.news18.com/lifestyle/savita-ambedkar-birth-anniversary-10-things-you-must-know-about-dr-br-ambedkars-second-wife-8755765.html).
- **Family.** Her family came from Dorla (Dorle) village, Rajapur taluka, Ratnagiri. Her father was registrar of the Indian Medical Council [source: Times of India (PTI), 29 May 2003](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/mumbai/b-r-ambedkars-widow-passes-away/articleshow/47838403.cms).
- **Education and work.** She went to school in Pune and took an MBBS from Grant Medical College in 1937. She worked briefly as chief medical officer at a hospital in Gujarat, then joined Dr. Malvankar in Mumbai, where she met Dr. Ambedkar [source: Times of India (PTI)](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/mumbai/b-r-ambedkars-widow-passes-away/articleshow/47838403.cms).
- **First contact.** She came into contact with him in 1947, when he had diabetes and blood pressure [source: Times of India (PTI)](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/mumbai/b-r-ambedkars-widow-passes-away/articleshow/47838403.cms). News18 says they exchanged 40–50 letters within a year [source: News18](https://www.news18.com/lifestyle/savita-ambedkar-birth-anniversary-10-things-you-must-know-about-dr-br-ambedkars-second-wife-8755765.html).

### The marriage

- **His decision.** He wrote to his friend Chitre in early 1948: "I have decided to marry Dr. Kabir. She is the best match I can find." He told Bhaurao Gaikwad that after his first wife's death he had resolved not to remarry. He now wanted an educated lady who knew cooking and was a medical practitioner, and chose a Saraswat Brahmin lady [source: Keer, p. 403](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- **Opposition.** He told Chitre that a colleague was sowing ill-feeling between Yashwant and his fiancée, and fixed the date "irrevocably" as 15 April 1948 [source: Keer, p. 403](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- **The wedding, 15 April 1948.** It took place in the morning at No. 1 Hardinge Avenue, New Delhi, under the Civil Marriages Act, before the Deputy Commissioner of Delhi. A few friends attended, followed by a luncheon. The New York Times called it more significant than a royal marrying a commoner [source: Keer, p. 404](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- **Her new name.** She took the name Savita [source: Pritchett 1940s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1940s.html), and followers called her "Mai" or "Maisaheb" [source: Times of India (PTI)](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/mumbai/b-r-ambedkars-widow-passes-away/articleshow/47838403.cms).
- **Name conflict.** The 1954 Keer calls her "Dr. Miss Lakshmi Kabir" [source: Keer 1954](https://archive.org/details/dli.csl.6522). The 3rd edition says "Dr. Miss Sharda Kabir" [source: Keer, p. 404](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).

### Together, 1948–1956

- **Routine.** She tried to enforce a regular routine on him "but she must have found the case almost impossible" [source: Keer 1954](https://archive.org/details/dli.csl.6522). He saw *Oliver Twist* with her [source: Keer, p. 471](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- **Public life.** She was cheered at the Nare Park meeting of 11 January 1950 [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, p. 397](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=426). She was garlanded with him at the 28 October 1954 meeting [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, p. 505](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=534).
- **14 October 1956.** She went with him to the conversion ceremony in a white sari [source: Keer, pp. 498–499](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). See also the [Commons photo of the diksha](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Dr_Babasaheb_Ambedkar_with_his_second_wife_Dr_Savita_Ambedkar%2C_holding_a_statue_of_the_Buddha%2C_during_the_Dhamma_Diksha_ceremony_in_Nagpur._October_14%2C_1956.jpg).
- **The unpublished preface (dated 6 April 1956).** It credits "the medical skill of my wife and Dr. Malvankar" for "the successful rekindling of this dying flame" [source: Pritchett](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/ambedkar_buddha/00_pref_unpub.html). Keer reproduces the passage and calls it "a mystery" that it did not appear in the published book [source: Keer, p. 520](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
  - **Date conflict.** The published preface is dated 15 March 1956 [source: Pritchett 1950s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1950s.html); the unpublished one is dated 6 April 1956.

### After his death

- **Disputes.** After his death, followers questioned the circumstances. Yashwant lodged a police complaint, and a Delhi police inquiry found a natural death (Pant to the Lok Sabha, 26 Nov 1957). His widow and his son went to court over the estate and reached a compromise [source: Keer, pp. 519–520](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). The publisher's blurb for her memoir says she was "hounded into obscurity" by some followers and returned to public life in 1970 [source: Penguin](https://www.penguin.co.in/book/babasaheb/). These matters are kept out of the public chapter, which is meant to stay respectful and non-political.
- **Memoir.** *Dr. Ambedkaranchya Sahavasat* (Marathi, 1990). English: *Babasaheb: My Life with Dr Ambedkar*, translated by Nadeem Khan. The Penguin listing shows a Vintage edition of December 2024; an earlier hardback edition may exist (**unverified**) [source: Penguin](https://www.penguin.co.in/book/babasaheb/).
- **Museum.** From 1982 she gave his belongings to the Symbiosis Dr. Babasaheb Ambedkar Museum and Memorial in Pune, and visited it every year until 2001 [source: News18](https://www.news18.com/lifestyle/savita-ambedkar-birth-anniversary-10-things-you-must-know-about-dr-br-ambedkars-second-wife-8755765.html).
- **Death.** She died at 5.30 a.m. on Thursday, 29 May 2003, at J. J. Hospital, Mumbai, aged 94, after about six weeks in hospital [source: Times of India (PTI), 29 May 2003](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/mumbai/b-r-ambedkars-widow-passes-away/articleshow/47838403.cms).

## 6. Habits and interests

Unless marked, everything below is from Keer's character chapter [source: Keer, pp. 469–475](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). The 1954 edition has the same text in the present tense [source: Keer 1954](https://archive.org/details/dli.csl.6522).

### Music, art and buildings

- He liked music, and held that everyone should love harmony in music and beauty in art.
- He was "taking lessons in playing the violin" in his last years [source: Keer, p. 470](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- Like Churchill he drew pictures. His servants had to coax him into drawing when he would not leave his books.
- He admired fine pictures and architecture, and complained that in India art was confined by caste.

### Dogs

- His pet dog fell ill; he visited it in hospital twice a day and grieved when it died. The dog's name is not given.
- The dog Peter came for his morning pat after night-long reading [source: Keer, p. 473](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- The 1934 family photo includes the dog Tobby [source: Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rajagriha%2C_Bombay%2C_February_1934._%28L_to_R%29_Yashwant%2C_BR_Ambedkar%2C_Ramabai%2C_Laxmibai%2C_Mukundrao%2C_and_Tobby.jpg).
- He "loved fine dogs" and would bring one from the far corners of the country if he saw a charming breed [source: Keer, p. 472](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).

### Clothes and possessions

- He had a vast library, rich clothes, enormous fountain pens, a grand car, many pairs of shoes and boots, and a rare collection of pictures.
- Keer reads these as "living marks" of a man who had once been thrown out of carts and hotels [source: Keer, pp. 470–471](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- **Second-hand.** S. R. Darapuri, citing Savita Ambedkar, says his clothes were made by the tailor of the Lieutenant Governor of the Bombay Presidency [source: The Week (PTI)](https://www.theweek.in/news/india/2018/04/15/how-colour-blue-got-associated-with-ambedkar.html).

### Food

- He did not smoke and was "a thorough teetotaller" [source: Keer, p. 471](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- He was "an expert cook" but never insisted on any particular dish, and sometimes ate in his library. When angry with his family he would go without speaking or eating.
- **Second-hand.** Darapuri, citing *Ambedkar ki Dincharya* by Ambedkar's secretary, says he enjoyed good food and cooked for guests [source: The Week (PTI)](https://www.theweek.in/news/india/2018/04/15/how-colour-blue-got-associated-with-ambedkar.html).

### Sport and leisure

- He played cricket as a boy. When Ramabai was at Dharwar he played with the boys of the Depressed Classes hostel there.
- In the early 1920s he played cards and bridge, and bathed in the sea daily [source: Keer, p. 471](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).

### Films

He rarely went to the cinema. He saw *Uncle Tom* with Ramabai, *Achhut Kanya* "with peculiar emotion", and *Oliver Twist* with Savita [source: Keer, p. 471](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).

### Gardening

As a boy in Satara he was so taken with gardening that he spent every pie he could get on new plants. He then switched to tending cattle and goats [source: Keer, p. 15](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). We found no reliable source on gardening in his adult life, so it is not claimed on the site.

### Temper and humour

- Moving his books made him furious, but he cooled down quickly.
- He told earthy country jokes, wearing a lungi and shirt at home.
- He wept at sad films, at his son's death and at a colleague's cremation [source: Keer, pp. 469–470](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).

### Daily routine (before 1942)

He slept from dawn to morning after reading all night. He went to court by car with a new book, came home through the bookstalls, ate dinner "in a nook with a book", and ended the night with a glass of milk [source: Keer 1954](https://archive.org/details/dli.csl.6522).

- **Ashok Ambedkar.** Mukund's son, who took part in the BSI case [source: Indian Express 2013](https://indianexpress.com/article/india/india-others/battle-to-head-ambedkars-society-nears-end-in-hc-2/).

## 8. Inspirations

### The 28 October 1954 speech

The primary text is BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, item 151, pp. 504–505 [source: BAWS](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=533). It is a **police report** ("Police Report: Source Material, Vol. 1, Pp. 421–422"), so it is reported speech, not a verbatim transcript.

**The occasion**

- A meeting of about 25,000 people at Purandare's Stadium, Naigaum, Bombay, at 6.30 p.m., with R. D. Bhandare presiding.
- It was organised by the Dr. Ambedkar Diamond Jubilee Celebration Committee. He had turned 60 on 14 April 1952, but the celebration was held late.
- He was given a purse of Rs. 1,18,000. He said it would be used to build a hall on land already bought.

**What the report says he said**

- He was an ordinary boy, not a brilliant student.
- His exact birth date was not recorded.
- The book on Gautam Buddha "had great impression on him and since that time he became the follower of Buddha".
- "He further said that he was a devotee of Guatam Buddha, Kabir and Mahatma Phule and worshipper of learning, self-respect and character. He had none of the bad habits but he was fond of reading."
- Learning was essential to progress.
- People should give part of their earnings to the community and work for the Federation, and should not quarrel over leadership.
- He would not be able to guide them for more than 7 or 8 more years.

**Other versions**

- **Marathi (via The Mooknayak).** "I have three gurus"; the Buddha first and greatest; Kabir second, linked to his father being a Kabirpanthi; Phule the teacher of humanity [source: The Mooknayak (Geetha Sunil Pillai, 9 April 2025)](https://en.themooknayak.com/bahujan-nayak/mahatma-phule-jayanti-2025-the-third-guru-who-shaped-dr-ambedkars-fight-against-caste-oppression). The Marathi original was not seen.
- **Round Table India.** Gives the "devotee … worshiper of learning, self-respect and character" line from his "Diamond Jubilee celebrations" without a date [source: Round Table India (Dr. SPVA Sairam, 2023)](https://www.roundtableindia.co.in/?p=29932). This is the same event as the BAWS report.
- **"Three deities".** This wording is not found in any English source we checked. It is probably a rendering of the Marathi *daivat* ("deity"). **Unverified.**

### The radio talk, 3 October 1954

"My Philosophy of Life", All India Radio [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, p. 503](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=532). Key lines, verified:

- "Positively, my Social Philosophy, may be said to be enshrined in three words : Liberty, Equality and Fraternity."
- "Let no one, however, say that I have borrowed my philosophy from the French-Revolution. I have not."
- "My philosophy has roots in religion and not in political science. I have derived them from the teachings of my Master, the Buddha."

The talk also rejects the Bhagavad Gita's social philosophy based on the *triguna*.

### What scholars and biographers say

- **Keer.** "Three personalities influenced the life and actions of this great man … the life of the Buddha, the teachings of Kabir and the struggle of Phooley" [source: Keer, p. 475](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- **Zelliot.** "Ambedkar himself claimed that he had three gurus: The Buddha, the fifteenth century iconoclastic saint-poet Kabir, and the nineteenth century radical social reformer, Mahatma Jotiba Phule" [source: Zelliot, p. 225](https://archive.org/details/sochara.fromuntouchablet0000zell). Also: Phule's "picture is often found in Mahar institutions" [source: Zelliot, p. 122 (note)](https://archive.org/details/sochara.fromuntouchablet0000zell).

### Kabir and the family

- His father's family "belonged to the devotional Kabir school of thought", which set aside caste rigidity [source: Keer, pp. 7–9](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- His mother Bhimabai's Murbadkar family "also belonged to the Kabir cult" [source: Keer, p. 10](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- Siddharth College's collection includes Kabirpanthi literature [source: The Wire](https://thewire.in/caste/unpacking-library-babasaheb-ambedkar-world-books).

### Phule

- Jotiba Phule (1827–1890) began the Satya Shodhak Samaj in 1873 and was the first Hindu to run schools for "untouchables" [source: Zelliot, p. 122](https://archive.org/details/sochara.fromuntouchablet0000zell).
- *Who Were the Shudras?* was published in October 1946 [source: Pritchett 1940s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1940s.html). Keer notes the dedication [source: Keer, pp. 386–387](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).

### John Dewey and Columbia

- **Studies.** He attended Columbia from 1913 to 1916. A 1920s CV lists Dewey among his professors [source: Pritchett 1910s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).
- ***Annihilation of Caste* (1936).** "Prof. John Dewey, who was my teacher and to whom I owe so much, has said: 'Every society gets encumbered with what is trivial …'" [source: BAWS Vol. 1, p. 79](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=94).
- **Dewey elsewhere in his writings.** Dewey is cited in BAWS Vol. 3 (pp. 306, 451), Vol. 4 (pp. 281–282) and Vol. 5 (p. 414), and *Democracy and Education* (1916) appears in his bibliographies.
- **Columbia alumni magazine, 1930.** "The best friends I have had in my life were some of my classmates at Columbia and my great professors, John Dewey, James Shotwell, Edwin Seligman and James Harvey Robinson" [source: Zelliot, p. 84](https://archive.org/details/sochara.fromuntouchablet0000zell).
- **Savita's memory.** She remembered him "happily imitating John Dewey's distinctive classroom mannerisms" thirty years later [source: Zelliot, p. 84](https://archive.org/details/sochara.fromuntouchablet0000zell).
- **Honorary degree, 1952.** He flew to New York on 1 June 1952, and on 5 June received an honorary LL.D. at Columbia's 198th Commencement. The citation called him "a framer of the Constitution, Member of the Cabinet and of the Council of States, one of India's leading citizens, a great social reformer and a valiant upholder of human rights". Lester B. Pearson was also honoured [source: Keer, p. 443](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer) [source: Pritchett 1950s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1950s.html).
- **Not used.** Claims online that he said "I owe all my intellectual life to John Dewey" were **not verified** and are not used.

### Patrons and helpers

#### K. A. "Dada" Keluskar (Krishnaji Arjun Keluskar)

- An assistant teacher at Wilson High School and a Marathi author. He met Bhimrao reading in a garden, lent him books and gave him his *Life of Gautama Buddha* [source: Keer, p. 20](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- Ambedkar's own words: "a copy of his book on the life of the Buddha … which he had written for the Baroda Sayajirao Oriental Series" [source: unpublished preface](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/ambedkar_buddha/00_pref_unpub.html).
- **Which exam, and when.**
  - Pritchett: 1907, at the matriculation celebration, which Keluskar presided over [source: Pritchett 1900s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1900s.html).
  - Keer: S. K. Bole presided.
  - Zelliot: 1908 [source: Zelliot, p. 137](https://archive.org/details/sochara.fromuntouchablet0000zell).
  - Ambedkar himself: "The year I passed the English Fourth Standard Examination".
- **Later help from Keluskar.** He took Bhimrao to Sayajirao for a college scholarship [source: Keer, p. 21](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). He tried to get him a Baroda post in 1917 [source: Keer 1954](https://archive.org/details/dli.csl.6522). He also appealed to the Maharaja of Baroda for money for a Depressed Classes college [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part II, p. 444](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=469).
- **Help in return.** In 1933 Ambedkar asked the Baroda Diwan to grant the aged Keluskar a monthly pension [source: Keer, p. 240](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).

#### Sayajirao Gaekwad III of Baroda

- Rs. 25 a month for college, granted after an interview [source: Keer, p. 21](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- A Columbia scholarship of £11.50 a month for three years (1913) [source: Pritchett 1910s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).
- On 4 June 1913 he signed an agreement to serve Baroda State for ten years after his studies [source: Keer, p. 26](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- In 1917 he was appointed Military Secretary to the Gaekwad [source: Pritchett 1910s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).

#### Shahu Maharaj of Kolhapur

- **Mook Nayak.** Contact came in 1919 through Dattoba Powar. The fortnightly *Mook Nayak* ("Leader of the Dumb/Voiceless") started on 31 January 1920 with help from the Maharaja [source: Keer, p. 41](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer) [source: Pritchett 1920s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1920s.html). Pritchett calls it a weekly. **Minor conflict:** weekly or fortnightly.
- **Mangaon, 21 March 1920.** Shahu said: "You have found your saviour in Ambedkar. I am confident that he will break your shackles …", predicting all-India fame. Keer's source is Shivtarkar in *Janata*, April 1933. The conference ended with an inter-caste dinner [source: Keer, p. 43](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). Pritchett: Shahu dined with Ambedkar and his caste members [source: Pritchett 1920s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1920s.html).
- **Nagpur, May 1920.** Shahu presided over the first All-India conference of "untouchables" [source: Keer, p. 43](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- **Return to London, July 1920.** Partly paid for by loans from the Maharaja of Kolhapur and Rs. 5,000 from Naval Bhathena [source: Keer, p. 44](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer) [source: Pritchett 1920s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1920s.html).

#### Other helpers

Professor Muller of Elphinstone College lent him books and clothes [source: Keer, p. 22](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).

### People and movements he inspired

- **Babu Hardas L. N.** "Admirer and devoted follower of Dr. Ambedkar from Kamptee (Vidarbha), popularly known as Babu Hardas". He was secretary of the All India Depressed Classes Congress, Nagpur, in 1930 [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, p. 15](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=44). He chaired the reception committee at Kamptee in 1932 [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, pp. 77–79](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=106).
- **Samata Sainik Dal.** It still organises the annual Hardas Mela at Kamptee, held the day after Makar Sankranti [source: The Wire, 21 Jan 2026](https://thewire.in/caste/the-collective-memory-of-resistance-exploring-babu-hardass-influence).
- **Buddhist Society of India.** Registered on 4 July 1955 [source: Indian Express 2013](https://indianexpress.com/article/india/india-others/battle-to-head-ambedkars-society-nears-end-in-hc-2/).
- **Republican Party of India.**
  - Its blueprint was published in Marathi in *Prabuddha Bharat* in 1957.
  - The SCF executive met on 30 September 1956 at his Delhi residence and resolved to dissolve the SCF and set up the "Republican Party" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part II, pp. 151–152](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=174).
  - At a press conference in Nagpur on 13 October 1956 he said he had drafted its constitution.
  - Its guiding principles were liberty, equality and fraternity [source: Keer, p. 498](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
  - Formally organised in October 1957 [source: Zelliot, p. 115](https://archive.org/details/sochara.fromuntouchablet0000zell).
- **Buddhist conversion movement.** The 1961 census counted 3,250,227 Buddhists, against 180,823 in 1951 [source: Zelliot, p. 187](https://archive.org/details/sochara.fromuntouchablet0000zell).
- **Dalit Panthers.** Formed in 1972 by young educated Buddhists. They brought "Dalit Sahitya" (Dalit literature) to public attention [source: Zelliot, p. 180](https://archive.org/details/sochara.fromuntouchablet0000zell) [source: Zelliot, p. xx](https://archive.org/details/sochara.fromuntouchablet0000zell).
- **Bahujan Samaj Party.** Kanshi Ram formed it, with Mayawati as his close aide [source: Zelliot, p. xi](https://archive.org/details/sochara.fromuntouchablet0000zell). We did not find a source for its founding date, which is usually given as 14 April 1984 (**unverified here**). The BSP adopted blue as its colour [source: The Week (PTI)](https://www.theweek.in/news/india/2018/04/15/how-colour-blue-got-associated-with-ambedkar.html).
- **Popular art.** Calendars show him with the Buddha, Phule, Shahu, Kabir, Gadge Maharaj, Birsa Munda, Annabhau Sathe, Periyar, Ravidas and Guru Nanak. They count years in both "Buddhabda" and "Bhimabda" [source: Columbia University Libraries](https://exhibitions.library.columbia.edu/exhibits/show/ambedkar/art/calendars).
- **Not covered.** International influence, such as Hungary's Roma "Jai Bhim" schools, was not researched because the search budget ran out.

## 9. Flags, colours and symbols

### 9.1 What is documented about blue (primary records)

1. **Samata Sainik Dal constitution.** The text is BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, Appendix II [source: BAWS, p. 566](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=595).
   - **Origin.** A conference at Kanpur on 30 January 1944 (president B. K. Gaikwad) set up a drafting committee: B. K. "Dadasaheb" Gaikwad, M. M. Sasalekar, S. B. Jadhao, R. R. Patil, K. N. Walmiki, A. L. Kosare and P. L. Lalingkar (*Janata*, 6 January 1945).
   - **Approval.** The draft was sent to Dr. Ambedkar on 8 December 1944, and he acknowledged it (Khairmode, Vol. 9, pp. 405–407).
   - **Flag clause.** Section XI [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, p. 572](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=601), verbatim:

     > "The flag of the All India Samata Sainik Dal will be four feet in length and 2½ feet in width. The whole ground of the flag will be deep blue and in the top left corner there shall be 11 pointed stars in white colour. Also there shall be a sun in the middle of the flag in white colour. The letter S. C. F, shall be boldly written under the sun in blue colour. In the Bottom right corner of the flag there shall be letters S. S. D. in white colour. The meaning of the FLAG is liberty, equality & fraternity and to fight to achieve our goal."

   - **Ambiguity.** "11 pointed stars" could mean eleven stars, or stars with eleven points. The site reads it as eleven stars.
2. **Scheduled Castes Federation constitution.**
   - The All India Depressed Class Conference met at Nagpur on 17–20 July 1942. It resolved on 19 July 1942 to set up the All India Scheduled Castes Federation. The constitution was made public on 7 September 1942, then "amended and finally published in January 1955" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part II, p. 455](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=480).
   - **Article XXVI(1):** "The flag of the Federation shall consist of stars against a blue cloth cut in triangle." [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part II, p. 474](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=499)
   - **Open question.** We could not check whether the 1942 text had the same clause. Only the 1955 text is reprinted.
3. **Reports of flags in use.**
   - **17 Feb 1946, Nare Park, Bombay.** Dr. Ambedkar unfurled "the Scheduled Castes flag" and received a guard of honour of Scheduled Caste volunteers [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, p. 363](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=392).
   - **14 Apr 1947, Delhi.** For his 55th birthday, "Federation Flags were hoisted in all important localities" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, p. 369](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=398).
   - **11 Jan 1950, Nare Park, Parel.** "Large processions of Harijans, carrying blue party flags" (*Janata*, 14 Jan 1950) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, p. 397](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=426).
   - **22 Nov 1951, Bhoiwada.** The chair, R. G. Kharat, said "they had build up a strong unity under the Blue Flag and now even the other people were gathering round the Blue Flag" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, p. 450](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=479).
   - **25 Nov 1951, Shivaji Park.** "The blue star-studded flags of the Federation and the Red flags of the Socialist Party were very much in evidence. Hundreds of volunteers, in blue and red caps of the two parties …" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, p. 456](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=485).
4. **Earliest Samata Sainik Dal record.** Volunteers looked after the arrangements at a meeting on 28 October 1932 [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, p. 85](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=114). A general meeting of the Dal was held on 8 November 1936 [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, p. 151](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=180). At Nagpur on 18 July 1942, 5,000 uniformed Sainiks formed a guard of honour [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, p. 242](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=271). Its founding year of 1927 comes from Wikipedia only [source: Wikipedia (pointer)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samata_Sainik_Dal).
5. **Independent Labour Party (August 1936).** Founding date from [Pritchett 1930s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1930s.html).
   - **No flag description was found** in BAWS Vol. 17 or in Keer.
   - SabrangIndia (14 April 2018) reports that a 2017 paper on Pa. Ranjith's films, "Fabric-Rendered Identity", says (citing Jaffrelot's *Dr. Ambedkar and Untouchability*) that Ambedkar introduced the blue "Mahar's flag" as the ILP flag [source: SabrangIndia](https://sabrangindia.in/significance-colour-blue-dalit-movement/). This is **unverified**: Jaffrelot's text was not seen.
   - Keer mentions "red flags" at a 1938 labour tour of mill areas with communist leaders, but that refers to the joint strike campaign, not the ILP flag [source: Keer](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).

### 9.2 The Republican Party of India flag

- **Darapuri's account.** S. R. Darapuri (retired IPS officer and activist, via PTI) says the SCF flag of 1942 "was blue in colour and had an Ashok Chakra in the centre", and that the RPI (dated 1956 in this source) "was also given the same blue flag" [source: The Week (PTI), 15 April 2018](https://www.theweek.in/news/india/2018/04/15/how-colour-blue-got-associated-with-ambedkar.html) [source: WION, 15 April 2018](https://www.wionews.com/india-news/why-the-colour-blue-is-associated-with-dr-br-ambedkar-130524).
  - **Conflict.** The SCF constitution and the 1951 reports describe stars, not a chakra. Possibly the chakra flag replaced the star flag at some point, perhaps in 1956–57 with the RPI. **Unresolved.**
- **Today's flag.** A plain blue field with a white Ashoka Chakra. The Commons SVG "Flag of various Republican Parties of India" draws it in #000066 with a white wheel [source: Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Flag_of_various_Republican_Parties_of_India.svg).
- **Wider use.** Wikipedia's Buddhist flag page shows a blue Dharma-wheel flag "used by the Dalit Buddhist movement" [source: Wikipedia – Buddhist flag (pointer)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_flag). Commons has many photos titled "Indian Buddhist flag" showing this blue flag [source: Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Buddhist_flag_of_Indian_Buddhists.jpg).

### 9.3 Popular explanations for blue (not documented as his own words)

- **Raosaheb Kasbe** (former political science professor, Savitribai Phule Pune University): blue is the colour of the sky and stands for non-discrimination. He also says there is "no settled history" of why blue became the colour of Dalit resistance [source: SabrangIndia](https://sabrangindia.in/significance-colour-blue-dalit-movement/).
- **Kancha Ilaiah:** said he had read, but could not recall where, that Ambedkar spoke of the blue sky being over everyone; he also suggests an American influence (presidents' blue suits) [source: SabrangIndia](https://sabrangindia.in/significance-colour-blue-dalit-movement/).
- **Beena Pallical (NCDHR):** the blue suit was a main inspiration for the blue flag [source: SabrangIndia](https://sabrangindia.in/significance-colour-blue-dalit-movement/).
- **Lalji Nirmal (Ambedkar Mahasabha):** "Blue was his favourite colour" [source: The Week (PTI)](https://www.theweek.in/news/india/2018/04/15/how-colour-blue-got-associated-with-ambedkar.html).
- **S. R. Darapuri:** blue, "in another shade", is the sky, showing vastness [source: The Week (PTI)](https://www.theweek.in/news/india/2018/04/15/how-colour-blue-got-associated-with-ambedkar.html).
- **Blue-collar workers; Mahar identity.** The 2017 paper cited by Sabrang links blue to blue-collar workers and to Mahar self-identification [source: SabrangIndia](https://sabrangindia.in/significance-colour-blue-dalit-movement/).
- **Ramachandra Guha (2002, via Sabrang):** a man of his background was not supposed to wear a suit, blue or otherwise. Paraphrased here, because the quote is over 15 words [source: SabrangIndia](https://sabrangindia.in/significance-colour-blue-dalit-movement/).
- **Badaun statue (2018).** A newly installed 5-foot statue in a saffron coat at Kuwargaav, Badaun district (UP) was repainted blue after objections. Local BSP leaders started the repainting [source: The Week (PTI)](https://www.theweek.in/news/india/2018/04/15/how-colour-blue-got-associated-with-ambedkar.html) [source: SabrangIndia](https://sabrangindia.in/significance-colour-blue-dalit-movement/). Not used on the site, to keep the page non-political.

### 9.4 Ashoka Chakra / Dhamma Chakra and the national flag

- **The Flag Committee.** On 23 June 1947 the Constituent Assembly set up a "nine-member ad hoc 'Flag Committee'" under Rajendra Prasad, so that the flag would not be identified with the Congress alone [source: S. Chakrabarti, ICV27 London 2017 (FIAV)](https://fiav.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ICV27-A8-Chakrabarti.pdf).
- **Members.** Wikipedia names Azad, Sarojini Naidu, Rajagopalachari, K. M. Munshi and Ambedkar, citing the Flag Code [source: Wikipedia – Flag of India (pointer)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_India). Keer confirms Ambedkar "was a Member of the Flag Committee" [source: Keer, p. 394](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). The full list of nine was not verified.
- **The decision.** The committee kept the INC flag, "simply replacing the charkha with Asoka's Dharma Chakra". The sample flag made by Suraiya Badr-ud-Din Tyabji was adopted on 22 July 1947. Gandhi was unhappy about losing the charkha [source: Chakrabarti, FIAV](https://fiav.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ICV27-A8-Chakrabarti.pdf). Wikipedia says the committee recommended on 14 July 1947 (pointer only).
- **Keer on Ambedkar's part** [source: Keer, pp. 394–395](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer):
  - Before the vote, Maratha and Hindu Sabha leaders in Bombay asked him to back a saffron (*geruva*) flag. He promised to "put in a word" if there was public pressure, and joked about whether they expected "the son of a Mahar" to unfurl it.
  - Then: "It is said that Ambedkar put in a word, but as there was no agitation from outside for the adoption of the Geruva flag he threw his weight in favour of the Ashoka Chakra."
  - Keer adds that Savarkar too had appealed to Rajendra Prasad to replace the Charkha with the Chakra.
  - **Summary:** Ambedkar's role is reported as hearsay ("It is said").
- **2 May 1950, Delhi (reported).** He said "the President of the Republic had to approach Buddhism for the symbol over free India's national flag Ashoka Chakra, finding nothing from a search into Brahminism" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, p. 402](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=431).
- **Prakash Ambedkar's claim (not opened).** A search snippet of a Deccan Herald article quotes him saying his grandfather "played a very big role" in the choice of the Lion Capital and the Ashoka Chakra in navy blue. **Not opened, not used.**
- **Zelliot on the chakra.** By 1947, knowledge of the Buddhist period meant "the chakra (the wheel of dharma) on the national flag and the lion capital … were acceptable to almost all Indians" [source: Zelliot, p. 190](https://archive.org/details/sochara.fromuntouchablet0000zell). Also pp. 187 and 208.
- **The wheel itself.** Navy blue with 24 equally spaced spokes, from the abacus of the Sarnath Lion Capital of Ashoka. Standards: IS 1 (1951), revised in 1964 and as IS 1:1968 [source: BIS blog (J. K. Gupta, 20 Jan 2020)](https://www.services.bis.gov.in/php/BIS_2.0/BISBlog/standardizing-the-tricolour-that-symbolizes-the-worlds-largest-democracy/).
- **Meaning.** In Buddhism the first sermon is the *Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta*, "setting in motion the wheel of Dhamma". Dr. Ambedkar refers to the "Dharma Chakra Pravartana Sutta" as the Buddha's first sermon [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, p. 552](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=581). Wikipedia attributes the official explanation of the chakra as the wheel of dharma and of motion to S. Radhakrishnan, via the Flag Code (pointer only; the MHA Flag Code PDF was blocked).

### 9.5 The Panchsheel / international Buddhist flag

- **Design.** Designed in 1885 by the Colombo Committee. Members named in the sources: Ven. Hikkaduwe Sri Sumangala (chair), Ven. Migettuwatte Gunananda, Don Carolis Hewavitharana, Andiris Perera Dharmagunawardhana, Charles A. de Silva, Peter De Abrew, William De Abrew, H. William Fernando, N. S. Fernando and Carolis Pujitha Gunawardena (secretary) [source: Wikipedia – Buddhist flag (pointer)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_flag) [source: Tricycle, 28 May 2010](https://tricycle.org/article/international-buddhist-flag/).
- **First flown.** On Vesak day, 28 May 1885, at Dipaduttamarama, Kotahena (Wikipedia). Tricycle says the modified flag was first hoisted on Vesak 1886.
- **Shape.** Olcott suggested national-flag proportions.
- **Adoption.** Adopted by the World Fellowship of Buddhists in 1950 [source: Tricycle](https://tricycle.org/article/international-buddhist-flag/). A search snippet says G. P. Malalasekera proposed it at Kandy on 25 May 1950 (**not opened, unverified**).
- **Ambedkar at the 1950 conference.** He attended the WFB conference in Ceylon (Kandy and Colombo), 25 May–6 June 1950 [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, p. 404](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=433). Keer says he flew to Colombo on 25 May 1950 with his wife and his party secretary P. N. Rajbhoj. At Kandy he "declined to address the Conference in its representative capacity", disapproved of some of its resolutions, and urged it to commit to spreading Buddhism [source: Keer, pp. 421–422](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). He also attended the 4th WFB conference at Kathmandu in November 1956 [source: Pritchett 1950s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1950s.html).
- **Colours and meanings** [source: Tricycle](https://tricycle.org/article/international-buddhist-flag/) [source: Wikipedia (pointer)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_flag):

  | Colour | Pali name | Meaning |
  |---|---|---|
  | Blue | *nīla* | universal compassion, or loving-kindness and peace |
  | Yellow | *pīta* | the Middle Way |
  | Red | *lohita* | the blessings of practice |
  | White | *odāta* | purity of the Dhamma |
  | Orange | *mañjeṭṭha* | wisdom |
  | Sixth band (all five combined) | *pabhassara* | "essence of light" |

- **At Nagpur, 14 October 1956.** People came "with Buddhist flags in the hands" (*Prabuddha Bharat* special number, 27 October 1956) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, p. 545](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=574). Keer: "Buddhist flags consisting of blue, red and green stripes fluttered everywhere". The 14-acre enclosure near the Vaccine Institute had a replica of the Sanchi stupa on the dais [source: Keer, p. 498](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- **The name "Panchsheel flag".** This usage in India is attested by Commons file titles such as "Buddhist Panchsheel flag and Bodhi tree, Nagpur" [source: Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Buddhist_Panchsheel_flag_and_Bodhi_tree,_Nagpur.jpg). No scholarly source for the name was found.

### 9.6 "Jai Bhim"

- **Zelliot.** "The Indian nationalist cry, Jai Hind, has been transformed into Jai Bhim … with Jai Buddh added at religious occasions". Invitations say "Saprem Jai Bhim", and "Bhim Raj" is sometimes used for "Ram Raj" [source: Zelliot, p. 144](https://archive.org/details/sochara.fromuntouchablet0000zell).
- **Keer.** Ambedkar responded to the new greeting "by repeating 'Jai Bhim' to their salutation at the end of his letters" [source: Keer, p. 469](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- **BBC News Hindi** (Tushar Kulkarni, BBC Marathi, 17 Nov 2021) [source: BBC](https://www.bbc.com/hindi/india-59297949):
  - The slogan was first given by Babu Hardas L. N. (Laxman Nagrale) in 1935, according to Ramchandra Kshirsagar's *Dalit Movement in India and Its Leaders*.
  - Hardas was secretary of the Samata Sainik Dal.
  - **J. V. Pawar** (Dalit Panther co-founder): Hardas asked volunteers to use "Jai Bhim" in place of "Namaskar", "Ram Ram" or "Johar Maybap", with "Bal Bhim" as the reply. In 1938, at Makranpur (Kannad taluka, Aurangabad district), a meeting organised by Bhausaheb More with Ambedkar present took up the slogan.
  - **Retired judge Suresh Ghorpade:**
    - Hardas was born in 1904 and joined social work in 1920.
    - He founded the Sant Chokhamela hostel at Kamptee in 1924 and a bidi workers' union in 1925.
    - He chaired the reception committee at the Kamptee conference of 1932.
    - He died of tuberculosis in 1939, and Ambedkar is said to have remarked "my right hand has gone".
  - **Others quoted.** Vasant Moon (from *Basti*, translated into English as *Growing Up Untouchable in India*) and Narendra Jadhav are also quoted.
- **The Wire** (21 Jan 2026) [source: The Wire](https://thewire.in/caste/the-collective-memory-of-resistance-exploring-babu-hardass-influence):
  - The eponym "emerged in the speech that Babu Hardas delivered, at the Akhila Bharatiya Dalit Sammelan in Kamptee, 1932".
  - In 1938 at Makranpur he used it to address the Samata Sainik Dal.
  - Hardas died on 12 January 1939 and was cremated on the Kanhan river bank. The Hardas Mela is held each year by the Samata Sainik Dal.
- **Wikipedia (pointer only).** Gives 1935, citing Kshirsagar. It also mentions:
  - a claim that the origin is "obscure" and may date to 1818 (Times of India, 18 April 2016, which was blocked);
  - the poet Bihari Lal Harit proclaiming it in a 1946 poem.

  Neither was verified [source: Wikipedia – Jai Bhim (pointer)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jai_Bhim).
- **A newspaper of the same name.** *Jai Bhim* was a paper edited by N. Sivaraj in Madras. BAWS reprints a report from its issue of 13 August 1946 [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, p. 578](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=607) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part II, p. 240](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=263).
- **Unverified.** P. T. Ramteke's 2000 paper (the "Salam Alekum" inspiration story) was seen only in a search snippet from drambedkarbooks.com, which could not be opened.

### 9.7 Statues and the blue suit

- **Parliament House statue.** "Pressure from the Republican Party has resulted in a life-size statue placed at the Northwest corner of Parliament House compound. A copy of the Constitution is in one of Ambedkar's hands; the forefinger of the other points at Parliament." [source: Zelliot, p. 76](https://archive.org/details/sochara.fromuntouchablet0000zell). Note: statues in the Parliament complex were reportedly moved to a new "Prerna Sthal" in 2024. That is **not verified** here, so the site uses the past tense.
- **Photographs.** They "more often than not show him in a blue pinstripe business suit surrounded by books" [source: Zelliot, p. 145](https://archive.org/details/sochara.fromuntouchablet0000zell). Also: "usually clad in a blue business suit, a book in his hand, a fountain pen in his pocket", placed beside the yellow-robed Buddha [source: Zelliot, p. 225](https://archive.org/details/sochara.fromuntouchablet0000zell).
- **The pose.** Statues show "a blue coat with the Constitution in one hand", the other finger raised "as a symbol of moving ahead" (Darapuri) [source: WION](https://www.wionews.com/india-news/why-the-colour-blue-is-associated-with-dr-br-ambedkar-130524). Sabrang mentions many towns with statues in a "three-piece blue suit" holding the Constitution [source: SabrangIndia](https://sabrangindia.in/significance-colour-blue-dalit-movement/).
- **Dress and caste.** Chandra Bhan Prasad (via Sabrang), citing Manu, says Dalits were told to wear only the "apparel of corpses" or rags. The suit is therefore read as resistance [source: SabrangIndia](https://sabrangindia.in/significance-colour-blue-dalit-movement/).
- **Large statues (Commons only).**
  - Statue of Social Justice, Vijayawada [source: Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Statue_of_Social_Justice,_Vijayawada.jpg). Its height and date were **not verified**: the search budget ran out.
  - Hyderabad's 125-ft statue (2023) was not researched.

### 9.8 Deekshabhoomi stupa

- **Architect.** Sheo Dan Mal (Mokha), born 5 August 1923 at Phalodi, Jodhpur district. He moved to Nagpur in 1956 and died on 24 January 2007, aged 84 [source: OneIndia (UNI), 25 Jan 2007](https://www.oneindia.com/2007/01/24/dr-ambedkar-memorial-architect-sheo-dan-mal-passes-away-1169695243.html). His first Nagpur building was the Panchasheel cinema. Wikipedia also lists a co-architect, Shashi Sharma (pointer only).
- **Construction.** Began in July 1978 (Wikipedia, pointer only; its cited BSNL page is a dead link).
- **Inauguration.** On Tuesday 18 December 2001, by President K. R. Narayanan [source: Times of India (Ramu Bhagwat), 19 Dec 2001](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/mumbai/Ambedkar-memorial-set-up-at-Deekshabhoomi/articleshow/189472379.cms) [source: India Post stamp text](https://istampgallery.com/deekshabhoomi/).
- **India Post stamp text** [source: istampgallery](https://istampgallery.com/deekshabhoomi/):
  - **Stamps.** Two se-tenant stamps of Rs. 5 each, issued on 14 April 2017. 3.10 lakh of each were printed, by wet offset at the Security Printing Press, Hyderabad.
  - **Size.** Dome diameter 36 m; height 22 m (30 m from ground); hall 64 × 64 m; verandah 4 m.
  - **Site.** 22 acres.
  - **Claims.** Calls it the "largest hollow stupa among all Buddhist stupas in India" and says "nearly 4 lakhs" converted.

## 11. Conflicts summary (quick list)

1. **Marriage year:** 1906 (Pritchett, PARI) or after the 1907 matriculation (Keer).
2. **His age at marriage:** 14 (PARI) or "hardly seventeen" (Keer).
3. **Ramabai's father:** Bhiku Walangkar (Keer) or Bhiku Dhutre (Pritchett).
4. **Order of Ramesh and Gangadhar:** differs between Keer and Pritchett.
5. **Ramabai's death:** 27 May 1935 (Keer); 26 May (some web articles, unverified).
6. ***Thoughts on Pakistan*:** December 1940 (BAWS, Pritchett) or 1941 (date on the printed title page).
7. **Second wife's name in Keer:** "Lakshmi Kabir" (1954 edition) or "Sharda Kabir" (later edition).
8. **Number of books:** "thousands" (Keer) or 50,000 (popular, unsourced).
9. **Keluskar's gift:** 1907 (Pritchett) or 1908 (Zelliot); the 4th Standard exam (Ambedkar's own account) or the matriculation (Keer, Pritchett).
10. **SCF flag:** stars on blue (constitution, 1951 report) or blue with Ashoka Chakra (Darapuri).
11. **Origin of "Jai Bhim":** 1932 (The Wire), 1935 (BBC/Kshirsagar) or 1938 Makranpur (Pawar); outlier claims of 1818 and 1946.
12. **Flag Committee:** nine members (Chakrabarti) but only six names commonly listed.
13. **Buddhist flag first flown:** 1885 (Wikipedia) or 1886 for the modified flag (Tricycle).
14. **Flags at Nagpur in 1956:** "blue, red and green stripes" (Keer), which does not match the five-colour Buddhist flag.
15. **Deekshabhoomi:** "largest hollow stupa" in India (India Post) or in the world (Tribune, government portals).
16. **The two 1956 prefaces:** the published one dated 15 March 1956, the unpublished one 6 April 1956.
