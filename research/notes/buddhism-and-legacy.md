# Research dossier: Buddhism, final days and legacy (1935–today)

Covers chapters `10-buddhism.md`, `11-final-days.md` and `12-legacy.md`. Everything here was read in the
source linked. Page numbers for BAWS are given as **printed page (PDF page)**; links use the working MEA
path `https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/<file>.pdf#page=<PDF page>` (lower-case `images`; the
`Images/attach/amb/...` links are dead). PDF page numbers were cross-checked against
`library/baws-en/<volume-id>.jsonl`.

Keer page numbers refer to the 3rd edition of Dhananjay Keer, *Dr. Ambedkar: Life and Mission* (Popular
Prakashan, Bombay; first published May 1954), as scanned on the Internet Archive
[source: Internet Archive – Keer](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
The OCR text was read in full for the relevant chapters ("Shadow of Buddhism", "Revival of Buddhism",
"The Last Journey"). Keer acknowledges material from Nanak Chand Rattu (Ambedkar's secretary) for the
second edition, which is why his account of the last days is so detailed [source: Keer, preface to the third edition](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).

## 1. Early encounter with the Buddha (Keluskar)

- **The unpublished preface** to *The Buddha and His Dhamma* (dated "26 Alipur Road, Delhi 6-4-56") tells the
  story in his own words. Text provided to Columbia by Eleanor Zelliot "as prepared by Vasant Moon"
  [source: Columbia University – unpublished preface](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/ambedkar_buddha/00_pref_unpub.html).
  Key points:
  - Father Ramji was a Kabirpanthi; grandfather Ramanandi; father made the boys read the Mahabharata and Ramayana aloud.
  - Occasion: "The year I passed the English Fourth Standard Examination" – community meeting; father refused; "Dada Keluskar"
    intervened and **presided**; gave him "a copy of his book on the life of the Buddha, which he had written for the
    Baroda Sayajirao Oriental Series".
  - Verified quote: "I read the book with great interest, and was greatly impressed and moved by it."
  - Verified quote: "This conviction has grown in me after thirty-five years of close study of all religions."
  - He describes arguing with his father about Bhishma, Drona, Krishna and Rama; says "This is how I turned to the Buddha,
    with the help of the book given to me by Dada Keluskar."
  - Says the urge to write the book came after a Maha Bodhi article, which he dates to **1951**.
  - "I claim no originality for the book. It is a compilation and assembly plant." Thanks Ashvaghosha's *Buddhacharita*.
  - Thanks Nanak Chand Rattu (village Sakrulli) and Parkash Chand (village Nangal Khurd), Hoshiarpur district, for typing;
    thanks his wife and Dr. Malvankar ("doctors talked of me as a dying flame"); thanks M. B. Chitnis for proofs.
  - Says the book is "one of the three books which will form a set for the proper understanding of Buddhism"; the
    others: *Buddha and Karl Marx* and *Revolution and Counter-Revolution in Ancient India*.
- **Vasant Moon** (BAWS Vol. 11 supplement preface, 1995): Krishnaji Arjun Keluskar, "former Principal, Wilson High
  School, Bombay", published a Marathi biography of the Buddha in **1898**, and presented it to Bhimrao "on his success
  in the Matriculation examination of the University of Bombay, in **1907**" [source: BAWS Vol. 11, p. ix (PDF 628)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume11.pdf#page=628).
  Moon notes Keluskar's book contains the Rohini river water-dispute story that Ambedkar used; he also notes the
  influence of Dharmanand Kosambi's 1940 Buddha biography and his 1949 Marathi play *Bodhisatwa* [source: BAWS Vol. 11, pp. ix–xi (PDF 628–630)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume11.pdf#page=628).
- **Keer**: Matriculation 1907 (282/750 marks); meeting in Bombay "under the presidentship of S. K. Bole"; Keluskar,
  "an assistant teacher at Wilson High School", was *present*, had met Bhimrao in a garden where both read, and gave him
  "his new book, *Life of Gautama Buddha*" [source: Keer, pp. 19–20](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- In a 1954 speech (police report) he said that after reading the Mahabharata and Gita in youth, "when he came across the book
  about Gautam Buddha, it had great impression on him and since that time he became the follower of Buddha" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 3, p. 505 (PDF 534)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=534).

**Conflicts:** exam (4th English standard vs Matriculation 1907); who presided (Keluskar per preface vs S. K. Bole per
Keer); Keluskar's post (assistant teacher vs former principal – could be different dates).

## 2. Yeola, 1935, and the search

- 12 Oct 1935: welcomed at Nashik; 13 Oct 1935: Yeola Municipality address, then conference "attended by about 10,000
  Untouchables", including delegates from Hyderabad State and Central Provinces; reception chairman Amritrao Rankhambe;
  speech "over an hour and a half"; referred to the five-year Kalaram temple satyagraha; quote "I solemnly assure you that I
  will not die a Hindu" (from Keer, pp. 252–53); resolution to stop the struggle for equality inside Hinduism
  [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 3, pp. 94–96 (PDF 123–125)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=123).
- Gandhi (Wardha, 15 Oct 1935, A.P.): "religion is not like house or cloak which can be changed at will"; urged him to
  reconsider [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 3, p. 97 (PDF 126)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=126).
  Gandhi's *Harijan* articles of 26 Oct 1935 ("On Its Last Legs") and 21 Mar 1936 are reprinted in Appendix I
  [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 3, pp. 561–563 (PDF 590–592)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=590).
- Ambedkar's reply (Bombay Chronicle, 16 Oct 1935): "we have decided one thing, and that after due deliberations and with
  deep conviction, that Hindu religion is not good for us"; "Inequality ... is the very basis of it"; "I agree with
  Gandhiji that religion is necessary"; "I have made up my mind to change my religion. I do not care if the masses do not come"
  [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 3, pp. 98–99 (PDF 127–128)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=127).
- A Sindhi Hindu reportedly wrote him a letter in blood threatening death if he renounced Hinduism (Vividha Vritta, 3 Nov 1935, via Keer)
  [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 3, p. 97 (PDF 126)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=126).
- 8 Dec 1935 Foras Road meeting (~10,000) – first Bombay meeting after Yeola [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 3, p. 100 (PDF 129)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=129).
- 13–14 Apr 1936: Sikh Mission Conference, Amritsar, reiterated intention to leave Hinduism [source: Columbia – Pritchett timeline 1930s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1930s.html).
  BAWS Vol. 17 Part 3 contents list further 1936 speeches, e.g. "What Way Emancipation?" (31 May 1936, p. 113) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 3, contents](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=18).
- Keer: "He did not believe in hurried action; and therefore he took more than twenty years to think and arrive at a mature decision" [source: Keer, p. 502](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).

## 3. 1950: Delhi, the Maha Bodhi essay, Sri Lanka

- **2 May 1950, Delhi** (Buddha Jayanti meeting, presided by Burma's ambassador): *The Bharat* (3 May 1950) reported he
  "called upon 70 million Harijans to embrace Buddhism", said religion should "no longer be inherited from father to son
  like goods and chattels", and that "I believe that religion is necessary for the mankind" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 3, pp. 402–403 (PDF 431–432)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=431).
  But on 5 May 1950 he told *Janata* he had **not** embraced Buddhism nor asked followers to do so [source: Keer, p. 421](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). (Conflict between press report and his clarification.)
- **"Buddha and the Future of His Religion"**: *Maha Bodhi*, Calcutta, "Vaishak Number, Vol. 58, May 1950" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 2, pp. 97–108 (PDF 120–131)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=120).
  Structure: I – Buddha vs Jesus, Mohammed, Krishna (Buddha as *Margadata*, not *Mokshadata*); II – Buddhism vs Hinduism
  ("Hinduism is a religion which is not founded on morality"; "the official gospel of Hinduism is inequality"; critique of the
  Gita's guna theory of chaturvarna); III – optimism about revival; IV – four tests (morality; science/reason; liberty-equality-
  fraternity; must not "sanctify or ennoble poverty") and "the only religion which satisfies all these tests is Buddhism";
  V – three steps: a Buddhist Bible (short life, Chinese Dhammapada, dialogues, ceremonies), reform of the Sangha, a world
  Buddhist mission. Ends: "the duty of a Buddhist is not merely to be a good Buddhist, his duty is to spread Buddhism."
  Keer summarises the same four tests [source: Keer, p. 421](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- Related essay: "The Rise and Fall of the Hindu Woman: Who Was Responsible for It?" (Maha Bodhi, 1950, replying to an Eve's
  Weekly article of 21 Jan 1950) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 2, p. 109 (PDF 132)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=132).
- **Sri Lanka**: went with his wife and party secretary P. N. Rajbhoj; reached Colombo by air 25 May 1950; invited by the
  Young Men's Buddhist Association [source: Keer, p. 421](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
  First WFB conference at Kandy 25 May–6 June 1950; representatives of 27 countries; on 26 May he said "I am an interested
  observer, not a delegate" and listed his three aims (see ceremonial; see how pure Buddhism is there; see whether the Sangha
  serves the laity) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 3, pp. 404–405 (PDF 433–434)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=433).
  Keer: at Kandy he "declined to address the Conference in its representative capacity" and disapproved of some resolutions [source: Keer, p. 421](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
  6 June 1950 Colombo speech on rise and fall of Buddhism (Vedic → Brahmanism → Hinduism; causes of decline; "Shankaracharya
  himself was a Buddhist"; "as a spiritual force it still exists in India") [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 3, pp. 406–409 (PDF 435–438)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=435).
  Also urged Untouchables in Ceylon to become Buddhist; returned via Trivandrum and Madras [source: Keer, pp. 422–423](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- 29 Sept 1950, Buddha Temple, Worli: declared "he would devote the rest of his life to the revival and spread of Buddhism in India"
  (Sunday News, 1 Oct 1950) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 3, p. 410 (PDF 439)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=439).
- Pritchett links a modern study: "Pragmatism, Persuasion, and Force in Bhimrao Ambedkar's Reconstruction of Buddhism" (SSRN) [source: Columbia – Pritchett timeline 1950s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1950s.html).

## 4. 1951–1955

- Began writing the Buddha book in **November 1951**; also began *Revolution and Counter Revolution* and *Buddha and Karl Marx*
  [source: Keer, p. 488](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- Lived at 26 Alipur Road from 1 Nov 1951 (house of the Maharaja of Sirohi) [source: PIB, 2 Dec 2003](https://archive.pib.gov.in/release02/lyr2003/rdec2003/02122003/r021220037.html).
- **May 1954, Rangoon**: Buddha Jayanti celebrations; left as the Bhandara by-election results came (lost by 8,381 votes) and read
  of his defeat in a Rangoon paper; stayed nearly a fortnight [source: Keer, pp. 453–454](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- 1954: bedridden two months; at a vihara near Poona announced he was writing a book on Buddhism and would convert when it was
  finished [source: Columbia – Pritchett timeline 1950s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1950s.html).
- **3 Oct 1954**, All-India Radio, "My Philosophy of Life": rejects the Gita's triguna-based social philosophy; his philosophy is
  "Liberty, Equality and Fraternity", derived "from the teachings of my Master, the Buddha" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 3, p. 503 (PDF 532)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=532).
- 28 Oct 1954 Naigaum meeting (~25,000; purse of Rs 1,18,000): "a devotee of Gautam Buddha, Kabir and Mahatma Phule" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 3, pp. 504–505 (PDF 533–534)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=533).
- **Dec 1954, Rangoon** (third WFB conference; BAWS calls it the International Buddhist Conference, 4 Dec 1954; with wife and
  attendant S. V. Savadkar) [source: Keer, pp. 481–482](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
  "Memorandum I – Record of My Talk to the Buddhist Sasana Council of Burma" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 3, pp. 506–511 (PDF 535–540)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=535):
  - India as first target; "the Buddha needs no passport nor does He require any visa".
  - Untouchables and Backward Classes prefer Buddhism to Christianity: not alien, social equality, rational.
  - Preliminary steps: (i) a Buddhist Gospel stressing social and moral teaching; (ii) a lay conversion ceremony "like Baptism";
    (iii) paid, possibly married lay preachers; (iv) a seminary; (v) Sunday congregational worship.
  - Big temples/viharas in Madras, Bombay, Nagpur, Delhi; schools and colleges; essay prizes.
  - Para 11 argues Brahmins should be kept out of positions of power "at least in the early stages" of the revival (record
    accurately if used; not used in chapter).
  - Editors' note: of the plan, he himself fulfilled two points – the Buddhist Gospel (*The Buddha and His Dhamma*) and a
    conversion ceremony.
  - Memorandum II: South India tour "from 7th to 14th July" (year not stated; probably 1954) – Buddhist communities at Perambur,
    Kanchipuram, Kolar Gold Fields, Bangalore etc.; Maharaja of Mysore donated 5 acres in Bangalore (8 July) for a seminary.
- Keer on the Rangoon speech: he listed the Pali provision in the Constitution, a Buddhist aphorism on Rashtrapati Bhavan, the
  Ashoka Chakra, and the Buddha Jayanti holiday as his achievements [source: Keer, pp. 481–482](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). (Not verified independently.)
- **25 Dec 1954, Dehu Road**: unveiled a Buddha image brought from Rangoon; claimed Vithoba/Pandurang of Pandharpur was
  originally the Buddha [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 3, p. 513 (PDF 542)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=542) [source: Keer, p. 482](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
  Attendance: about 4,000 (police source material, BAWS footnote) vs 20,000 (Keer).
- **Buddhist Society of India** (Bharatiya Bauddha Mahasabha): registered 4 May 1955 with the Registrar of Companies, Bombay;
  announced at Nare Park 8 May 1955 (Janata, 14 May 1955). Aims (10 points), membership by "Dhammadiksha as prescribed by the
  Society", President for life (first: Ambedkar), advisory council of ten. Founding members: B. R. Ambedkar, Madhav G. Malvankar
  (medical consultant), C. S. Pillai (architect), Bhalchandra K. Kabir, Bhagawant Sayaji Gaikwad, S. D. Gaikwad, Kashiram Vishram
  Sawadkar [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 2, pp. 478–484 (PDF 503–509)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=503).
  The Marathi/Hindi name "Bharatiya Bauddha Mahasabha" is widely used (seen in secondary sources; the BAWS constitution uses only
  the English name). The Nagpur ceremony handbill was issued by the "Bharatiya Bouddha Jana Samiti" (Nagpur branch), and BAWS
  also calls the organiser the "Indian Buddhist Council" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 3, pp. 524–525 (PDF 553–554)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=553).

## 5. 1956 before Nagpur

- Feb 1956: two chapters added to the book – "There is no god", "There is no soul"; the book had been printed earlier for
  private circulation as *The Buddha and His Gospel* (~50 copies) [source: Keer, p. 489](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- 5 Feb 1956, Delhi (Maha Bodhi Society): "The only religion, in my view, that can serve as an antidote to Communism is
  Buddhism"; "I have full faith in the rebirth" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 3, p. 514 (PDF 543)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=543).
- Preface: Keer says written 15 March 1956 in his own hand and dictated to Rattu [source: Keer, p. 489](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer); the surviving unpublished preface is dated 6 April 1956.
- 12 May 1956 BBC talk "Why I like Buddhism": prajna, karuna, samata; "Buddhism is a complete answer to Marx"; signed at 26 Alipur Road [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 3, pp. 515–516 (PDF 544–545)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=544).
- 20 May 1956, Voice of America, "Prospects of Democracy in India" (caste as "an ascending scale of hatred and descending scale of contempt") [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 3, pp. 519–523 (PDF 548–552)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=548).
- 24 May 1956, Nare Park (~75,000; 2500th Buddha Jayanti; presided by B. G. Kher): declared he would embrace Buddhism in
  October 1956; "Hinduism believes in God. Buddhism has no God. Hinduism believes in soul. According to Buddhism there is no soul.
  Hinduism believes in Chaturvarnya and the Caste System. Buddhism has no place for the Caste System and Chaturvarnya." (Keer, pp. 492–93) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 3, pp. 517–518 (PDF 546–547)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=546).
- June–Oct 1956 bedridden in Delhi; failing eyes; diabetes drug side-effects; depression [source: Columbia – Pritchett timeline 1950s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1950s.html).
- First week of August: told Shankaranand Shastri the ceremony could be at Bombay, Sarnath or Nagpur; he preferred Nagpur [source: Keer, p. 495](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- 21 Sept 1956 handbill (W. M. Godbole, Secretary, Bharatiya Bouddha Jana Samiti, Nagpur): "Venerable Bhikkhu Chandramani Maha Thera of Burma, now in India, shall perform the ceremony"; white garments [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 3, pp. 524–525 (PDF 553–554)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=553).
- 23 Sept 1956 statement: Nagpur, "Dussehra* day i.e. 14th October 1956" (*footnote "Ashoka Vijaya Dashmi"), 9–11 a.m.; converts must be over 18 (Prabuddha Bharat, 29 Sept 1956) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 3, p. 524 (PDF 553)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=553).
- 24 Sept 1956 letter to "Reverend Bhikku Chandramani, Kushenara, Gorakhpur District" – "I and my wife have decided to embrace Buddhism"; "You being the oldest Buddhist Monk in India" (Khairmode Vol. 12, p. 48) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 3, pp. 525–526 (PDF 554–555)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=554).
- Wrote to D. Valisinha that apart from customary rituals "he himself had framed ... a series of vows" [source: Keer, p. 496](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- Logistics: special train from Bombay V.T. 12 Oct, 9.15 p.m.; people lodged in Nagpur schools; ground near Vaccine Institute,
  South Ambazari Road; ~3,000 bulbs; separate enclosure for women; programme 13–15 Oct (Navyug, 21 Oct 1956) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 3, pp. 526–527 (PDF 555–556)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=555).
  Keer: 14-acre ground at Shradhanand Peth; replica of the Sanchi stupa on the dais; arrived Nagpur 11 Oct with wife and Rattu; stayed at Shyam (Sham) Hotel [source: Keer, pp. 497–498](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- **13 Oct 1956 press conference** (Keer): his Buddhism would follow the Buddha's own tenets, avoiding Hinayana/Mahayana
  differences; "His Buddhism would be a sort of neo-Buddhism or Navayana" (Keer's narration); predicted India would become
  Buddhist in 10–15 years; announced plan for the Republican Party open to all who accept liberty, equality and fraternity;
  disagreement that night with lieutenants who wanted to postpone conversion until after elections [source: Keer, pp. 498–499](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).

## 6. 14–16 October 1956

- BAWS account (Prabuddha Bharat, Ambedkar Buddha Deeksha special number, 27 Oct 1956) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 3, pp. 528–532 (PDF 557–561)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=557):
  - 2–2.5 lakh by 7 a.m.; Samata Sainik Dal (Nagpur and Bombay) managed it; only 10–11 police; ~30 press reporters; entry
    cards ran out, ceremony declared open to all.
  - Ambedkar arrived with "Mahasthavir Bhikkhu Chandramani" at 9.30 a.m.; "about 5-6 lakh people were assembled".
  - Other bhikkhus: Thero Pannatis (Sanchi Vihar, Bhopal), H. Siddhatissa (Ceylon), M. Sangharatna (Sarnath), G. Pradnyanand
    (Buddha Vihar, Lucknow), Ner Shramner (West Bengal), Rev. Paramsandhi.
  - Welcome song by Indutai Warale; tribute to his father Ramji Maloji Subhedar (death anniversary per Keer).
  - Chandramani recited Namo Tassa, Tisarana, Pancasila in Pali; Dr. and Mrs. Ambedkar (Maisaheb) repeated; he garlanded the
    Buddha and bowed thrice; mass ceremony from 10 a.m.; 22 oaths; Valisinha presented a Buddha image.
  - Prominent converts: Rajabhau Khobragade, Dadasaheb Gaikwad, R. D. Bhandare, Shantabai Dani, C. N. Mohite, G. T. Parmar,
    Yashwantrao Ambedkar (son), Mukundrao Ambedkar and others; "High Caste Hindus" Justice Bhavani Shankar Niyogi, V. M. Kulkarni,
    M. B. Chitnis (Principal, Milind College), B. S. Kabir.
  - Messages from U Ba Swe (PM of Burma), U Nu, H. W. Amarasuriya (Colombo), Dr. Arvind Barua (Calcutta), U Pannaloka (Rangoon).
  - Ended 11 a.m.; evening play *Yug Yatra* by Milind College students.
- Keer's account: "over three lakhs" watched; Chandramani described as "eighty-three-year-old Mahasthaveer Chandramani of
  Kushinara" with four bhikkhus; his declaration after conversion (Keer's report, includes "By discarding my ancient religion
  which stood for inequality and oppression today I am reborn"); voice choked when renouncing Hinduism; pledges "framed by
  himself" and "twenty-two in number"; "Nearly three lakhs of his followers embraced Buddhism"; Dr. M. B. Niyogi (former Chief
  Justice, Nagpur High Court) said denunciation of Hinduism had no place in original Buddhist rituals; no messages from Nehru,
  Radhakrishnan, Rajagopalachari or Rajendra Prasad [source: Keer, pp. 499–501](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
  (Note: Keer gives Niyogi's initials as "M. B."; BAWS gives "Bhavani Shankar Niyogi" – same person, initials differ.)
- Reactions (Keer, pp. 503–504): *Indian Express* saw it as "essentially Indian"; *Hitavada* compared him to Ashoka; Savarkar
  called it a jump into the fold of Hinduism; a leaflet by Chandramani and other bhikkhus said Hinduism and Buddhism are branches of
  the same tree [source: Keer, pp. 503–504](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). (Keer's own
  opinions in his last chapter are those of a Hindu biographer; do not present them as fact.)
- **15 Oct 1956 speech** (Marathi; Prabuddha Bharat 27 Oct 1956):
  - BAWS translation [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 3, pp. 533–545 (PDF 562–574)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=562) – "I do not want blind followers. Those who wish to embrace Buddhism, they should accept it with comprehension" (p. 536, PDF 565); "I feel I have been liberated from the hell"; "Religion is necessary for the poor"; "every Buddhist person has the right to give Deeksha" (p. 545); asked for a twentieth of income for the cause.
  - Damle & Zelliot translation, "Why Was Nagpur Chosen?" (1964), 37 sections; Zelliot notes he was very ill [source: Columbia – Pritchett](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/txt_ambedkar_conversion.html). Reasons for Nagpur: the Nag people spread Buddhism; denies choosing it to confront the RSS.
  - BAWS editors' note: eyewitnesses say he said "in the pocket of my coat" rather than "bag" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 3, p. 536 (PDF 565)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=565).
- 15 Oct: second initiation at the same place; Nagpur Municipal Corporation address at the Town Hall; meeting of partymen at Shyam
  Hotel ("his last political speech", per Keer) [source: Keer, pp. 501–504](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- Valisinha eyewitness (from Hoti Lal Nim, ed., *Thoughts on Dr. Ambedkar*, Agra 1969, pp. 94–95): "Dr. Ambedkar with 500,000 of his
  followers recited Trisarana and Panchasila"; fourteen-acre site; "tears of joy ran down my cheeks" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 3, pp. 545–546 (PDF 574–575)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=574).
- **16 Oct 1956, Chanda (Chandrapur)**: "Ambedkar attended a conversion ceremony at Chanda, where the vast gathering of the
  Depressed Class people repeated the pledges which he gave"; left by train, then flew from Nagpur to Delhi [source: Keer, p. 504](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
  SabrangIndia (2006) claims "another four lakh people" – unverified [source: SabrangIndia – "Caste out"](https://sabrangindia.in/article/caste-out/).
- 22 Oct 1956 letter from U Chan Htoon (Supreme Court of Burma): about 5,000 Tamils accepted Buddhism in Rangoon on 28 Sept;
  Asia Foundation help for publishing his book had fallen through; he was leading a Burmese delegation to the 4th WFB conference
  in Kathmandu [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 3, pp. 546–548 (PDF 575–577)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=575).
- 30 Oct 1956 letter to Valisinha: "it was a great event and the crowd that came forward for conversion was beyond my
  expectation"; monks should become "like the Christian missionaries, social workers and social preachers" [source: Keer, p. 506](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).

## 7. The 22 vows – exact BAWS text

Source: Prabuddha Bharat special number, 27 Oct 1956, as printed in [BAWS Vol. 17 Part 3, pp. 530–532 (PDF 559–561)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=559).
Spelling as printed (including errors):

1. I shall have no faith in Brahma, Vishnu and Mahesh, nor shall I worship them.
2. I shall have no faith in Rama and Krishna, nor shall I worship them.
3. I shall have no faith in 'Gouri', 'Ganpathi' and other Gods and Goddesses of Hindu religion, nor shall I worship them.
4. I do not believe in the theory of incarnation of Gods.
5. I do not and shall not believe that the Lord Buddha was the incarnation of Vishnu. I believe this to be, mischievous and false propaganda.
6. I shall not perform 'Shraaddha' nor, shall I give 'pind-dan'. (footnote: rituals performed after a death)
7. I shall not act in any manner contrary to the principles and teachings of the Buddha.
8. I shall not perform any ceremony through Brahmins.
9. I believe in the equality of mankind.
10. I shall endeavour to establish equality.
11. I shall follow the Eightfold Path taught by the Buddha.
12. I shall follow the 'Ten Paramitas' enunciated by the Buddha.
13. I shall be compassionate to all living beings and nurture them with care.
14. I shall not steal.
15. I shall not lie.
16. I shall not commit carnal sins.
17. I shall not consume liquor.
18. I shall strive to lead my life in conformity with the three principles of Buddhism i. e. Pradnya (wisdom), Sheel (character) and Karuna (compassion).
19. I hereby embrace Buddhism by renouncing my old Hindu religion which is detrimental to the prosperity to the humankind and descriminate human beings and treat them low.
20. I firmly believe that the Buddha Dhamma is the Saddhamma.
21. I believe, I am entering the new life.
22. Hereafter I pledge to conduct myself in accordance with the teachings of the Buddha.

Notes: the vows were recited in Marathi; a Marathi printed version is on Wikimedia Commons
([File:22 Vows (in Marathi)…](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:22_Vows_(in_Marathi)_administered_by_Most_Revered_Bodhisattva_Dr._Babasaheb_Ambedkar_on_the_occasion_of_Buddhist_Dhamma_Deeksha_on_14_October_1956_at_Deeksha_Bhoomi,_Nagpur.jpg)).
English versions online differ in wording (e.g. "I shall not consider Brahma, Vishnu and Mahesh as God" vs "I shall have no
faith in"); the BAWS text is used as the reference. Background: his 1954 Rangoon memorandum asked for a lay ceremony "like
Baptism", saying "Merely uttering the Pancha Sila is not enough" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 3, p. 509 (PDF 538)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=538).

Related: the Pali Grammar, Pali Dictionary and the booklet *Bouddha Pooja Patha* ("printed and published by Dr. Ambedkar himself,
for the followers of Buddhism") are in BAWS Vol. 16 [source: BAWS Vol. 16, foreword (PDF 9)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume16.pdf#page=9) [source: BAWS Vol. 16, Book IV (PDF 747)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume16.pdf#page=747).
Observation (not used in chapters): the 1997 preface to BAWS Vol. 16 by Maharashtra CM Manohar Joshi states "Buddha is the 10th
incarnation of God" – the opposite of vow 5 [source: BAWS Vol. 16, preface (PDF 7)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume16.pdf#page=7).

## 8. Navayana – evidence and debate

- Keer (p. 498), 13 Oct 1956 press conference, indirect speech: "His Buddhism would be a sort of neo-Buddhism or Navayana."
- UGC e-PG Pathshala module (Priyanka Gupta, 2018) quotes him as saying "Our Bouddha Dharma is a new Bouddha Dharma Navayan"
  without a primary citation; uses "Navayana Buddhism or Neo-Buddhism" interchangeably; cites Virginia Hancock (practice differs
  from his principles) and Timothy Fitzgerald (rural Mahar Buddhism changed little) [source: INFLIBNET](https://ebooks.inflibnet.ac.in/socp12/chapter/navoyana-buddhism/).
- Ananya Vajpeyi (Open, 2012): "called Navayana, or the New Way"; debates whether it was a new school or a political identity;
  notes the first critical edition of *The Buddha and His Dhamma* by Aakash Singh Rathore and Ajay Verma [source: Open magazine](https://openthemagazine.com/essays/arts-letters/the-guru-ambedkar).
- *Journal of Global Buddhism* (Tereza Menšíková, 2023) uses "Ambedkarite Buddhists" [source: Journal of Global Buddhism](https://www.globalbuddhism.org/article/view/3840).
- Keer (p. 522) calls it "Ambedkarism"/"Ambedkarayan" and reports Buddhist critics (Maha Bodhi, Dec 1959: "a dangerous book";
  The Light of the Dhamma, Rangoon, Jan 1959) [source: Keer, p. 521](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- Columbia lists Virginia Hancock, "New Buddhism for New Aspirations: Navayana Buddhism of Ambedkar and His Followers," *Manushi* 145 (2004) and Gail Omvedt, "Ambedkar's New Buddhism," *The Hindu*, 25 Feb 1999 as further reading [source: Columbia – Pritchett "since then"](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1960s_andafter.html).
- The 22 vows themselves say only "Buddha Dhamma"/"Saddhamma"; the book title uses "Dhamma".
- The book's Introduction poses four "problems": the reason for Parivraja; whether the Four Aryan Truths are original; how karma
  and rebirth fit with no-soul; whether the bhikkhu is a "perfect man" or "social servant" [source: Columbia – Introduction](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/ambedkar_buddha/00_intro.html).
- Not found: any statement (in sources opened) that "neo-Buddhist" is considered offensive by followers – widely said, but not verified here.

## 9. His Buddhist works and where the manuscripts are

**The Buddha and His Dhamma**
- Begun Nov 1951; typed/retyped by N. C. Rattu and Parkash Chand; paragraphs "cut away and pasted"; printed privately as *The
  Buddha and His Gospel* (~50 copies); Feb 1956 chapters added; preface 15 Mar 1956 (Keer) [source: Keer, pp. 488–489](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- Printing began May 1956, slowed by proof revisions (Keer, pp. 488–489, 491) [source: Columbia – Pritchett 1950s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1950s.html).
- On the night of 5 Dec 1956 he had the typescripts of the Preface and Introduction put on his bedside table [source: Keer, p. 513](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- Published 1957: title page "Siddharth College Publication: I, 1957" [source: BAWS Vol. 11 (PDF 12)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume11.pdf#page=12);
  Vasant Moon says People's Education Society, which later issued four editions and then entrusted the book to the Maharashtra
  government for BAWS Vol. 11; Hindi translation by Bhadant Anand Kausalyayan 1961; Adele M. Fiske (University of Pennsylvania)
  MA dissertation on its use of scriptures; Moon's 1995 supplement lists Pali sources and an index [source: BAWS Vol. 11, pp. vii–xii (PDF 626–631)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume11.pdf#page=626).
- Columbia's e-text is based on BAWS Vol. 11 (Education Department, Government of Maharashtra, **1992**) [source: Columbia – editor's introduction](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/ambedkar_buddha/00_fwp.html).
- The preface was left out of the 1957 book; Keer quotes a passage (thanks to wife and Malvankar) and calls the omission "a mystery" [source: Keer, pp. 519–520](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).

**Buddha or Karl Marx**
- "The Committee found three different typed copies of an essay on Buddha and Karl Marx in loose sheets, two of which have
  corrections in the author's own handwriting" [source: BAWS Vol. 3, p. 439 (PDF 452)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume3.pdf#page=452). Text pp. 441–462.
- Keer: up to March 1956 it needed one more chapter; completed the last chapter after taking out *Das Kapital* (placed after 3 Dec) [source: Keer, pp. 488 and 511](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
  Pritchett: completed 2 Dec 1956, "his last finished work" [source: Columbia – Pritchett 1950s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1950s.html).
- Separate speech of the same title, Kathmandu, 20 Nov 1956 (Report of the 4th WFB Conference) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 3, pp. 549–558 (PDF 578–587)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=578).

**Revolution and Counter-Revolution in Ancient India**
- Planned as seven books; "The Committee was able to find some pages and few chapters in his collection. The chapters are also
  incomplete"; 13 chapters printed (Ancient India on Exhumation … The Women and the Counter-Revolution); two typed copies of the
  first chapter with handwritten corrections [source: BAWS Vol. 3, pp. 149–151 (PDF 162–164)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume3.pdf#page=162).
  The plan is printed in "Schemes of Books" [source: BAWS Vol. 3, p. 465ff](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume3.pdf#page=12).

**Where the papers went (what we could establish)**
- He took "some of the manuscripts of his incomplete books" to Kathmandu in Nov 1956 [source: Keer, p. 507](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- After his death his widow and son went to court over his property and later compromised [source: Keer, p. 520](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- Manuscripts of the Pali Dictionary were "received from the custody of Administrator General of Maharashtra State" [source: BAWS Vol. 16 (PDF 23)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume16.pdf#page=23).
- Other papers "were found in the record received by the Government from the Administrator General, Maharashtra State in 1979" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 2, p. 96 (PDF 119)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=119) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 2 (PDF 386)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=386).
- A typed outline was "in the custody of People's Education Society, Bombay" [source: BAWS Vol. 12, p. 77 (PDF 98)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume12.pdf#page=98).
- Unfinished typescripts and drafts "were found among his notes and papers and gradually made available" [source: Columbia – Pritchett 1950s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1950s.html).
- The unpublished preface text reached Columbia via Eleanor Zelliot "as prepared by Vasant Moon" [source: Columbia – unpublished preface](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/ambedkar_buddha/00_pref_unpub.html).
- Pritchett's later-years page (entry mislabelled "1901") says his widow donated his ashes to the Symbiosis museum in Pune [source: Columbia – Pritchett "since then"](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1960s_andafter.html).
- **Not established:** the present physical location of the original handwritten/typed manuscript of *The Buddha and His Dhamma*, or
  whether the Government of Maharashtra's Source Material Publication Committee still holds the typescripts used for BAWS Vols. 3, 11
  and 16. Needs direct enquiry (Maharashtra Education Department; People's Education Society / Siddharth College library;
  Dr. Ambedkar Foundation).

## 10. Final days (Nov–Dec 1956) – detailed notes

- Elphinstone College centenary invitation; signed a fund appeal [source: Keer, p. 506](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- Before leaving for Nepal: civil suit in Bombay High Court by contractors over the extension of Rajagriha; Rs 40,000 deposited [source: Keer, p. 507](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- Stay in Nepal arranged through M. Jyoti of Calcutta; Dr. Malvankar accompanied; M. B. Chitnis and B. H. Varale went too; flew
  Patna → Kathmandu 14 Nov 1956; conference inaugurated by King Mahendra in the Singha Durbar gallery hall, 15 Nov; Nepal declared a
  holiday [source: Keer, pp. 506–507](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- 20 Nov speech: G. P. Malalasekera had asked for "Non-violence in Buddhism"; delegates wanted "Buddha or Karl Marx"; U Chan Htoon
  and his wife escorted him; King Mahendra present; Chandramani gave Tisarana and Pancasila (Bhikshu Sumedha, pp. 126–127)
  [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 3, p. 549 (PDF 578)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=578).
  Verified quotes: "although I am here, I am physically a very ill man" (p. 549); "if we all become one tenth as enlightened as the
  Buddha was, we can bring about the same result by the methods of love, of justice, and good will" (p. 558, PDF 587); criticises
  idle monks and Ceylon's two-minute sermon (pp. 556–557).
- Return: spoke at Banaras Hindu University (on Shankara's "Brahma Satyam Jagan Mithya") and Kashi Vidyapith; second round of
  Buddhist holy places; reached Delhi from Kushinagar by air 30 Nov 1956 [source: Keer, p. 509](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- 1 Dec: Buddhist art exhibition (Mathura Road), bookshop at Connaught Place. 2 Dec: function at Ashok Vihar for the Dalai Lama. 3 Dec:
  group photo on the lawn (taken by Balu Kabir); visited his sick gardener ("I am not [afraid of death]"). Planned train to Bombay 14 Dec
  for conversions on 16 Dec (later decided to fly) [source: Keer, pp. 509–511](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- 4 Dec: Council of States; letters to P. K. Atre and S. M. Joshi about the Republican Party; letter to M. B. Samartha [source: Keer, p. 511](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- 5 Dec: upset in the afternoon; Jain delegation c. 8 p.m. (gave him *Jain aur Buddha*); sang "Buddham Saranam Gachhami" and asked for
  the record; sang Kabir's "Chal Kabir tera bhav sagar dera"; 11.15 p.m. Rattu left; asked for typescripts of the Preface and
  Introduction and the letters to Atre, Joshi and the Burma Government [source: Keer, pp. 511–513](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- 6 Dec, ~6.30 a.m.: Savita Ambedkar found him dead; Rattu called; artificial respiration failed; "suffering severely from diabetic
  neurosis; his heart ... had stopped beating" [source: Keer, pp. 513–514](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- Visitors: Nehru, Pant, Jagjivan Ram, Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha; Jagjivan Ram arranged the plane; 5-hour procession to the airport;
  took off 9.30 p.m. with Shankaranand Shastri, Bhikkhu Anand Kausalyayan, Mrs. Ambedkar, Rattu, Sudama (cook) and others [source: Keer, pp. 514–515](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- 7 Dec: Santa Cruz 3 a.m.; Rajagriha; Bombay mills, docks, railway workshops, schools, cinemas closed; hartals in Sholapur, Nagpur;
  Ahmedabad mills closed; procession from 1.50 p.m. via Vincent Road (now Dr. Babasaheb Ambedkar Road), Poibavdi, Elphinstone Bridge,
  Sayani Road, Gokhale Road to the "Dadar Hindu crematorium"; two miles long; ~4 hours; "More than half a million people"; "Over a lakh
  of people embraced Buddhism at the crematorium"; pyre lit by Yashwantrao at 7.50 p.m.; police sounded "last post" ("first time in
  Bombay to a non-official person"); speeches by Anand Kausalyayan and P. K. Atre; coincided with end of 2500th Buddha Jayanti
  celebrations at Sanchi [source: Keer, pp. 515–517](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- Nehru in Lok Sabha (Keer's paraphrase): would be remembered as the "symbol of revolt against all the oppressing features of Hindu
  society"; House adjourned. Tributes from Rajendra Prasad, Savarkar, Rajagopalachari (critical of conversion), press, U Nu [source: Keer, pp. 517–519](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
  (Not checked against Lok Sabha debates – check eparlib for exact words before quoting.)
- Inquiry: on the 11th day a Delhi meeting asked for an investigation; son lodged a police complaint; on 26 Nov 1957 Home Minister Pant
  told the Lok Sabha that a DIG Delhi Police inquiry found a natural death [source: Keer, p. 519](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
  (Lok Sabha record not checked.)
- Pritchett: died "on the night of Dec. 5 or the early morning of Dec. 6"; "huge Buddhist-style funeral procession" on Dec. 7; cremated on
  the seashore [source: Columbia – Pritchett 1950s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1950s.html).
- Indian mission in Geneva: funeral at Dadar Chowpatty on 7 Dec 1956 "thronged by at least half a million mourners" [source: Permanent Mission of India, Geneva](https://www.pmindiaun.gov.in/pageinfo/MTI4MQ,,).

## 11. Legacy – detailed notes

- **Republican Party of India**: plan announced 13 Oct 1956 (Keer p. 498); "In conformity with the directions of Dr. Ambedkar the
  Republican Party was formed; but it was a mere conversion of the Scheduled Castes Federation"; split into two factions after
  Maharashtra's formation [source: Keer, pp. 520–521](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
  Exact founding date **3 October 1957** appears on Wikipedia (pointer only; not verified from a primary source).
- **After-death government actions** (Keer p. 520): Maharashtra CM Yashwantrao Chavan declared his birthday a public holiday; gave
  11 acres at Nagpur (Diksha ground) and land at the Dadar crematory for memorials; a statue near the old Secretariat, Bombay; a stupa at
  Chaitya Bhoomi in 1968 (Keer) vs inaugurated 5 Dec 1971 by Meera Ambedkar (Forward Press) [source: Forward Press](https://forwardpress.in/?p=20295).
- **Buddhist population**: 2011 Census (as tabulated by Pew): India 8,440,000 Buddhists; Maharashtra 6,530,000 [source: Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2021/09/PF_09.21.21_AppendixA.pdf).

  Avinash Mahatekar; mostly Mahar Buddhist youths in their 20s; Little Magazine movement; *Golpitha*; split over Ambedkarism vs Marxism [source: The Quint](https://www.thequint.com/amp/story/photos/in-photos-ambedkarites-hold-events-to-celebrate-golden-jubilee-of-dalit-panther).

  Not verified: the 27 July 1978 legislature resolution date and the Pochiram Kamble killing (both seen only in search extracts).
- **BAWS**: series begun 14 April 1979 by the Education Department, Government of Maharashtra, for the "Dr. Babasaheb Ambedkar Source Material
  Publication Committee"; Vol. 1 compiled by Vasant Moon [source: BAWS Vol. 1 (PDF 4–5)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=5).
  First-edition dates printed: most volumes "14 April, 1979" (series start); Vol. 14 "6 December, 1995"; Vol. 16 "14 April, 1998"; Columbia gives
  Vol. 11 as 1992. Dr. Ambedkar Foundation reprints Jan 2014, Aug 2019, Aug 2020; Hindi Vols. 22–40 published 2019 [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 3 (PDF 5, 9)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=9).
  2003: "translated into 11 Indian languages so far"; Vajpayee urged all Indian languages [source: PIB, 2 Dec 2003](https://archive.pib.gov.in/release02/lyr2003/rdec2003/02122003/r021220037.html).
- **Bharat Ratna 1990** (posthumous) [source: Permanent Mission of India, Geneva](https://www.pmindiaun.gov.in/pageinfo/MTI4MQ,,). The date 31 March 1990 (presentation) appears in a Logically Facts fact-check citing Hindustan Times – could not open; unverified.
- **26 Alipur Road**: land acquired by exchange deed 14 Oct 2003; dedicated 2 Dec 2003 by PM Vajpayee [source: PIB, 2 Dec 2003](https://archive.pib.gov.in/release02/lyr2003/rdec2003/02122003/r021220037.html) [source: PIB, 24 Oct 2003](https://archive.pib.gov.in/release02/lyr2003/roct2003/24102003/r241020038.html);
  foundation stone of new memorial 21 Mar 2016; inaugurated 13 Apr 2018; book-shaped; ~7,400 sq m; Rs 100 crore; museum, meditation hall,
