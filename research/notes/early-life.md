## 1. Caste, untouchability and the Mahar community

### Caste in Ambedkar's own words
- Endogamy (no marrying outside the group) is "the only one that can be called the essence of Caste" — *Castes in India* (paper read at Columbia, 9 May 1916) [source: BAWS Vol. 1, p. 8](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=23).
- "Caste System is not merely division of labour. It is also a division of labourers." Caste is "an heirarchy [sic] in which the divisions of labourers are graded one above the other". It assigns work by "the social status of the parents" — *Annihilation of Caste* (1936) [source: BAWS Vol. 1, p. 47](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=62).
- Savarna vs Avarna: "The Brahmins, Kshatriyas, Vaishyas and Shudras are Savarnas. The Untouchables or Ati-Shudras are called Avarnas, those who have no Varna." (The BAWS text runs "AtiShudras" together at a line break.) — *Who Were the Shudras?* [source: BAWS Vol. 7, p. 36](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume7.pdf#page=55).
- Untouchables were "outside the pale of Hindu Society which recognizes only four classes namely Brahmins, Kshatriyas, Vaishyas and Shudras" [source: BAWS Vol. 5, p. 241](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume5.pdf#page=256).
- A person's "occupation, his dwelling, his gods and his politics are all determined for him by the group to which he belongs" [source: BAWS Vol. 5, p. 63](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume5.pdf#page=78).
- The village is split into touchable and untouchable quarters, at a distance from each other. The untouchable quarters are named Maharwada, Mangwada, Chamrotti, Khatkana and so on. De jure they belong to the village; de facto they are separate [source: BAWS Vol. 5, p. 62](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume5.pdf#page=77).
- A 1943 government profile describes "untouchables whose very shadows polluted high caste Hindus in their vicinity" [source: BAWS Vol. 10, p. 3](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=20).
- Opening of *Waiting for a Visa*: untouchables live "on the edge of a village", clean its filth, run errands, collect food at Hindu doors, buy spices and oil "from a distance", and "yet never touch nor be touched by any one belonging to the village" [source: BAWS Vol. 12, p. 663](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume12.pdf#page=684).

### The Mahars
- Largest "untouchable" caste in Maharashtra (about 9% of the area's population). Found "on the outskirts of every village". The maharwada lay traditionally to the east of a village or downriver. Some Mahars migrated to Madhya Pradesh and Baroda. 1981 census: 1,648,269 Mahars and 3,946,149 Buddhists in Maharashtra [source: Eleanor Zelliot, "Mahar", Encyclopedia of World Cultures](https://www.encyclopedia.com/humanities/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/mahar).
- Traditional duties (Zelliot): deciding voice in land disputes; bringing wood to the burning ground; removing dead animals; carrying messages to other villages; looking after the horses of travelling officials; repairing the village wall; watchman; town crier for the headman. They were *watandars* (hereditary holders) with some inalienable land, and were never primarily farmers [source: Zelliot](https://www.encyclopedia.com/humanities/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/mahar).
- Zelliot: Mahars served in Shivaji's armies in the 17th century and in British forces until the army was reorganised on a "martial peoples" basis. Ex-army Mahars were the first to petition the government for redress [source: Zelliot](https://www.encyclopedia.com/humanities/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/mahar).
- Ambedkar's 1928 speech moving Bill No. XII of 1928 to amend the Bombay Hereditary Offices Act 1874 (B.L.C. Debates Vol. XXIII, pp. 708–21, 3 August 1928). Points:
  - The "inferior" hereditary officers were Mahars in the Deccan, Vethias/Varthanias in Gujarat, and Ramoshis/Juglias/Holiyas in Karnataka [source: BAWS Vol. 2, p. 75](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=94).
  - Every department could claim their services: irrigation, revenue, vaccination, education, local self-government, police, excise [source: BAWS Vol. 2, p. 75](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=94).
  - They were "maid of all work", liable day and night, and the whole family was liable, including female members [source: BAWS Vol. 2, p. 76](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=95).
  - Monthly cash paid by government (answer to a March 1925 question): Thana Rs 1-8-0; Ahmednagar Rs 1-8-0; East Khandesh Rs 1-12-0; West Khandesh 9 annas 4 pies; Nasik Re 0-13-4; Poona Rs 1-1-4; Satara Re 0-2-1; Sholapur Re 0-3-3; nothing in Belgaum; no figures for Ratnagiri and Kolaba [source: BAWS Vol. 2, pp. 76–77](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=96).
  - Income came from inam land and baluta (grain from villagers). Baluta was left to "the sweet will of the ryots" [source: BAWS Vol. 2, p. 77](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=96).
  - The bill itself is dated 13 April 1928 [source: BAWS Vol. 2, p. 74](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=93).
- "In Provinces like Bombay the Untouchables are village servants … One of the duties … is to skin and carry the dead animals … They have to choose between doing the dirty work or facing starvation." [source: BAWS Vol. 5, p. 257](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume5.pdf#page=272).
- Ambedkar's own theory, in an untitled paper the editors called "The Mahars: Who were they and how they became the Untouchables?": Mahars were not aboriginal. Their *kuls* (clans) match those of the Marathas, and their greeting "Johar" comes from a Kshatriya salutation. They were "broken men" settled outside the village as watch-and-ward [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part II, pp. 137–145](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=160). (This is his interpretation, not settled history.)

## 2. Mahars in the army, and the ban

- Ambedkar: India was conquered between 1757 (Plassey) and 1818 (Koregaon). "The men who fought with Clive in the battle of Plassey were the Dusads … The men who fought in the battle of Koregaon were the Mahars, and the Mahars are Untouchables." The Bombay and Madras armies were "mostly drawn from the Untouchables, the Mahars in Bombay and the Pariahs in Madras". He wrote that the British "since about 1890" banned untouchable recruitment, and that by about 1910 they had "completely disappeared from the Army". The ban came with the "class composition" and "martial races" principles of 1890. A battalion, the "111 Mahars", was raised in the Great War and later disbanded. Many had risen to "Jamadar, Subhedar and Subhedar Majors". — "The Untouchables and the Pax Britannica" (unpublished MS) [source: BAWS Vol. 12, pp. 83–89](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume12.pdf#page=104).
- Ambedkar's Ranade address (published 1943 as *Ranade, Gandhi and Jinnah*): in his father's papers he found a petition from Mahar commissioned and non-commissioned officers "against the orders issued in 1892 banning the recruitment of the Mahars in the Army", and was told Ranade had drafted it [source: BAWS Vol. 1, p. 211](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=226). Ashok Gopal finds the Ranade claim "implausible" [source: Ashok Gopal, Scroll excerpt from *A Part Apart*](https://scroll.in/article/1047284/how-ramji-sakpal-struggled-for-the-education-that-gave-us-his-son-bhimrao-ambedkar).
- 1943 profile: "Had the recruitment of Mahars to the Army not been stopped in 1892, it is possible that the Subedar's son would have followed the profession of arms" [source: BAWS Vol. 10, p. 4](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=21).
- Constable (2001): Dalit ex-soldiers organised against "caste-based exclusion from the Indian army after 1892" [source: Constable, *Journal of Asian Studies* 60(2), abstract](https://knowledge.lancashire.ac.uk/id/eprint/57158/).
- Keer: the 1892 orders; Ramji Sakpal "took a leading part" in protesting and approached Ranade to draft a petition [source: Keer, *Dr. Ambedkar: Life and Mission*, 3rd ed., p. 12](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- Gopal (citing Ardythe Basham's 1986 PhD thesis and others):
  - The Konkan supplied nearly half the Bombay Army's infantry (about 20,000 men) until 1877.
  - Mahars ("Parwaris", said to come from *parwar*, the grain left on the threshing floor) were about a sixth of recruits, mostly from Ratnagiri.
  - The 1880 Ratnagiri Gazetteer counted 2,180 Mahars, including pensioners, on the rolls; about 6% were officers.
  - Infantry ranks for Indians: sepoy, lance naik, naik, havildar, havildar major, jemadar, subhedar, subhedar major. All were junior to the lowest British officer.
  - Pay in 1881: a sepoy got Rs 7 a month and a subhedar Rs 100; Bombay mill wages were Rs 5–50.
  - The army valued rank over caste. Every regiment had a school, and the Sappers and Miners had English-medium schools.
  - Mahar employment ended in 1892. On 25 years' service, Mahars had to take pension and leave.
  - [source: Ashok Gopal, Scroll](https://scroll.in/article/1047284/how-ramji-sakpal-struggled-for-the-education-that-gave-us-his-son-bhimrao-ambedkar)
- Petitions:
  - Gopal Baba Walangkar (retired, living in Dapoli) created "the first public petition of the Untouchable movement" in **1894**, asking the army to resume recruitment [source: Pritchett timeline, citing Zelliot](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1890s.html).
  - Gopal dates the petition of the Anarya Dosh Pariharak Mandali ("Non-Aryan Association for the Removal of Wrongs") to the commander-in-chief of the Bombay Army to **April 1895**. Walangkar came from Mahad, retired in 1886, met Jotirao Phule while posted in Pune, and joined the Satyashodhak Samaj [source: Ashok Gopal, Scroll](https://scroll.in/article/1047284/how-ramji-sakpal-struggled-for-the-education-that-gave-us-his-son-bhimrao-ambedkar).
- The Dapoli school struggle, 1892–94 (Gopal, citing Constable 2000 and Basham):
  - July 1892: pensioners asked the Dapoli municipality to admit 14 children (ten Mahar, four Chambhar). Refused.
  - Further letters followed in September 1892, November 1892 (to the assistant collector), January 1893 (to the collector) and November 1893.
  - February 1894: they wrote to Revenue Commissioner M. J. Nugent. One signatory was "Pensioner Subedar Ramnak Malnac", most likely Ramji Sakpal.
  - Nugent rejected municipal president Vishnu Hari Barve's "verandah" compromise, backed by Director of Public Instruction K. M. Chatfield. The children were admitted to the classroom, seated three to four feet apart.
  - [source: Ashok Gopal, Scroll](https://scroll.in/article/1047284/how-ramji-sakpal-struggled-for-the-education-that-gave-us-his-son-bhimrao-ambedkar)
- 1943 profile: the Mahars "once supplied the Bombay Army of the East India Company with its manpower" [source: BAWS Vol. 10, p. 3](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=20).

## 3. Bhima Koregaon, 1 January 1818

Do not confuse this with the Koregaon in Khatav taluka, Satara district, of the childhood journey (see section 6).

- Gazetteer of the Bombay Presidency, Vol. XVIII Part III, Poona (1885), pp. 244–247 [source: Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/wbsl.13909):
  - Koregaon village had a population of 960 in 1881. It lies 25 miles south-west of Sirur and about 16 miles north-east of Poona.
  - The headline says "800 British troops against 30,000 Marathas". The detailed account: the 2nd Battalion, 1st Regiment Bombay Native Infantry (500 rank and file) under Captain Francis Staunton, with 300 irregular horse and two six-pounders manned by 24 European Madras artillerymen, left Sirur at 8 pm on 31 December 1817.
  - They faced the Peshwa's army of 25,000 Maratha horse. Three parties of 600 "Arabs, Gosavis and regular infantry" stormed the village.
  - 275 of the 834 defenders were killed, wounded or missing, including 20 of the 26 gunners. The Marathas lost 500–600.
  - General Sir T. Hislop called it "one of the most heroic and brilliant achievements ever recorded on the annals of the Army". The battalion was made Grenadiers, with the motto "Mangalur and Koregaon". Staunton got a sword, 500 guineas and, in 1823, the CB.
  - The obelisk is 65 feet high (25 ft pediment), on a stone platform 32 ft 4 in square, with a wall and iron railing. Marathi inscriptions are on the north and south sides and the English one on the west. Names of the killed and wounded are inscribed.
  - The English inscription ends "MDCCCXXII" (1822) and says Staunton "accomplished one of the proudest triumphs of the British Army in the East".
- Kumbhojkar (EPW, 20 October 2012, vol. 47 no. 42): Lt Col Delamain, passing the next year, saw a 60-foot obelisk being built. Neither side won decisively. The battle was remembered as a triumph because the Company won the war. The obelisk was built to show British power and now serves the Mahars [source: EPW](https://www.epw.in/journal/2012/42/special-articles/contesting-power-contesting-memories.html).
- 22 Mahar soldiers are named on the obelisk, identifiable by the suffix -nak [source: Ashok Gopal, Scroll](https://scroll.in/article/1047284/how-ramji-sakpal-struggled-for-the-education-that-gave-us-his-son-bhimrao-ambedkar). Wikipedia (pointer only) cites V. Longer, *Forefront for Ever* (1981), p. 14, for the dead of the 2/1st: 22 Mahars/Parwaris, 16 Marathas, 8 Rajputs, 2 Muslims and 1–2 probably Indian Jews [source: Wikipedia, Battle of Koregaon (pointer)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Koregaon).
- Outlook (13 January 2018): the badge of the later Mahar Regiment carried the obelisk with a scroll reading "Mahar". It also says about 850 men on the Company side, and that only about 1,800 of the Peshwa's 25,000 saw action [source: Outlook](https://www.outlookindia.com/national/the-british-the-marathas-and-the-mahars-news-299709).
- Ambedkar visited the memorial in 1927 [source: Ashok Gopal, Scroll](https://scroll.in/article/1047284/how-ramji-sakpal-struggled-for-the-education-that-gave-us-his-son-bhimrao-ambedkar). Wikipedia gives 1 January 1927, but its photo caption says 28 December 1927 (unresolved; belongs to a later chapter).

## 4. Reformers before Ambedkar

- **Jotirao Phule** (Savitribai Phule Pune University, Mahatma Phule Chair timeline, based on *Mahatma Phule Samagra Wangmay*, ed. Y. D. Phadke, 1991) [source: Pune University](https://unipune.ac.in/chairs/mahatma_phule_chair/mphule_webfiles/life_sketch.htm):
  - Born 11 April 1827. Married Savitribai in 1840.
  - 1848: "Started a school for girls of Shudras & Atee Shudras/ Mahar & Mangs".
  - 1849: the couple had to leave home.
  - 1851: girls' schools at Rasta Peth and Chiplunkar Wada.
  - 16 November 1852: felicitated by the Education Department.
  - 1855: night school. 1856: an attempt on his life.
  - 1868: opened his well to untouchables.
  - June 1873: *Gulamgiri*. 24 September 1873: Satyashodhak Samaj founded.
  - 19 October 1882: memorial to the Hunter Education Commission.
  - 11 May 1888: given the title "Mahatma".
  - Died 28 November 1890.
- **Savitribai Phule**: born 3 January 1831 at Naigaon, Satara district. Married at nine. Became a qualified teacher in 1847. First teacher at the girls' school at Bhide Wada, Pune (1848). She was harassed, reportedly with stones and dung thrown at her. Died 10 March 1897 [source: The Week / PTI, 3 January 2020](https://www.theweek.in/news/india/2020/01/03/savitribai-phule-the-pioneer-of-womens-education-in-india.html).
- Keer says Phule started "in 1848, the first school in India for the Untouchables" [source: Keer, p. 4](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). The Pune University timeline describes the 1848 school as for girls of Shudras and Ati-Shudras (see conflicts).
- Keer: Ramji Sakpal was "a friend and admirer of Mahatma Phooley" [source: Keer, p. 11](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- Keer: Sayajirao Gaekwad started schools for untouchables in Baroda in 1883. He had to rely on Muslim teachers because caste Hindu teachers refused [source: Keer, p. 4](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- Ambedkar dedicated *Who Were the Shudras?* (first edition 1946, Thacker) to "Mahatma Jotiba Fule (1827–1890), The Greatest Shudra of Modern India …" [source: BAWS Vol. 7, dedication](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume7.pdf#page=23) [source: BAWS Vol. 7, edition note](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume7.pdf#page=25).
- Other contemporaries (Pritchett timeline):
  - 1902: Shahu of Kolhapur reserved 50% of state posts for backward classes; in 1907 he opened two hostels for Depressed Class boys.
  - 1903: Shivram Janba Kamble convened Mahars from 51 villages at Saswad and petitioned the Governor for jobs, schools, police and army.
  - [source: Pritchett 1900s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1900s.html)
- Keer adds:
  - Kamble edited *Somavanshiya Mitra* and sent a 1910 memorandum.
  - V. R. Shinde founded the Depressed Classes Mission in 1906.
  - [source: Keer, p. 21](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer)
- The Columbia CCNMTL timeline dates a second Kamble conference at Jejuri to 1910 [source: Columbia CCNMTL timeline](https://ccnmtl.columbia.edu/projects/mmt/ambedkar/web/timeline_files/timeline_content03.html).

## 5. The Sakpal family

- **Origin.** "Our family came originally from Dapoli Taluka of the Ratnagiri District … my fore-fathers had left their hereditary occupation for service in the Army of the Company. My father … was a Subhedar when he retired." [source: BAWS Vol. 12, p. 665](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume12.pdf#page=686)
- **Ancestral village.**
  - Keer: "Ambavade, five miles off Mandangad". The family kept the palanquin of the village goddess [source: Keer, p. 8](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
  - Gopal: the correct name is "Ambadave", and "Both Khairmode and Keer incorrectly named the village 'Ambavade'". He places it in "Mangaon taluka, about 60 kilometres from the taluka headquarters", with an Ambedkar memorial hall and Ashoka pillar [source: Ashok Gopal, Scroll](https://scroll.in/article/1047284/how-ramji-sakpal-struggled-for-the-education-that-gave-us-his-son-bhimrao-ambedkar).
  - Ratnagiri district: "Ambadwe", Mandangad taluka, about 13 km from Mandangad. The original house is now a memorial, and a national monument is being developed [source: Ratnagiri District](https://ratnagiri.gov.in/?p=2805).
  - 1915 Special Branch record: "native of Ambed, Taluka Dapoli, District Ratnagiri" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 472](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=497).
  - Loksatta (14 April 2016): MP Amar Sable, who adopted the village under the MPs' model-village scheme, says "Ambadave" is the correct name [source: Loksatta (Marathi)](https://www.loksatta.com/pune-news/about-name-of-ambadve-village-1227278/).
  - President Ram Nath Kovind visited Ambadawe on 12 February 2022. The village is called "Sphurti-Bhoomi". DICCI and KVIC helped after Cyclone Nisarga (June 2020) [source: The Tribune, 13 February 2022](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/nation/should-mark-nov-7-as-students-day-president-in-br-ambedkars-ancestral-village-369331/).
  - Wikipedia (pointer): 2011 population 240, PIN 402307 [source: Wikipedia, Ambadawe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambadawe).
- **Grandfather Maloji Sakpal.** A retired military man. "Of his children two survived": Ramji and a daughter, Mira [source: Keer, p. 8](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). Ambedkar calls him a Ramanandi (follower of the Ramananda sect) [source: unpublished preface](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/ambedkar_buddha/00_pref_unpub.html). Wikipedia, citing a 2024 Marathi book by Dnyanraj Gaikwad Rajvansh, says Maloji had four children (three sons and Mirabai) [source: Wikipedia, Ramji Sakpal (pointer)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramji_Sakpal).
- **Father Ramji Maloji Sakpal.**
  - Recruited into the 106 Sappers and Miners of the Bombay Army around 1866, at about 18. Recorded as "Ramnak Malnak" (Basham 1986: 297). Described as a stocky sharpshooter with sober habits.
  - Married Bhimabai in 1867 at Murbad; her father, Dharmaji Murbadkar, was his senior in the regiment.
  - Khairmode places him in Rawalpindi in 1879, and at Mhow from 1888 until he retired from the 107th Pioneers as a subhedar in 1893.
  - Trained at a military school in Pune and became headmaster of the regimental school at Mhow.
  - [source: Ashok Gopal, Scroll](https://scroll.in/article/1047284/how-ramji-sakpal-struggled-for-the-education-that-gave-us-his-son-bhimrao-ambedkar)
- **Ramji, according to Keer.**
  - A Normal School-trained teacher. Education was compulsory for military families.
  - Headmaster of the military school for 14 years. "Subhedar-Major in the 2nd Grenadiers", known as "Subhedar Ramji".
  - A teetotaller who "never touched meat"; played cricket and football in his youth; kept a key to Gokhale's *Arithmetic*.
  - In old age he petitioned the Governor of Bombay to open new Development-scheme buildings to untouchables.
  - [source: Keer, pp. 10–12](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer)
- The 1943 profile says Ramji "had once been a teacher in an Army school" [source: BAWS Vol. 10, p. 3](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=20).
- **Religion.** Ramji was "a Kabirpanthi, though his father was Ramanandi". He did not believe in idol worship but performed Ganapati puja "for our sake". He made the boys read the Mahabharata and Ramayana aloud each night to their sisters and visitors, to remove an "inferiority complex" (citing Drona, Karna and Valmiki). Ambedkar calls him "a Roman Patriarch" [source: unpublished preface (6 April 1956)](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/ambedkar_buddha/00_pref_unpub.html). Keer says the family belonged to the Kabir school because Kabir condemned caste, and quotes "Jat pant puchhe na koi, Har ko bhaje so Har ka hoi" (OCR-garbled; check a print copy) [source: Keer, pp. 8–9](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). Ramji also sang Moropant, Mukteshwar and Tukaram [source: Keer, p. 11](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- **Mother Bhimabai.** From the Murbadkars, "a rich family" of Murbad, Thana district. Her father and six uncles were Subhedar Majors, also of the Kabir cult [source: Keer, pp. 9–10](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). Wikipedia names her father as Laxman Murbadkar; Gopal as Dharmaji Murbadkar.
- **Children.** 14 in all. Seven are known: Balaram, Ganga, Ramabai, Anandrao, Manjula, Tulasa and "Bhiva" (Khairmode I:28) [source: Pritchett 1890s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1890s.html). At Bhimabai's death, three sons (Balaram, Anandrao, Bhimrao) and two daughters (Manjula, Tulasa) survived [source: Pritchett 1890s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1890s.html) [source: Keer, p. 10](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). The eldest sister had died by about 1901, leaving two sons [source: BAWS Vol. 12, p. 665](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume12.pdf#page=686). Ambedkar humorously called himself "Chaudave Ratna" (the 14th jewel) [source: Keer, p. 10](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). Balaram did not go to high school, and Anandrao dropped out to support the family [source: Gopal](https://scroll.in/article/1047284/how-ramji-sakpal-struggled-for-the-education-that-gave-us-his-son-bhimrao-ambedkar).
- **Aunt Mira / Mirabai.** Ramji's sister. "the kindest soul I know … somewhat of a dwarf and had some trouble with her legs" [source: BAWS Vol. 12, p. 665](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume12.pdf#page=686). Keer: "smart, short and hunchbacked" [source: Keer, p. 10](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). She hid Bhiva from beatings [source: Khairmode via Pritchett](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/khairmode_shukla.html).
- **Birth legend (Keer).** A relative of Ramji's who had become a sanyasi (holy man) passed through Mhow with other ascetics and blessed Ramji: a son would be born who would leave his mark on history [source: Keer, p. 9](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). This is family tradition, not verifiable.

## 6. Childhood, 1891–1904

- **Birth.** 14 April 1891 at Mhow, the "British-founded town … an important military center near Indore". The fourteenth and last child. Pritchett names him "Bhimrao Ramji Ambavadekar" at birth [source: Pritchett 1890s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1890s.html). Keer gives the same date [source: Keer, p. 9](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- **Birth details (Gopal, citing BAWS 18-3, the Marathi volume, pp. 399–400).** Born at noon, a large baby, under the *mool nakshatra*. In 1948 he told Sharda Kabir that astrologers gave him different dates. Some official documents found by Vijay Surwade show 1892 or 1893. Khairmode found 14 April 1891 in school and college records (K 1: 50). Mhow was renamed Dr Ambedkar Nagar in 2003. The idea that "Mhow" stands for "military headquarters of war" has no basis [source: Gopal](https://scroll.in/article/1047284/how-ramji-sakpal-struggled-for-the-education-that-gave-us-his-son-bhimrao-ambedkar).
- **1943 profile.** "He was born in 1893* at Mhow". The editor's footnote gives 14 April 1891 "as per school register at Satara" [source: BAWS Vol. 10, p. 4](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=21).
- **Mhow memorial (Bhim Janmabhoomi).**
  - Indore district: first inaugurated 14 April 1991 (100th birth anniversary) by Chief Minister Sunder Lal Patwa, and again on 14 April 2008. Architect E. D. Nimgade; about 4.52 acres [source: Indore District](https://indore.nic.in/?p=24198).
  - Gopal: efforts began in the early 1970s; the stupa memorial was inaugurated by L. K. Advani on 14 April 2008 [source: Gopal](https://scroll.in/article/1047284/how-ramji-sakpal-struggled-for-the-education-that-gave-us-his-son-bhimrao-ambedkar).
- **Retirement and Dapoli.**
  - Gopal: retired 1893; settled in the pensioners' colony at Dapoli, about 50 km from Ambadave, where an East India Company camp had been [source: Gopal](https://scroll.in/article/1047284/how-ramji-sakpal-struggled-for-the-education-that-gave-us-his-son-bhimrao-ambedkar).
  - Pritchett: retired 1894; settled in Dapoli 1894–96 [source: Pritchett 1890s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1890s.html).
  - Keer: Bhim was "hardly two years old" at the retirement and was put to school at Dapoli at five with his elder brother [source: Keer, p. 10](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- **Dapoli school (Selden, *New York Times*, 30 November 1932, based on an interview).** Untouchable children were kept in a separate room and could not drink from the school water supply. The teacher never entered their room; the six boys put their slates on the floor and stood back. He was "then 6 years old" and "the only one of the group who got beyond the first school" [source: Pritchett 1890s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1890s.html).
- **Satara.**
  - The family moved there in 1896; Ramji took a Public Works Department job "in Goregaon" [source: Pritchett 1890s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1890s.html).
  - Ambedkar: "The family left Dapoli for Satara where we lived till 1904" [source: BAWS Vol. 12, p. 665](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume12.pdf#page=686).
  - Keer's odd phrasing: Ramji "moved to Bombay and secured a job in the military quarters at Satara" [source: Keer, p. 10](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- **Mother's death.**
  - Pritchett: 1896 [source](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1890s.html).
  - Forward Press: 1897, in Satara [source](https://www.forwardpress.in/2019/10/life-and-times-of-dr-b-r-ambedkar/).
  - Keer: soon after arriving at Satara, when Bhim was "about six"; her samadhi (memorial) is at Satara [source: Keer, p. 10](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
  - Ambedkar: his mother "died in my childhood, leaving me to the care of my auntie" [source: unpublished preface](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/ambedkar_buddha/00_pref_unpub.html).
- **Father's second marriage.** Pritchett: 1898 [source: Pritchett 1900s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1900s.html). Keer: "Some time before" 1901; Ramji had meant not to remarry; Bhim "hated her for wearing his mother's ornaments" [source: Keer, p. 15](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). The stepmother's name is given as Jijabai only by Wikipedia (unverified).
- **Games and character (Khairmode I:42–43, 61).** Kho-kho, viti-dandu, chendu-lagorya, football, cricket. Bhimrao never admitted defeat and was "number one" at making a ruckus [source: Khairmode via Pritchett](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/khairmode_shukla.html).
- **Handwriting (Khairmode I:63).** Ramji's English, Marathi and Modi handwriting was fine. He beat Bhiva with a tamarind cane to make him practise, so "Bhiva's English handwriting had an ideal form" [source: Pritchett 1890s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1890s.html).
- **Keer's other Satara anecdotes.**
  - Teacher Pendse (a Brahmin) sent his son to take a rain-soaked Bhim home for a bath [source: Keer, pp. 13–14](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
  - Bhim was beaten "black and blue" for secretly drinking at a public water-course [source: Keer, p. 13](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
  - He grew plants, kept goats, and once did porter's work at Satara station: "My aunt terribly felt humiliated" [source: Keer, p. 15](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
  - The plan to become a "winding boy" in a Bombay mill; four nights trying to take his aunt's purse; half an anna; the decision to study (Keer quotes Ambedkar's speech, *Bombay Sentinel*, 20 January 1942; the OCR reads "1012") [source: Keer, pp. 15–16](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- **Satara schools.**
  - Khairmode: first a Marathi school (the "Camp School Satara") under "Ambedkar master". Enrolled in the English first standard of Satara High School on **7 November 1900**. Anandrao had entered the high school in 1898 [source: Khairmode via Pritchett](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/khairmode_shukla.html).
  - Pritchett: "Government Middle School at Satara" (1900); Selden: "the only untouchable pupil … always on the floor by himself in a remote corner. None could play with him or speak to him" [source: Pritchett 1900s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1900s.html).
  - Gopal: the Satara High School register entry reads "Bhiva Ramji Ambedkar" (photo from Vijay Surwade's collection) [source: Gopal](https://scroll.in/article/1047284/how-ramji-sakpal-struggled-for-the-education-that-gave-us-his-son-bhimrao-ambedkar).
  - Maharashtra marks 7 November as Students' Day. President Kovind proposed it nationally in 2022 [source: The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/nation/should-mark-nov-7-as-students-day-president-in-br-ambedkars-ancestral-village-369331/). Search snippets (not opened) say a government resolution was issued on 27 October 2017 and that the school is now Pratapsinh High School; both are unverified.
- **Ranade holiday.** "I was a student in the first standard in the Satara High School. On the 16th January 1901 the High School was closed … because Ranade was dead. I was then about 9 years old." [source: BAWS Vol. 1, p. 211](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=226). Keer instead says "reading in standard II" [source: Keer, p. 15](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- **Discrimination at school and home (Waiting for a Visa).**
  - Sat in a corner by himself, not by rank. Had a separate gunny cloth that the school cleaner would not touch, which he carried home daily [source: BAWS Vol. 12, p. 670](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume12.pdf#page=691).
  - "no peon, no water" [source: BAWS Vol. 12, p. 671](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume12.pdf#page=692).
  - His sisters washed the clothes, and his elder sister cut the boys' hair, because no washerman or barber would serve them [source: BAWS Vol. 12, p. 671](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume12.pdf#page=692).
  - Keer: teachers would not touch the brothers' notebooks or ask them questions; when thirsty, someone poured water into their upturned mouths "as if through a funnel" [source: Keer, pp. 14–15](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
  - The 1943 profile repeats the gunny cloth, tap, dhobi and haircut details [source: BAWS Vol. 10, p. 4](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=21).
- **The Koregaon journey (Waiting for a Visa, Part One).**
  - Written c. 1935–36, judging from internal evidence [source: Pritchett's introduction](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/txt_ambedkar_waiting.html). Manuscript held by the People's Education Society and published as a booklet on 19 March 1990 [source: BAWS Vol. 12, p. 661](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume12.pdf#page=682).
  - In "about 1901", with his mother dead, his father was a cashier at Koregaon, Khatav taluka, Satara district, where the government was excavating a tank for famine relief.
  - The household had Bhim, his elder brother, two sons of his dead eldest sister, and their aunt. They lived on pulav (rice and mutton) [source: BAWS Vol. 12, p. 665](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume12.pdf#page=686).
  - None had seen a train. Bhim, his brother and one nephew went (the text later says "We four children"). They had new English-style shirts, bejewelled caps and silk-bordered dhotis.
  - A tonga took them to the station 10 miles away; two annas pocket money each; lemonade; detrained at Masur about 5 pm. The text spells the destination "Goregaon" on this page [source: BAWS Vol. 12, p. 666](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume12.pdf#page=687).
  - The station-master took them for Brahmin children until Bhim said they were Mahars. No cartman would take them, even at double fare. One agreed if the boys drove and he walked; they set off about 6:30 pm, told the trip would take three hours [source: BAWS Vol. 12, p. 667](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume12.pdf#page=688).
  - The river pool was full of mud, urine and cattle dung, so they couldn't eat properly. After 4–5 miles the cartman jumped in and took the reins [source: BAWS Vol. 12, p. 668](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume12.pdf#page=689).
  - About 10 pm they cried, fearing murder (they wore gold ornaments). They reached the toll-collector's hut at midnight. Bhim, on the cartman's advice, claimed to be a Muslim and spoke Urdu, but was refused water [source: BAWS Vol. 12, p. 669](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume12.pdf#page=690).
  - They kept watch two at a time, left at 8 am and reached Koregaon at 11. The father's servant had never passed on their letter. "I was a boy of nine when it happened." [source: BAWS Vol. 12, p. 670](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume12.pdf#page=691).
  - "a shock such as I never received before" [source: BAWS Vol. 12, p. 671](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume12.pdf#page=692).
  - Keer's version: boarded at "Padali" station; father worked at "Goregaon"; the cartman "threw them out on the road"; the elder brother drove; travelled thirsty "from the evening till midnight". Keer cites Ambedkar's speech in *Janata*, 20 November 1937 [source: Keer, pp. 12–13](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
  - The 1943 profile: father at "some town off the railway line"; paid double to drive a cart themselves; meal spoilt for lack of water [source: BAWS Vol. 10, p. 4](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=21).
- **The surname.**
  - Keer: another Brahmin teacher "in the High School", surname Ambedkar, irregular but humane. He dropped part of his meal into Bhim's hands at recess and changed the surname "from Ambavadekar to his own surname Ambedkar in the school records". He worked as an accountant in a tobacco shop during school hours, left the class to a grown-up Muslim student, and covertly showed answers at inspections. He sent a congratulatory letter before the first Round Table Conference [source: Keer, p. 14](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
  - Khairmode (I:49, 52–53): Ambedkar's words, "We had a Brahmin master by the name 'Ambedkar' … he would drop the bread and vegetables into my hands from above". The teacher left the class to a boy named Rahimtulla and was Bhim's first teacher.
  - Khairmode continues: Ambedkar praised him in *Navayuga*, 13 April 1947, and kept his loving letter. The teacher visited in 1927 at the office behind Damodar Hall; Ambedkar stood, said "Master" and bowed, had a suit made for him, and gave 25 paise as gurudakshina (Khairmode says he witnessed this) [source: Khairmode via Pritchett](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/khairmode_shukla.html).
  - Wikipedia names the teacher "Krishnaji Keshav Ambedkar" and says the father registered the name as "Ambadawekar". Its Ambadawe article claims the teacher came from the village itself ("citation needed"). These rest on Marathi press (Divya Marathi 2016, Loksatta 2014), which we could not open [source: Wikipedia, B. R. Ambedkar (pointer)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar) [source: Wikipedia, Ambadawe (pointer)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambadawe).
  - Pritchett calls the original form "Ambavadekar" [source: Pritchett 1890s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1890s.html).

## 7. School and college, 1904–1913

- **Bombay homes.**
  - Dabak Chawl, Lower Parel, one room in a labour area. His married sisters also lived in Bombay. Father's pension: Rs 50 a month. He borrowed from his daughters and pawned their ornaments to buy books. He woke Bhim at 2 am to study by a chimney-less kerosene lamp, with a grindstone by Bhim's head and a she-goat at his feet [source: Keer, pp. 16–18](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). Pritchett places the move in 1901 [source: Pritchett 1900s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1900s.html).
  - Later: Improvement Trust Chawl No. 1, Parel, second floor, rooms 50 (study and parlour) and 51 [source: Keer, p. 22](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
  - In 1953 Ambedkar told the Council of States (Rajya Sabha) that he had "all my life lived in Parel in a chawl of the Improvement Trust, Room No. 50". He paid Rs 3-8-0 rent at first and still kept the room "as an emergency measure" (Estate Duty Bill debate, 18 September 1953) [source: BAWS Vol. 15, pp. 872–873](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume15.pdf#page=891).
- **Home tutoring.** Howard's English Reader and Tarkhadkar's three translation books, which increased his vocabulary [source: Keer, p. 16](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
- **Schools.**
  - Maratha High School first, then "After a few months" Elphinstone High School [source: Keer, pp. 16–17](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
  - Forward Press: joined Elphinstone High School in 1904 [source](https://www.forwardpress.in/2019/10/life-and-times-of-dr-b-r-ambedkar/).
  - Selden: entered at about 13, the only untouchable, on a back bench [source: Pritchett 1900s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1900s.html).
  - Gopal's caption: the Elphinstone High School building in Byculla now houses the Dr Babasaheb Ambedkar Central Railway Hospital [source: Gopal](https://scroll.in/article/1047284/how-ramji-sakpal-struggled-for-the-education-that-gave-us-his-son-bhimrao-ambedkar).
- **Incidents.**
  - The tiffin boxes behind the blackboard. A teacher told him study was useless for him, and he told the teacher to mind his own business. A workman brought his lunch to school. He carried relatives' tiffins to the mills and captained cricket and football teams [source: Keer, pp. 17–18](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
  - Khairmode: he organised neighbourhood hockey, football and cricket teams, played other castes' teams and explained batting [source: Khairmode via Pritchett](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/khairmode_shukla.html).
- **Sanskrit refused.** Both brothers were barred from Sanskrit as a second language "During his High School days" and "compelled to take Persian". Keer compares the case of M. R. Jayakar. Ambedkar later learned Sanskrit "partly by himself and sometimes with the help of some pandits" [source: Keer, pp. 18–19](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). We found no early source placing this at Elphinstone College.
- **Keluskar.**
  - Krishnaji Arjun "Dada" Keluskar. Keer calls him "assistant teacher at Wilson High School"; they met in a garden where both read in the evenings [source: Keer, pp. 19–20](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
  - Khairmode calls him "Wilson School's headmaster", a non-Brahmin scholar who read at Charni Road garden at 5 pm, taught Bhiva to read methodically and arranged the meeting with Sayajirao [source: Khairmode via Pritchett](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/khairmode_shukla.html).
  - Christopher Queen: "principal of nearby Wilson High School"; "Churni Road Garden" [source: Outlook, 5 February 2022](https://www.outlookindia.com/opinion/lit-again-paths-news-281946).
  - Vasant Moon: "former Principal, Wilson High School" [source: BAWS Vol. 11, p. ix](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume11.pdf#page=628).
  - Scott Stroud: met him around 1904–05. Ambedkar's annotated copy of Dewey and Tufts' *Ethics* (1908) is signed "K. A. Keluskar". Keluskar helped through the 1920s–30s and died in 1934 [source: Forward Press, 5 January 2018](https://www.forwardpress.in/2018/01/ambedkars-pragmatism-drew-heavily-on-the-1908-ethics/) [source: Stroud, CASTE (2024)](https://journals.library.brandeis.edu/index.php/caste/article/download/694/1810).
  - In 1930 Keluskar spoke at a meeting with Ambedkar [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part III, p. 63](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_III.pdf#page=92), and later appealed for funds for a Depressed Classes college [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part II, p. 444](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=469).
- **Matriculation.**
  - Keer: passed in 1907 from Elphinstone High School with 282 out of 750 (two separate OCR copies agree), best in Persian [source: Keer, p. 19](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
  - Forward Press: 382 out of 750 [source](https://www.forwardpress.in/2019/10/life-and-times-of-dr-b-r-ambedkar/).
  - Pritchett: 1907, average marks, best subject Persian [source](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1900s.html).
  - Moon: Matriculation of the University of Bombay, 1907 [source: BAWS Vol. 11, p. ix](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume11.pdf#page=628).
  - Gujarat government life sketch: 1908 [source](https://sje.gujarat.gov.in/dscw/downloads/life_sketch_eng.pdf).
  - PARI implies matriculation before the 1906 marriage [source](https://ruralindiaonline.org/en/articles/remembering-bhimrao-and-ramabai-ambedkar).
- **Felicitation and the Buddha book.**
  - Ambedkar's preface: "The year I passed the English Fourth Standard Examination". He was "the first boy in my community to reach this stage". His father refused permission; Dada Keluskar, "a personal friend of my father", intervened and presided, then gave him "a copy of his book on the life of the Buddha, which he had written for the Baroda Sayajirao Oriental Series". This led to a quarrel with his father over the epics, and "This is the origin of my interest in the Buddha and His Dhamma" [source: unpublished preface, 6 April 1956](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/ambedkar_buddha/00_pref_unpub.html).
  - Keer: after the 1907 matriculation, a meeting presided over by S. K. Bole; Keluskar present, presented *Life of Gautama Buddha* [source: Keer, pp. 19–20](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
  - Moon: Keluskar's Marathi biography of the Buddha was published in 1898 and presented "on his success in the Matriculation examination … in 1907". It mentions the Rohini river dispute between the Sakyas and Koliyas, which Ambedkar later used [source: BAWS Vol. 11, p. ix](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume11.pdf#page=628).
  - Christopher Queen and Forward Press (Stroud) also place the gift in 1907 [source: Outlook](https://www.outlookindia.com/opinion/lit-again-paths-news-281946) [source: Forward Press](https://www.forwardpress.in/2018/01/ambedkars-pragmatism-drew-heavily-on-the-1908-ethics/).
- **Marriage.**
  - Pritchett (Kadam p. 70): 1906, Ramabai, nine-year-old daughter of Bhiku Dhutre of Wanand, near Dapoli. "Some accounts" link her to Gopal Baba Walangkar (Zelliot, private communication) [source: Pritchett 1900s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1900s.html).
  - Keer: after matriculation. Ramji had rejected some girls and paid a Rs 5 fine to the caste panchayat for breaking one betrothal. The wedding was at night in the open shed of the Byculla Market, with gutters underfoot, and the families left before the fisherwomen arrived. Bhimrao "hardly seventeen", Rami nine, renamed Ramabai; second daughter of the late Bhiku Walangkar, a porter at Dapoli [source: Keer, pp. 20–21](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
  - PARI (Namita Waikar, 29 April 2022): born Rami Valangkar of Vanandgaon; father a porter; orphaned, she lived with a paternal uncle in a Byculla vegetable-market chawl; a younger brother and sister; married at night in 1906; Ramji chose the bride; Bhimrao 14; five children, four died young [source: PARI](https://ruralindiaonline.org/en/articles/remembering-bhimrao-and-ramabai-ambedkar).
  - Forward Press: 1907, after matriculation, Rami daughter of Bhiku Walangkar, vegetable market in Byculla. Its footnote cites Raj Kumar's *Encyclopaedia of Dr B. R. Ambedkar* for 1906 [source](https://www.forwardpress.in/2019/10/life-and-times-of-dr-b-r-ambedkar/).
  - Khairmode (I:273–276, on later years): Ramabai's first son Yashwant suffered from rheumatism from childhood; "the two children after him died early on"; Bhimrao called her "Ramu" [source: Khairmode via Pritchett](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/khairmode_shukla.html).
- **Elphinstone College.**
  - Enrolled 3 January 1908 (Khairmode I:66–67). Professors: Oswald Muller, Principal Covernton, George Anderson [source: Khairmode via Pritchett](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/khairmode_shukla.html).
  - Pritchett: 1908, stipend Rs 25 a month from Sayajirao, arranged by Keluskar [source](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1900s.html).
  - Columbia CCNMTL timeline: meets Sayajirao and receives the scholarship in **1910** [source](https://ccnmtl.columbia.edu/projects/mmt/ambedkar/web/timeline_files/timeline_content03.html).
  - Keer: lost a year to ill health. After passing the Inter Arts exam his father ran out of funds. Keluskar reminded Sayajirao of his recent Town Hall promise; after an audience, Rs 25 a month. Prof. Muller lent books and clothes. The Brahmin college "hotelkeeper" refused him tea or water [source: Keer, pp. 21–22](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
  - 1943 profile: "Half way through his university course, his father ran out of funds and a friend took the young undergraduate to the Gaekwar of Baroda" [source: BAWS Vol. 10, p. 4](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=21).
  - Khairmode I:68: learned tabla (Anandrao played well); produced the Sharada play at Gokulashtami; wrote *The Good Girl*, based on *King Lear*; collected money for kirtans and mocked performers who got the Puranic stories wrong [source: Khairmode via Pritchett](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/khairmode_shukla.html).
- **Sayajirao Gaekwad III.** Born 11 March 1863 at Kavlana. Became ruler of Baroda at 13. Tutored by F. A. H. Eliot. Founded many schools; held inter-caste dinners; ran employment schemes and scholarships for lower castes. Visited the US in 1906 and 1910 [source: Stroud, CASTE (2024)](https://journals.library.brandeis.edu/index.php/caste/article/download/694/1810).
- **BA.**
  - 1912 [source: Keer, p. 22](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer). Keer adds that A. B. Gajendragadkar topped the list.
  - Pritchett (Kadam p. 71): "special subjects: Economics and Politics" [source](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).
  - Forward Press: papers in Persian and English, 449 out of 1,000 [source](https://www.forwardpress.in/2019/10/life-and-times-of-dr-b-r-ambedkar/).
  - Gujarat government: Political Science and Economics, 1912 [source](https://sje.gujarat.gov.in/dscw/downloads/life_sketch_eng.pdf).
  - 1915 Special Branch: "a graduate of the Bombay University having passed his B. A. Examination from the Elphinstone College" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 472](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=497).
- **Yashwant.** Born December 1912 [source: Forward Press](https://www.forwardpress.in/2019/10/life-and-times-of-dr-b-r-ambedkar/); 1912 [source: Pritchett](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html). Wikipedia gives 12 December 1912 (unverified).
- **Baroda service.**
  - Keer: took service in Baroda "despite his father's repeated disapprovals"; "appointed to the post of a lieutenant in the Baroda State Forces"; served "about fifteen days in January 1913" (the OCR misreads "1918") [source: Keer, p. 24](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
  - Forward Press: "Joins Baroda State Force as a lieutenant" in 1913 [source](https://www.forwardpress.in/2019/10/life-and-times-of-dr-b-r-ambedkar/).
  - 1943 profile: after graduating he went to thank the Gaekwar, was offered study overseas, and "During the months of waiting before sailing for America, the Gaekwar advised Ambedkar to try the profession of his fore-fathers and made him a Lieutenant in the Baroda State Forces" [source: BAWS Vol. 10, pp. 4–5](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=22).
  - Assistant Resident, Baroda, January 1915: "He figures in the Baroda Service List as a Military Probationer, but has done no military training and was apparently appointed in order to draw pay" [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 472](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=497).
- **Father's death.** Telegram; got down at Surat to buy sweets and missed the train; arrived the next day; Ramji moved his hand over his son's back and died. "Jt [It] was February 2, 1913, the saddest day in Bhimrao Ambedkar's life." "Subhedar Ramji Maloji" died "poor in wealth, for he had run into debt" [source: Keer, pp. 24–25](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer) [source: Pritchett 1910s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).
- **Columbia arrangements (lead-in to chapter 3).**
  - Keer: in June 1913 Bhimrao told the Maharaja his story at the palace in Bombay [source: Keer, p. 25](https://archive.org/details/dr-ambedkar-life-and-mission-dhananjay-keer).
  - The Memorandum of Agreement of 4 June 1913 (a true copy is in the Khairmode Papers, University of Mumbai Archives) required him "to be trained up in finance and sociology in the Columbia University … to obtain the Master's degree in economics, finance and sociology", supervised by H. C. Bumpus. Vijay Surwade found he was first to be sent to study pedagogy [source: Stroud, CASTE (2024)](https://journals.library.brandeis.edu/index.php/caste/article/download/694/1810).
  - The agreement required ten years of service to Baroda [source: Forward Press note 2](https://www.forwardpress.in/2019/10/life-and-times-of-dr-b-r-ambedkar/).
  - Scholarship £11.50 a month from 15 June 1913 to 14 June 1916 [source: The Beacon (Hemant Devasthali, 30 December 2018)](https://thebeacon.in/?p=3103); three years (Kadam p. 72) [source: Pritchett 1910s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).
  - Left Bombay on the S.S. Sardegna on 15 June 1913 [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part I, p. 472](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=497). Arrived in New York in the third week of July [source: Pritchett 1910s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).

## 8. Ambedkar's later reflections on these years

- "I was a boy of nine when it happened. But it has left an indelible impression on my mind." [source: BAWS Vol. 12, p. 670](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume12.pdf#page=691)
- The father's argument for the epics: "See Drona and Karna—they were small men, but to what heights they rose! Look at Valmiki—he was a Koli, but he became the author of [the] Ramayana." [source: unpublished preface](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/ambedkar_buddha/00_pref_unpub.html)
- Keluskar's book: "I read the book with great interest, and was greatly impressed and moved by it." [source: unpublished preface](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/ambedkar_buddha/00_pref_unpub.html)
- Gopal quotes Ambedkar's March 1927 Mahad speech (BAWS 18-1: 35–36, Marathi volume, Gopal's translation): army service let Mahar subhedars receive salutes from Maratha soldiers [source: Gopal](https://scroll.in/article/1047284/how-ramji-sakpal-struggled-for-the-education-that-gave-us-his-son-bhimrao-ambedkar). This is not used in the chapters because it is a translation and we did not check the Marathi original.

## 10. Conflicts between sources (master list)

1. **Birth year.** 1891 (school register; most sources) vs 1892/1893 (some documents, per Surwade via Gopal); "1893" in the 1943 *Indian Information* profile [source: BAWS 10 p. 4](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=21).
2. **Name at birth / school name.** "Ambavadekar" (Keer, Pritchett) vs "Ambadawekar" (Wikipedia); "Bhiva Ramji Ambedkar" in the Satara High School register (Gopal).
3. **Village name and taluka.** Ambavade (Keer/Khairmode) vs Ambadave/Ambadawe/Ambadwe (Gopal, the government, Loksatta). Dapoli taluka (Ambedkar; the 1915 police record "Ambed, Taluka Dapoli") vs Mandangad taluka (Ratnagiri district today) vs "Mangaon taluka" (Gopal; probably an error, since Mangaon is not in Ratnagiri district — unverified). Distance from Mandangad: 5 miles (Keer) vs 13 km (district) vs about 20 km (Wikipedia Mandangad).
4. **Ramji's birth year.** About 1848 (Gopal: about 18 in 1866) vs "1838–1913" in the Commons file title.
5. **Ramji's rank and unit.** Subhedar (Ambedkar; Gopal: 107th Pioneers) vs Subhedar-Major, 2nd Grenadiers (Keer). Recruited into the 106 Sappers and Miners (Gopal).
6. **Ramji's retirement.** 1893 (Gopal/Khairmode; Keer "hardly two") vs 1894 (Pritchett; Gujarat government).
7. **Mahar recruitment ban.** 1892 (Ambedkar's Ranade speech; Keer; Constable; the 1943 profile) vs "about 1890" (Pax Britannica).
8. **First Mahar army petition.** 1894 (Pritchett/Zelliot) vs April 1895 (Gopal).
9. **Koregaon obelisk.** Built the year after the battle, 60 ft (Kumbhojkar, citing Delamain) vs inscription dated 1822, 65 ft (Gazetteer 1885) vs 1821 (Wikipedia Mahar Regiment, unverified). 22 Mahar names (Gopal; Longer) vs 23 with "-nac" (a secondary site quoting Kumbhojkar; unverified).
10. **Koregaon battle numbers.** 834 defenders (Gazetteer) vs "800" (Gazetteer headline) vs about 850 (Outlook). Peshwa's force 25,000 horse (Gazetteer detail) vs 30,000 (Gazetteer headline) vs 28,000 (Wikipedia). Outcome: Company "triumph" (inscription, Hislop) vs inconclusive (Kumbhojkar).
11. **Phule's 1848 school.** "first school in India for the Untouchables" (Keer) vs a school for girls of Shudras and Ati-Shudras (Pune University) vs a girls' school at Bhide Wada (The Week).
12. **Mother's death.** 1896 (Pritchett) vs 1897 (Forward Press) vs "about six" (Keer).
13. **Father's remarriage.** 1898 (Pritchett) vs "some time before" 1901 (Keer).
14. **Move to Bombay.** 1904 (Ambedkar: Satara "till 1904"; Forward Press) vs 1901 (Pritchett) vs 1897 (Wikipedia) vs "four years" after 1894, i.e. about 1898 (Gujarat government).
15. **The Koregaon journey.** "Koregaon" and "Goregaon" both appear in BAWS. Keer: boarded at Padali, thrown out of the cart, the brother drove. Ambedkar: "One of us took the reins". Three children set out, but the text says "We four children". The year is "about 1901", yet he was "a boy of nine" (he turned ten in April 1901).
16. **Ranade holiday.** First standard (Ambedkar) vs standard II (Keer).
17. **Surname teacher.** In the High School (Keer) vs his first teacher in the Marathi school (Khairmode). Full name Krishnaji Keshav Ambedkar (Wikipedia/Marathi press; unverified); a "Mahadev Ambedkar" variant appears in some online texts (search snippet only).
18. **Keluskar's post.** Assistant teacher (Keer) vs headmaster/principal (Khairmode, Moon, Queen).
19. **The exam celebrated.** English Fourth Standard (Ambedkar's preface) vs Matriculation 1907 (Keer, Moon, Queen, Stroud).
20. **Who presided.** Keluskar (Ambedkar) vs S. K. Bole (Keer).
21. **Matriculation year.** 1907 (Keer, Pritchett, Moon, Forward Press) vs 1908 (Gujarat government).
22. **Matriculation marks.** 282/750 (Keer) vs 382/750 (Forward Press).
23. **Marriage year.** 1906 (Kadam/Pritchett, PARI, Raj Kumar via Forward Press) vs after the 1907 matriculation (Keer, Forward Press main entry). Bhimrao's age: 14 (PARI), about 15 (Wikipedia), "hardly seventeen" (Keer).
24. **Ramabai's father.** Bhiku Dhutre (Kadam/Pritchett) vs Bhiku Walangkar (Keer, Forward Press, PARI "Rami Valangkar"). Her village: Wanand / Vanandgaon.
25. **When the Baroda scholarship began.** 1908 (Pritchett) vs 1910 (CCNMTL) vs after Inter Arts (Keer) vs halfway through the course (1943 profile).
26. **BA subjects.** Economics and Politics (Kadam/Pritchett; Gujarat government) vs Persian and English papers (Forward Press). BA year: 1912 in all sources opened. A 1913 claim appears only in a search snippet from drambedkarbooks.com, which we could not open.
27. **Baroda post.** Lieutenant, January 1913, before his father's death (Keer) vs lieutenant made while waiting to sail (1943 profile) vs "Military Probationer" with no military training (1915 police report).

The BAWS preface prints "18th January 1940", but Ranade (born 1842) would have turned 101 in 1943, and the preface is dated 15 March 1943 [source: BAWS Vol. 1, pp. 207–209](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=222). - The date *Waiting for a Visa* was written (c. 1935–36 is Pritchett's inference from internal evidence).
- Whether the Commons portrait "Bhimabai Ramji Sakpal" really shows her. The file page gives no provenance.
- Keer's page numbers come from the 3rd-edition OCR on the Internet Archive. Page numbers may differ in other editions or reprints.
