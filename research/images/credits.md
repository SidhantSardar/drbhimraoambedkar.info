## How to credit images on the site

- **CC BY and CC BY-SA images must be credited.** Show the author name, the licence name with a link to the licence, and a link to the Commons file page (the `author`, `license`, `licenseUrl` and `sourceUrl` fields). Say that the image was resized/converted (for example: "resized from the original"). For CC BY-SA, our resized copies stay under the same CC BY-SA licence.
- **GODL-India images** (Government Open Data License – India, [licence text](https://data.gov.in/government-open-data-license-india)) also require attribution to the government source, e.g. "India Post, Government of India, via Wikimedia Commons (GODL-India)".
- **Public-domain, CC0 and Flickr Commons ("no known copyright restrictions") images** do not legally require a credit, but the site should still show the source as good practice and so readers can check the original.
- A simple pattern for captions: `Photo: {author}, {license}, via Wikimedia Commons` linked to `sourceUrl`.

## Licensing notes

### Why old Indian photographs are public domain in India (PD-India)

Most historical photographs of Dr. Ambedkar on Commons are tagged with the [PD-India template](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Template:PD-India). Under the Indian Copyright Act, 1957, copyright in a **photograph** lasts 60 years counted from the beginning of the calendar year after the year it was **published** (section 25). Works of the government and of public bodies are also protected for 60 years from publication. For literary and artistic works (for example letters and manuscripts), the term is the author's life plus 60 years. So, as of 2026:

- an Indian photograph **published before 1 January 1966** is in the public domain in India;
- Dr. Ambedkar's own writings are public domain in India: he died in 1956, so the 60-year term ended and his works entered the public domain on 1 January 2017. (Jawaharlal Nehru died in 1964, so his writings became public domain in India on 1 January 2025.)

Points to keep in mind:

- The PD-India rule depends on the **publication** date, not on when the photo was taken. Many Commons files about Dr. Ambedkar have weak provenance ("Via Internet", navayan.com, newspapers' websites) and their dates are as stated by the uploader. Captions in `images.json` say "per Commons" or "undated" where this matters.
- Wikimedia Commons also asks for a file to be free in the **United States**. Many PD-India files do not carry a US status tag. Indian photographs published in 1936 or later (i.e. not yet public domain in India on 1 January 1996) could still be protected in the US under the URAA (see the [PD-India-URAA template](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Template:PD-India-URAA) on Commons). For a site run from India for an Indian audience, PD-India is the key test, but this is a small residual risk for US use.
- A few photographs in the set were probably taken outside India (Columbia University, New York, 1952; Rangoon 1954; Kathmandu 1956; Ceylon 1950). They are tagged PD-India on Commons; their status in their country of origin was not checked.
- Letters: the letter from Dr. Ambedkar to W. E. B. Du Bois (c. 1946) and the letter from Nehru to Dr. Ambedkar (1956) are public domain in India. As unpublished letters they may still be in copyright in the US (author's life + 70 years). The reply letter from W. E. B. Du Bois to Ambedkar (1946) was **not** used, because Du Bois died in 1963 and his estate's rights may still apply.
- Some old photographs were uploaded to Commons under **CC BY-SA 4.0** by the uploader as "own work" (for example the 1927 Bhima Koregaon photograph, the 1950 People's Education Society office photograph and the 1928 Simon Commission photograph). The underlying photographs are old Indian photographs and would in any case be PD-India if published before 1966. The `license` field records exactly what Commons states; the `author` field notes when the uploader is not the photographer.
- Many PD-India files were also tagged `{{Cc-zero}}` by their uploader. The underlying status is public domain in India, so we record them as "Public domain (PD-India)".

### Photos of statues, memorials and reliefs (freedom of panorama)

Many modern photos show statues and memorials. India's Copyright Act (section 52) allows photographs of sculptures and works of architecture permanently situated in public places, so the Indian statue and memorial photos are fine with the photographer's licence. Notes:

- The four relief panels (Mhow birthplace, early childhood, Kabirpanthi tradition, school education) are **modern sculptures** at the Statue of Social Justice memorial in Vijayawada, photographed by Anandajoti Bhikkhu (CC BY-SA 4.0). They are artistic depictions, not historical records, and captions say so.
- The statue at Koyasan University, Japan: Japanese copyright law allows photos of public outdoor sculptures except when the purpose is selling copies; the site is free and non-commercial, so this is fine, but do not use it on merchandise.
- The statue in Miskolc, Hungary (CC0 photo): Hungary has freedom of panorama for works permanently in public places.
- The bust at Ambedkar House, London is from a Government of India (PIB) photo released under GODL-India. UK freedom of panorama covers sculptures permanently in public places.

## Gaps: life periods and subjects with few or no good free images

- **Before birth / family origins:** no verified photographs of Ambedkar's mother Bhimabai; the image of his father Ramji Sakpal has an unknown source. Ambadave and Mhow are covered only by modern memorial photos and one c. 1900 Mhow photograph.
- **Childhood (1891–1904):** no photographs of Bhimrao as a child were found. Nothing free was found for **Satara** (the Government High School / Camp Satara) or **Dapoli**. The section relies on modern relief sculptures (Vijayawada) and memorial photos.
- **School years (1904–1913):** no photo of Elphinstone High School, of his 1906 marriage to Ramabai, or of his 1913 service in Baroda. Only two early portraits of uncertain date.
- **Columbia and London (1913–1923):** only two small portraits from Columbia and two small group photos from the LSE. No free 1910s–1920s photo of Gray's Inn; no covers of his theses (The Evolution of Provincial Finance in British India; The Problem of the Rupee).
- **1920s movement:** no image of the burning of the Manusmriti (25 December 1927) or of the Bahishkrit Hitakarini Sabha (1924). Janata and Prabuddha Bharat appear only in a montage; no free copy of an issue of Janata was found.
- **Constitution:** no free photograph of Prem Behari Narain Raizada, and no photograph of Dr. Ambedkar himself signing the Constitution. Most Constituent Assembly photos are small (under 800 px).
- **Law Minister / Hindu Code Bill (1947–1951):** few images of the Hindu Code Bill debate and nothing on his resignation in 1951.
- **Final days:** no free, politician-free photo of the building at 26 Alipur Road (the Dr. Ambedkar National Memorial) — Commons has mainly PIB photos of officials there. Nothing usable for Shantivan (Chicholi).
- **Statues:** the Hyderabad statue is officially the 125-foot Ambedkar statue; note that the "Statue of Equality" in Hyderabad is a different monument (Ramanuja). The Vijayawada statue is the Statue of Social Justice.
- **Symbols:** no free image of the Samata Sainik Dal flag was found.
- **Unsplash:** blocked, so no modern stock photos (Parliament, Nagpur street scenes, protests) from Unsplash.
