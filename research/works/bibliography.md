# Dr. Ambedkar's works – a catalogue

This catalogue brings together everything Dr. B. R. Ambedkar is known to have written or said in a recorded form: theses, books, pamphlets, papers, memoranda to commissions, speeches, articles, newspapers, letters and unpublished manuscripts. For each item it says where you can read it online, and – if there is no online copy – who holds the physical original. The same data is in machine-readable form in `src/data/works.json`, `src/data/speeches.json` and `src/data/baws-volumes.json`.

## How to use this catalogue

Most of his writings are collected in **Dr. Babasaheb Ambedkar: Writings and Speeches** (BAWS). The Government of Maharashtra began publishing this series in 1979, after setting up an advisory committee in 1976 [source: BAWS Vol. 1, 2014 reprint, Foreword by Chief Minister Sharad Pawar (Internet Archive full text)](https://archive.org/stream/HindSwaraj-Ambedkar-01/Ambedkar_Volume_01_djvu.txt). The Dr. Ambedkar Foundation (Government of India) reprinted the English series in 2014, 2019 and 2020 [source: BAWS Vol. 1, imprint page](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=5).

- **English series:** 17 volumes in 20 books (Vols. 14 and 17 are in parts). The Ministry of External Affairs hosts them all as free PDFs [source: Ministry of External Affairs – Books & Writings of Ambedkar](https://www.mea.gov.in/books-writings-of-ambedkar.htm).
- **Marathi volumes 18–22:** his Marathi speeches (Vol. 18, three parts, 2002), his journalism (Vols. 19 and 20, 2005), correspondence (Vol. 21) and a photo-biography (Vol. 22) [source: University of Wisconsin–Madison catalogue record for the series](https://search.library.wisc.edu/catalog/999513632302121) [source: Internet Archive – Marathi Vol. 18 Part 1](https://archive.org/details/mkbook2300mkga).
- **Hindi series:** *Babasaheb Dr. Ambedkar Sampoorna Vangmay*, 40 volumes, translated and published by the Dr. Ambedkar Foundation; Vols. 22–40 were first published in 2019 [source: BAWS Vol. 1 (2020 reprint), photo caption](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=9) [source: Internet Archive – all 40 Hindi volumes](https://archive.org/details/05_20220926_202209).

**Important note on links.** Every page link in this catalogue points to the official MEA PDF, in the form `https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/VolumeN.pdf#page=P`, where `P` is the page number *in the PDF file* (not the number printed on the page). The address must use lowercase `images`; the older form with `Images` and the `/Images/attach/amb/` files now lead to an error page. The page ranges written in the text (e.g. "pp. 3–22") are the *printed* page numbers of the book.

**Status words used below:**
- *Published in his lifetime* – he published it himself before 6 December 1956.
- *Posthumous* – first printed after his death, usually in BAWS.
- *Unfinished* – he left it incomplete.
- *Unpublished* – exists, but has never been printed.
- *Lost* – known to have existed but no longer survives.

## Where his unpublished papers came from

Many items below were never published by Ambedkar. After his death in 1956, his papers, including unpublished writings, were taken into custody by the custodian of the High Court of Delhi and later passed to the Administrator General of the Government of Maharashtra. A Nagpur advocate, J. B. Bansod, went to court asking that the writings be published. In the end his widow Savita Ambedkar, his grandson Prakash Ambedkar and his family, and Bansod agreed, and on 18 September 1981 Vasant Moon took possession of five iron trunks of papers for the Government of Maharashtra. The editors counted 51 titles of unpublished writings among them, and received 14 more unpublished essays from S. S. Rege, former librarian of Siddharth College, Bombay [source: BAWS Vol. 3, Introduction (2014 reprint, Internet Archive full text)](https://archive.org/stream/HindSwaraj-Ambedkar-03/Ambedkar_Volume_03_djvu.txt). More on this in `physical-archives.md`.

## Theses and academic papers

His university work at Columbia (New York) and the London School of Economics, and his first published research.

- **Administration and Finance of the East India Company** (1915) – This was Ambedkar's M.A. thesis at Columbia University in New York. It explains how the British East India Company was run and how it raised and spent money while it ruled parts of India. It stayed unpublished until the Government of Maharashtra printed it in BAWS Vol. 6 (1989). *Dates:* Submitted 15 May 1915 for the M.A. degree, Columbia University. [source: BAWS Vol. 6, pp. 5–50](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume6.pdf#page=20) [source: Columbia University – Pritchett timeline](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html) *Physical copy:* Columbia University (New York) (New York, USA) – see `physical-archives.md`.
- **Castes in India: Their Mechanism, Genesis and Development** (1916) – A student paper that asks how caste began and how it keeps itself going. Ambedkar argues that caste is kept alive mainly by forbidding marriage outside the group (endogamy), and by customs such as child marriage and enforced widowhood that serve that purpose. *Dates:* Read on 9 May 1916 at Prof. A. A. Goldenweiser's anthropology seminar, Columbia University; printed in Indian Antiquary, May 1917 (Vol. XLI, as given in BAWS). [source: BAWS Vol. 1, pp. 3–22](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=18) [source: Columbia University (Frances Pritchett) – edited text](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/txt_ambedkar_castes.html) [source: Internet Archive – pamphlet reprint (Patrika Publications, Jullundur)](https://archive.org/details/castesinindia035140mbp) [source: Columbia University – Pritchett timeline](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html)
- **The National Dividend of India: A Historic and Analytical Study** (1916) – This was the doctoral research Ambedkar did at Columbia University. He later reworked it into the book "The Evolution of Provincial Finance in British India" (1925), and Columbia then awarded him the Ph.D. *Dates:* Offered at Columbia in 1916 (Keer, via Pritchett). Revised and published in London in 1925 as "The Evolution of Provincial Finance in British India"; Columbia formally awarded the Ph.D. on 8 June 1927 according to his transcript (Pritchett). Other sources give other dates. [source: BAWS Vol. 6, pp. 51–312](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume6.pdf#page=66) [source: The Evolution of Provincial Finance in British India (1925 P. S. King edition), Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/dli.ministry.12679) [source: Columbia University – Pritchett timeline](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html) [source: Columbia University – Pritchett timeline](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1920s.html) *Physical copy:* Columbia University, Office of the Registrar / University Archives (New York, USA) – see `physical-archives.md`.
- **Small Holdings in India and Their Remedies** (1918) – An economics paper about Indian farms that were split into tiny plots. Ambedkar argues that making farms bigger is not enough; the real cure is industrialisation, which would take surplus workers off the land. *Dates:* Journal of the Indian Economic Society, Vol. I, 1918. [source: BAWS Vol. 1, pp. 453–480](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=468) [source: Columbia University – Pritchett timeline](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html)
- **Provincial Decentralization of Imperial Finance in British India (M.Sc. thesis)** (1921) – Ambedkar's master's thesis at the London School of Economics, about how the British government in India handed some money powers to the provinces. Its subject is close to his 1925 book on provincial finance, but the thesis itself has not been printed as a separate text. *Dates:* M.Sc. (Economics), London School of Economics. LSE's archivist says that on returning in 1920 he applied to complete his master's with a thesis titled "The Provincial Decentralisation of Imperial Finance in India". Pritchett's timeline dates the M.Sc. thesis to 1922; other sources say 1921. [source: LSE South Asia blog (LSE Archivist)](https://blogs.lse.ac.uk/southasia/2016/03/30/no-more-worlds-here-for-him-to-conquer-br-ambedkar-at-lse) [source: Columbia University – Pritchett timeline](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1920s.html) *Physical copy:* LSE Library Archives / University of London (London, UK) – see `physical-archives.md`.
- **The Problem of the Rupee: Its Origin and Its Solution** (1923) – A history of Indian money from the silver standard to the "gold exchange standard". Ambedkar criticises the gold exchange standard, under which the government could issue rupees at its discretion, and argues for a return to a gold standard with a fixed limit on the issue of rupees so that prices stay stable. *Dates:* Doctoral thesis, University of London (LSE): submitted March 1923, resubmitted August 1923, accepted November 1923 (LSE archivist); published by P. S. King & Son, London, 1923. Reissued in 1947 as "History of Indian Currency and Banking, Vol. 1". [source: BAWS Vol. 6, pp. 315–620](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume6.pdf#page=332) [source: Internet Archive – 1923 P. S. King edition (Digital Library of India scan)](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.84521) [source: Internet Archive – History of Indian Currency and Banking (1947)](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.76538) [source: LSE South Asia blog (LSE Archivist)](https://blogs.lse.ac.uk/southasia/2016/03/30/no-more-worlds-here-for-him-to-conquer-br-ambedkar-at-lse) [source: Columbia University – Pritchett timeline](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1920s.html) [source: BAWS (MEA PDF)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume6.pdf#page=330) [source: BAWS (MEA PDF)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume6.pdf#page=340) [source: BAWS (MEA PDF)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume6.pdf#page=343)

## Books and pamphlets

Books and booklets, most of them published by Ambedkar himself in his lifetime. "The Buddha and His Dhamma" came out in 1957, a year after his death.

- **The Evolution of Provincial Finance in British India: A Study in the Provincial Decentralization of Imperial Finance** (1925) – This book traces how the provinces of British India came to have their own finances: from the old centralised "Imperial System", through budgets by assignments and by shared revenues, to the changes made by the Government of India Act, 1919, which Ambedkar criticises. *Dates:* P. S. King & Son, London, 1925, with a foreword by Prof. Edwin R. A. Seligman of Columbia. Based on his Columbia doctoral research (see "The National Dividend of India"). Pritchett's 1920s page also calls it his "London School of Economics M.A. thesis" — the sources are not consistent. [source: BAWS Vol. 6, pp. 51–312](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume6.pdf#page=66) [source: Internet Archive – 1925 P. S. King edition](https://archive.org/details/dli.ministry.12679) [source: BAWS (MEA PDF)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume6.pdf#page=72) [source: Columbia University – Pritchett timeline](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1920s.html)
- **Annihilation of Caste** (1936) – Ambedkar was invited to give a speech against caste in Lahore, but the organisers backed out when they read his text. He printed it himself. In it he argues that caste cannot be ended by small reforms, because it rests on religious beliefs in the Hindu scriptures, and that real change needs liberty, equality and fraternity. *Dates:* Written as the presidential address for the 1936 annual conference of the Jat-Pat-Todak Mandal, Lahore, which was never delivered; Ambedkar published it himself in 1936. Second edition 1937 (with Gandhi's articles and Ambedkar's reply); third edition 1944. BAWS reprints the 1944 text. [source: BAWS Vol. 1, pp. 23–96](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=38) [source: Columbia University (CCNMTL / Frances Pritchett) – annotated e-text for students](https://ccnmtl.columbia.edu/projects/mmt/ambedkar/web/index.html) [source: Internet Archive – 1944/1945 third edition scan (Digital Library of India)](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.71655) [source: BAWS (MEA PDF)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=40) [source: Columbia University – Pritchett timeline](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html)
- **Federation versus Freedom** (1939) – A lecture examining the federal scheme in the Government of India Act, 1935. Ambedkar argues that the plan, which joined British provinces with princely states, would not bring India real freedom or responsible government. *Dates:* Kale Memorial Lecture, Gokhale Institute of Politics and Economics, Poona, 29 January 1939; published as "Tracts for the Times No. 3". [source: BAWS Vol. 1, pp. 279–354](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=294) [source: Internet Archive – 1939 edition scan (Digital Library of India)](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.83949) [source: BAWS (MEA PDF)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=298)
- **Pakistan or the Partition of India** (1940) – Written after the Muslim League's 1940 Lahore resolution, this book sets out the Muslim case for Pakistan and the Hindu case against it, and asks what the alternatives are. Ambedkar concludes that if Muslims really wanted it, partition might be better than a forced and unhappy union, and discusses the exchange of populations, citing the example of Turkey, Greece and Bulgaria. *Dates:* First published December 1940 as "Thoughts on Pakistan"; second edition February 1945 and third edition 1946 under the title "Pakistan or the Partition of India" (Thacker & Co., Bombay). BAWS reprints the 1946 edition. [source: BAWS Vol. 8, pp. 1–483](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume8.pdf#page=26) [source: Internet Archive – Thoughts on Pakistan (Thacker, 1941 printing)](https://archive.org/details/dli.ernet.1802) [source: Internet Archive – Pakistan or Partition of India (1945)](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.514118) [source: Columbia University (Frances Pritchett) – online edition](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/ambedkar_partition/index.html) [source: BAWS (MEA PDF)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume8.pdf#page=20) [source: BAWS (MEA PDF)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume8.pdf#page=24) [source: BAWS (MEA PDF)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume8.pdf#page=140)
- **Labour and Parliamentary Democracy** (1943) – A speech to trade union workers about what parliamentary democracy should mean for labour. He urged labour to aim at taking charge of government by organising its own Labour Party, wider than the trade unions. *Dates:* Speech at the concluding session of the All India Trade Union Workers' Study Camp, Delhi (8–17 September 1943); published by the Indian Federation of Labour. [source: BAWS Vol. 10, pp. 106–112](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=123) [source: BAWS (MEA PDF)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=128)
- **Mr. Gandhi and the Emancipation of the Untouchables** (1943) – A short book giving the number of untouchables, their political demands and the Hindu opposition to them, including the debate over joint and separate electorates. Ambedkar says in the preface that it began as a paper for the Institute of Pacific Relations conference of December 1942. *Dates:* Thacker & Co., first edition December 1943. [source: BAWS Vol. 9, pp. 389–436](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume9.pdf#page=418) [source: Internet Archive – scan (Digital Library of India)](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.66066) [source: BAWS (MEA PDF)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume9.pdf#page=421) [source: BAWS (MEA PDF)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume9.pdf#page=426)
- **Ranade, Gandhi and Jinnah** (1943) – A speech in honour of the social reformer Mahadev Govind Ranade. Ambedkar praises Ranade's commitment to social reform and criticises the blind hero-worship he saw around Gandhi and Jinnah. *Dates:* Address on the 101st birthday of M. G. Ranade, Gokhale Memorial Hall, Poona, 18 January 1943; published 1943. [source: BAWS Vol. 1, pp. 205–240](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=220) [source: Columbia University (Frances Pritchett) – full text of the 1943 edition](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/txt_ambedkar_ranade.html) [source: BAWS (MEA PDF)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=242)
- **Communal Deadlock and a Way to Solve It** (1945) – A proposal for sharing seats in the legislature among India's communities. Ambedkar argues that "majority rule" by one community is unjust: a majority community may have more seats than others, but never an absolute majority on its own. *Dates:* Address at the session of the All India Scheduled Castes Federation, Bombay, 6 May 1945; published 1945. [source: BAWS Vol. 1, pp. 355–380](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=370) [source: BAWS (MEA PDF)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=388)
- **What Congress and Gandhi Have Done to the Untouchables** (1945) – A detailed, document-based criticism of the Congress party and of Gandhi's approach to the untouchables from 1917 onwards. Ambedkar argues that their programmes were charity rather than real political power for the untouchables. *Dates:* Thacker & Co., June 1945; second edition 1946 (Chapter IX of the 1946 edition is added as an annexure in BAWS Vol. 9). [source: BAWS Vol. 9, pp. 1–388](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume9.pdf#page=20) [source: BAWS Vol. 9, pp. 439–470](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume9.pdf#page=468) [source: Internet Archive – 1945 Thacker edition (Central Secretariat Library scan)](https://archive.org/details/dli.csl.7774) [source: Internet Archive – 1946 edition](https://archive.org/details/whatcongressgand00bram) [source: BAWS (MEA PDF)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume9.pdf#page=21) [source: BAWS (MEA PDF)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume9.pdf#page=22) [source: BAWS (MEA PDF)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume9.pdf#page=466)
- **The Cabinet Mission and the Untouchables** (1946) – A leaflet asking how the Cabinet Mission's statement of 10 May 1946 had ignored the untouchables. *Dates:* Printed leaflet, 1946 (BAWS Vol. 10); also printed from The Times of India, 15 October 1946, in BAWS Vol. 17, Part 2. [source: BAWS Vol. 10, pp. 537–550](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume10.pdf#page=558) [source: BAWS Vol. 17 Part 2, pp. 263–276](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_II.pdf#page=286)
- **Who Were the Shudras? How They Came to Be the Fourth Varna in the Indo-Aryan Society** (1946) – A historical study of the Shudras, the fourth and lowest varna. Ambedkar rejects the theory that they were a conquered non-Aryan race and argues that they were originally a Kshatriya (warrior) community who were pushed down after conflicts with the Brahmins. *Dates:* First edition 1946 (Thacker & Co., Bombay); second edition 1947; third 1970 (as listed in BAWS Vol. 7). [source: BAWS Vol. 7, pp. 1–228](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume7.pdf#page=20) [source: Internet Archive – 1946 Thacker edition scan (Digital Library of India)](https://archive.org/details/dli.ernet.522953) [source: BAWS (MEA PDF)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume7.pdf#page=26) [source: BAWS (MEA PDF)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume7.pdf#page=25) [source: BAWS (MEA PDF)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume7.pdf#page=23)
- **The Untouchables: Who Were They and Why They Became Untouchables?** (1948) – A study of how untouchability began. Ambedkar argues that the untouchables were descended from "Broken Men" who lived outside villages, that they stayed Buddhists, and that untouchability was born "some time about 400 A.D.", partly because they continued to eat beef after the Brahmins had given it up. *Dates:* First published October 1948 by Amrit Book Co., New Delhi (BAWS Vol. 7). [source: BAWS Vol. 7, pp. 229–380](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume7.pdf#page=248) [source: Internet Archive – 1948 Amrit Book Co. edition scan (Digital Library of India)](https://archive.org/details/dli.ernet.238047) [source: BAWS (MEA PDF)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume7.pdf#page=252) [source: BAWS (MEA PDF)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume7.pdf#page=253) [source: BAWS (MEA PDF)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume7.pdf#page=254) [source: BAWS (MEA PDF)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume7.pdf#page=256) [source: BAWS (MEA PDF)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume7.pdf#page=398)
- **Thoughts on Linguistic States** (1955) – Written in response to the report of the States Reorganisation Commission, this short book accepts "one state, one language" but warns against very large states. Ambedkar proposes dividing Uttar Pradesh into three states and suggests that India needs a second capital to ease tension between North and South. *Dates:* First published 1955. [source: BAWS Vol. 1, pp. 137–202](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=152) [source: Internet Archive – scan](https://archive.org/details/thoughts-on-linguistic-states) [source: BAWS (MEA PDF)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=156) [source: BAWS (MEA PDF)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=166) [source: BAWS (MEA PDF)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=185)
- **The Buddha and His Dhamma** (1957) – Ambedkar's retelling of the Buddha's life and teaching for ordinary readers, in eight books with a prologue and epilogue. He presents the Dhamma as a moral and social path based on reason, equality and compassion. *Dates:* Completed in 1956; published posthumously in 1957 by the People's Education Society, Bombay (BAWS Vol. 11 supplement preface). The 2014 BAWS reprint names "Siddharth Publication: 1957" as the first edition. [source: BAWS Vol. 11, pp. 1–600](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume11.pdf#page=20) [source: Internet Archive – 1957 edition scan (IGNCA collection)](https://archive.org/details/in.gov.ignca.15980) [source: Columbia University (Frances Pritchett) – online edition](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/ambedkar_buddha/index.html) [source: BAWS (MEA PDF)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume11.pdf#page=15) [source: BAWS (MEA PDF)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume11.pdf#page=626) [source: BAWS (MEA PDF)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume17_Part_I.pdf#page=469) *Physical copy:* People's Education Society, Mumbai (Mumbai, Maharashtra) – see `physical-archives.md`.
- **Bouddha Pooja Path** (undated) – A short book of Buddhist prayers and verses (vandana) for use by the new Buddhist community. *Dates:* Compilation of Buddhist prayers printed in Devanagari script; BAWS Vol. 16 (1998) prints it as Book IV. BAWS does not give the year of its first separate printing (it is often dated 1956), and the exact mix of Pali and Marathi was not checked. [source: BAWS Vol. 16, pp. 723–744](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume16.pdf#page=742)

## Memoranda and evidence to commissions

Statements he wrote for, or gave in person to, British and Indian committees deciding India's future.

- **Evidence before the Southborough Committee on Franchise** (1919) – Ambedkar told a British committee planning new voting rules that "untouchable" Hindus formed a separate social group from "touchable" Hindus. He asked for separate representation so that they could speak for themselves in the legislatures. *Dates:* Written statement and oral examination on 27 January 1919 (Reforms Committee (Franchise) Report, Vol. II, 1919). [source: BAWS Vol. 1, pp. 243–278](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=258) [source: Columbia University – Pritchett timeline](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html)
- **Statement of Evidence and Oral Evidence before the Royal Commission on Indian Currency and Finance** (1925) – Ambedkar gave written and spoken evidence to a British commission on India's currency. He argued against the "gold exchange standard" and for a system that would keep prices steady. *Dates:* Written statement in reply to the Commission's questionnaire; oral evidence on 15 December 1925 (printed in the Commission's Report, Vols. II and IV, 1926). [source: BAWS Vol. 6, pp. 625–670](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume6.pdf#page=640) [source: Internet Archive – Royal Commission on Indian Currency and Finance, Vol. IV, Minutes of Evidence (1926)](https://archive.org/details/dli.csl.334) [source: BAWS (MEA PDF)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume6.pdf#page=651)
- **Evidence before the Indian Statutory Commission (Simon Commission)** (1928) – Ambedkar was questioned by the Simon Commission about the condition and political rights of the depressed classes. *Dates:* Oral evidence, 23 October 1928. [source: BAWS Vol. 2, pp. 459–490](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=478) [source: BAWS (MEA PDF)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=15)
- **Statement concerning the Safeguards for the Protection of the Interests of the Depressed Classes as a Minority** (1928) – A memorandum arguing that the depressed classes were a minority needing legal protection, including representation in legislatures and in government jobs. *Dates:* Submitted on behalf of the Bahishkrita Hitakarini Sabha to the Indian Statutory Commission, 29 May 1928. [source: BAWS Vol. 2, pp. 429–458](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=448) [source: BAWS (MEA PDF)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=15)
- **Statement concerning the State of Education of the Depressed Classes in the Bombay Presidency** (1928) – Using official numbers, this memorandum shows how few children of the depressed classes were in school and college, and asks for scholarships, hostels and special help. *Dates:* Submitted on behalf of the Bahishkrita Hitakarini Sabha to the Indian Statutory Commission, 29 May 1928. [source: BAWS Vol. 2, pp. 407–428](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=426) [source: BAWS (MEA PDF)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=15)
- **Report on the Constitution of the Government of Bombay Presidency (Views and Recommendations to the Indian Statutory Commission)** (1929) – Ambedkar wrote his own report for the committee working with the Simon Commission. He recommended adult franchise, reserved seats for the depressed classes, and more powers for elected ministers. *Dates:* Prepared as a member of the Bombay Provincial Committee that worked with the Simon Commission; Pritchett dates it to 1929. [source: BAWS Vol. 2, pp. 315–406](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=334) [source: BAWS (MEA PDF)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume2.pdf#page=15) [source: Columbia University – Pritchett timeline](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1920s.html)

## Where sources disagree

- **His 1915 M.A. thesis.** BAWS Vol. 6 prints "Administration and Finance of the East India Company" as the thesis "submitted in partial fulfilment of the requirements of the degree of Master of Arts, May 15, 1915" [source: BAWS Vol. 6, p. 5](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume6.pdf#page=18). Frances Pritchett's timeline, citing Keer's biography, says the 1915 M.A. thesis was "Ancient Indian Commerce" [source: Columbia University – Pritchett timeline, 1910s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).
- **"The Evolution of Provincial Finance".** Pritchett's 1910s page says this book grew out of his 1916 Columbia thesis "National Dividend of India", and his 1920s page says Columbia awarded the Ph.D. for it on 8 June 1927 (from his transcript). The same 1920s page also calls it his "London School of Economics M.A. thesis" [source: Columbia University – Pritchett timeline, 1920s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1920s.html). His LSE master's thesis was on a related subject – "The Provincial Decentralisation of Imperial Finance in India" [source: LSE South Asia blog – Sue Donnelly, LSE Archivist](https://blogs.lse.ac.uk/southasia/2016/03/30/no-more-worlds-here-for-him-to-conquer-br-ambedkar-at-lse).
- **"The Problem of the Rupee".** The LSE Archivist calls it a doctoral (PhD) thesis, submitted in March 1923, resubmitted in August and accepted in November 1923; it is usually described as his D.Sc. [source: LSE South Asia blog](https://blogs.lse.ac.uk/southasia/2016/03/30/no-more-worlds-here-for-him-to-conquer-br-ambedkar-at-lse). The same post notes that stories of the examiners calling it "too revolutionary" find no support in his student file.
- **First-edition dates of BAWS volumes.** The 2020 Ambedkar Foundation reprints print "First Edition: 14 April, 1979" on every volume [source: BAWS Vol. 13 (2020 reprint), imprint page](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume13.pdf#page=5), which is only true of Vol. 1. The 2014 reprints give the real dates, for example 14 April 1994 for Vol. 13 and 4 October 2003 for Vol. 17 [source: Internet Archive – BAWS Vol. 17 Part 1 (2014 reprint) full text](https://archive.org/stream/Dr.BabasahebAmbedkarWritingsAndSpeechespdfsAllVolumes/Volume_17_01_djvu.txt).
- **Mooknayak's frequency and end date** – see `journals.md`.
- **The Russell review.** BAWS gives the "Journal of the Indian Economic Society, Vol. I, 1918" [source: BAWS Vol. 1, p. 481](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=496); Pritchett's timeline names the "Journal of Indian Economics" [source: Columbia University – Pritchett timeline, 1910s](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).
- **"Thoughts on Pakistan" and "Pakistan or the Partition of India"** are the same book: BAWS lists a first edition of December 1940 and later editions of February 1945 and 1946 [source: BAWS Vol. 8, edition page](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume8.pdf#page=20). A Digital Library of India scan of "Thoughts on Pakistan" is dated 1941 [source: Internet Archive – Thoughts on Pakistan (1941)](https://archive.org/details/dli.ernet.1802).

## Gaps and things still to check

- Volume 14 (both parts) and Volume 16 of the MEA PDFs are image scans without searchable text. Their page links here were checked by looking at the scanned pages: PDF page = printed page + 15 (Vol. 14 Part 1), printed page − 777 (Vol. 14 Part 2) and printed page + 19 (Vol. 16).
- The Dr. Ambedkar Foundation's own site, drambedkarwritings.gov.in, did not respond when this catalogue was made, so its copies were not checked.
- No complete list exists of the letters Dr. Ambedkar wrote. The Marathi BAWS Vol. 21 (Correspondence) and the letters printed in BAWS Vols. 10 and 17 are the best starting points.
- Several manuscripts that BAWS describes (for example the 31-page handwritten "Manu and the Shudras") are known only from the BAWS editors' notes; their present storage place is not documented online.
