In July 1913 a young man from Bombay stepped off a ship in New York. He was 22 years old. In India, the caste system treated his community, the Mahars, as "untouchable": people who were kept out of temples, wells and many schools. In America, he later wrote, he could forget that he was "an untouchable" at all [source: BAWS Vol. 12, p. 673](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume12.pdf#page=694). For the next three years Dr. Ambedkar lived and studied at Columbia University, and the experience changed his life [source: Columbia University – Pritchett timeline](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).

## A scholarship from Baroda

Dr. Ambedkar had graduated from Elphinstone College, Bombay, in 1912 with a BA in economics and politics [source: Pritchett timeline](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html) [source: Supreme Court of India](https://www.sci.gov.in/centenary-of-dr-b-r-ambedkars-enrolment-as-an-advocate/). In 1913 the ruler of Baroda State, Maharaja Sayajirao Gaekwad III, offered scholarships for higher study at Columbia University. Dr. Ambedkar was given one worth £11.50 a month for three years [source: Pritchett timeline, citing Kadam](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).

The money came with a condition. In return, he had to work for Baroda State after his studies. According to The Beacon, which draws on the Marathi biography by C. B. Khairmode, the agreement asked for ten years of service and the scholarship ran from 15 June 1913 to 14 June 1916 [source: The Beacon](https://thebeacon.in/?p=3103). The historian Eleanor Zelliot noted that the stipend came through Baroda's Education Department, and that it is not clear whose idea the American plan was [source: Zelliot, "Dr. Ambedkar and America" (1991)](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/graphics/txt_zelliot1991.html).

## Arriving in New York

He reached New York in the third week of July 1913. He first stayed in Hartley Hall, a university dormitory, but disliked the food and left after a week. In August he moved to the Cosmopolitan Club at 554 West 114th Street, a house run by Indian students. Finally he settled in Livingston Hall (now called Wallach Hall), sharing with a Parsi student, Naval Bhathena, who stayed his friend for life [source: Pritchett timeline, citing Keer](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).

Years later he told his biographer that he enjoyed the social side of campus at first. Then one night he decided to study "in dead earnest" [source: Pritchett timeline](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html). He spent long hours in the reading room under the dome of Low Library [source: Pritchett timeline](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).

## A huge course load

Columbia's records show just how hard he worked. Over three years, including summer terms, he took 29 courses in economics, 11 in history, 6 in sociology, 5 in philosophy, 4 in anthropology and 3 in politics, plus beginner French and German [source: Pritchett timeline, citing Columbia's Registrar](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html). The course list includes public finance, statistics, socialism, railway economics, the history of India and Persia, and the French Revolution [source: Pritchett – list of courses](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/graphics/courses.html).

He was admitted to Columbia's Graduate School as a whole, not to one narrow department [source: Pritchett timeline](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).

## His teachers

Some of America's best-known thinkers taught him:

- **Edwin R. A. Seligman**, an expert on taxes and public money. He became Dr. Ambedkar's main mentor and they wrote to each other for years [source: Pritchett timeline](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).
- **John Dewey**, the philosopher of democracy and education. In *Annihilation of Caste* (1936), Dr. Ambedkar called Dewey "my teacher" [source: Pritchett timeline](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html) [source: Zelliot (1991)](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/graphics/txt_zelliot1991.html).
- **James Harvey Robinson** and **James Shotwell**, historians [source: Pritchett timeline](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).
- **Alexander Goldenweiser**, an anthropologist (someone who studies human societies), and **Franklin Giddings**, a sociologist [source: Pritchett – list of courses](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/graphics/courses.html).

In 1930, Columbia's alumni magazine printed his memory of those years:

> "The best friends I have had in my life," Dr. Ambedkar says, "were some of my classmates at Columbia and my great professors, John Dewey, James Shotwell, Edwin Seligman, and James Harvey Robinson."
>
> — Dr. B. R. Ambedkar, quoted in *Columbia Alumni News*, 19 December 1930 [source: Columbia University Archives – Alumni News scan](https://guides.library.columbia.edu/ld.php?content_id=43945313)

## His first Columbia degree: an MA in 1915

An MA (Master of Arts) is a degree you earn after a first degree, usually by taking advanced courses and writing a long essay called a thesis. On 15 May 1915 Dr. Ambedkar submitted his MA thesis, "Administration and Finance of the East India Company". It looked at how the British trading company that ruled much of India was run and how it raised and spent money [source: BAWS Vol. 6, p. 18 of PDF](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume6.pdf#page=18). Columbia lists his degree as "MA 1915" [source: Columbia University Archives](https://guides.library.columbia.edu/uarchives/notables). He passed the MA examination in June 1915, with economics as his main subject [source: Pritchett timeline](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).

The thesis was never published in his lifetime. In 1979 a copy was obtained from Columbia, and it now appears in his collected works [source: BAWS Vol. 6, p. 20 of PDF](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume6.pdf#page=20).

## "Castes in India" (9 May 1916)

On 9 May 1916 Dr. Ambedkar read a paper to Alexander Goldenweiser's anthropology seminar. It was called "Castes in India: Their Mechanism, Genesis and Development" [source: BAWS Vol. 1, p. 18 of PDF](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=18). He argued that caste was not caused by race [source: Zelliot (1991)](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/graphics/txt_zelliot1991.html). Its key feature, he said, was endogamy: the rule that people may marry only inside their own group [source: BAWS Vol. 1, p. 9 (PDF p. 24)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=24). The paper was printed in the journal *Indian Antiquary* in May 1917 [source: BAWS Vol. 1](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=18). He stayed proud of it all his life [source: Pritchett timeline](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).

## The PhD: finished early, awarded late

A PhD (Doctor of Philosophy) is the highest research degree. It needs a long, original study called a dissertation.

By the time he left New York in 1916, Dr. Ambedkar had done most of the work [source: Pritchett timeline, citing Keer](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html). In 1920 Professor Seligman wrote that he "passed his examination for the Doctor's degree with considerable distinction" [source: Seligman's letter of 23 Sept. 1920, LSE student file, PDF p. 56](https://www.lse.ac.uk/library/assets/documents/Ambedkars-LSE-student-file.pdf#page=56). Yet Columbia's own records say the PhD was given in 1927 [source: Columbia University Archives](https://guides.library.columbia.edu/uarchives/notables). His official transcript gives the date as 8 June 1927 [source: Pritchett timeline](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1920s.html). Why the ten-year gap?

The sources point to three reasons:

1. **Columbia needed a printed book.** At that time Columbia's Faculty of Political Science did not award a PhD until the dissertation was actually published and copies were handed in. A 1936 faculty memo describes this rule [source: Irwin Collier, Columbia PhD printing requirement](https://www.irwincollier.com/columbia-reform-of-the-phd-dissertation-printing-requirement-1936-1940/).
2. **His manuscript sank.** In 1917 the ship carrying his books and papers home was torpedoed. In 1922 he wrote to Seligman that he had lost "my manuscript of the original thesis" this way [source: Ambedkar to Seligman, 16 Feb. 1922, Seligman papers](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/graphics/seligmanletter.html). (The next chapter tells this story.)
3. **He had to earn a living first.** He had to work in India before he could return to his studies [source: Pritchett timeline](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).

The dissertation finally appeared in London in 1925 as *The Evolution of Provincial Finance in British India*. It studied how money was shared between the British government of India and the provinces [source: BAWS Vol. 6, title page](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume6.pdf#page=66). Seligman wrote the foreword, dated October 1924, calling it Dr. Ambedkar's "excellent dissertation" [source: BAWS Vol. 6, foreword](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume6.pdf#page=70). Dr. Ambedkar dedicated the book to the Maharaja of Baroda "as a token of my gratitude for his help in the matter of my education" [source: BAWS Vol. 6, dedication](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume6.pdf#page=67). Columbia conferred the PhD two years later.

No source says outright that the book caused the timing. But the rule, the lost manuscript and the 1925 book fit together, so this is the most likely explanation.

## Breathing free air

Looking back about twenty years later, probably in 1935 or 1936, Dr. Ambedkar wrote [source: Pritchett's note on *Waiting for a Visa*](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/txt_ambedkar_waiting.html):

> "My five years of stay in Europe and America had completely wiped out of my mind any consciousness that I was an untouchable …"
>
> — Dr. B. R. Ambedkar, *Waiting for a Visa* [source: BAWS Vol. 12, p. 673 (PDF p. 694)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume12.pdf#page=694)

Columbia says he later called it the place where he first experienced social equality [source: Columbia – "Columbians Ahead of Their Time"](https://c250.columbia.edu/c250_celebrates/remarkable_columbians/bhimrao_ambedkar.html). After this freedom, his return to India's caste rules in 1917 came as a shock, as the next chapter shows [source: BAWS Vol. 12, p. 673](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume12.pdf#page=694).

## Columbia remembers him

In January 1952 Columbia renewed an earlier offer to give him an honorary Doctor of Laws (LLD), a degree given to honour someone's life work. Dr. Ambedkar agreed, but wrote that the trip would cost him nearly Rs. 15,000. Thanks to gifts from friends of the university, Columbia then offered $1,500 towards his travel [source: Columbia honorary degree correspondence](https://guides.library.columbia.edu/ld.php?content_id=43945058). He received the degree at Commencement, Columbia's graduation ceremony, on 5 June 1952. The citation called him "a great social reformer and a valiant upholder of human rights" [source: Columbia honorary degree correspondence](https://guides.library.columbia.edu/ld.php?content_id=43945058) [source: Columbia – "Columbians Ahead of Their Time"](https://c250.columbia.edu/c250_celebrates/remarkable_columbians/bhimrao_ambedkar.html).

On 24 October 1995 a bronze bust of him was presented to Columbia's Lehman Library. It was donated by the Federation of Ambedkarite and Buddhist Organisations UK [source: Columbia – "Columbians Ahead of Their Time"](https://c250.columbia.edu/c250_celebrates/remarkable_columbians/bhimrao_ambedkar.html). Columbia's archives still keep his file, photographs and honorary degree letters [source: Columbia University Archives](https://guides.library.columbia.edu/uarchives/notables).

## Where sources disagree

- **When did he get his PhD?** Four dates appear:
  - In his own 1921 application to the University of Bonn, he wrote that he gained the doctorate in 1916 [source: Bellwinkel-Schempp, with facsimile](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/graphics/germanvisit.pdf).
  - A 1951 career summary in his LSE file, apparently the one his biographer C. B. Khairmode sent to LSE, says 8 June 1917 [source: LSE student file, PDF pp. 34–36](https://www.lse.ac.uk/library/assets/documents/Ambedkars-LSE-student-file.pdf#page=36).
  - Columbia's archives and his transcript say 1927, specifically 8 June 1927 [source: Columbia University Archives](https://guides.library.columbia.edu/uarchives/notables) [source: Pritchett timeline](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1920s.html).
  - Columbia's own 1930 alumni magazine calls him "'28PhD", and a 1950 Columbia card says "Ph.D. … 1928" [source: Alumni News scan](https://guides.library.columbia.edu/ld.php?content_id=43945313) [source: Columbia honorary degree file](https://guides.library.columbia.edu/ld.php?content_id=43945058).
  - The best way to read this: he finished his studies and examinations in 1916–17, but the degree was formally conferred in 1927. When he gave evidence to the Southborough Committee in January 1919, he was introduced only as "an M.A. of the Columbia University" [source: BAWS Vol. 1, p. 274 (PDF p. 289)](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume1.pdf#page=289).
- **The MA thesis title.** Columbia's catalogue and his collected works give "Administration and Finance of the East India Company" (1915) [source: Columbia University Archives](https://guides.library.columbia.edu/uarchives/notables) [source: BAWS Vol. 6](https://www.mea.gov.in/images/CPV/Volume6.pdf#page=18). Dhananjay Keer's biography names "Ancient Indian Commerce" [source: Pritchett timeline, citing Keer](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html).
- **The PhD thesis title.** Keer says his 1916 thesis was "National Dividend of India – A Historic and Analytical Study" and that it grew into the PhD [source: Pritchett timeline](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1910s.html). Columbia lists the dissertation as *The Evolution of Provincial Finance in British India* [source: Columbia University Archives](https://guides.library.columbia.edu/uarchives/notables). The lost 1917 draft may explain the change.
- **Where the "best friends" quote first appeared.** Columbia's archives give the *Columbia Alumni News* of 19 December 1930 [source: Columbia University Archives](https://guides.library.columbia.edu/uarchives/notables). Columbia's anniversary website says it came from a 1930 *New York Times* interview [source: "Columbians Ahead of Their Time"](https://c250.columbia.edu/c250_celebrates/remarkable_columbians/bhimrao_ambedkar.html).
- **The 1952 ceremony.** The letters in Columbia's files describe the normal annual Commencement of 5 June 1952 [source: Columbia honorary degree correspondence](https://guides.library.columbia.edu/ld.php?content_id=43945058). Pritchett's timeline calls it part of a "Bicentennial Special Convocation" [source: Pritchett timeline](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1950s.html).
